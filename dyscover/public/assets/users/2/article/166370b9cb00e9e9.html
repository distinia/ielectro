Cusea, officially the People’s Republic of Cusea, is a unitary parliamentary one-party republic located in northern Tayanusa, with a population of over 78 million and a territory of 148,732 km². Its capital and largest city is Virindal, and the official language is Tayanusan. The country is governed by a state-above-party model, where the President serves as head of state and Commander-in-Chief, while the Prime Minister leads the executive People’s Council. Legislative power rests in the unicameral People’s Congress, and justice is administered by the People’s Court, ensuring a system of law rooted in constitutional discipline and administrative efficiency.

Cusea is a secular state, though 93% of its population is Muslim, with a Christian minority of 7%. Religious practice is legally protected but institutionally separated from politics, and religious institutions operate under state supervision. The ethnic composition is overwhelmingly Cusean (95%), with small but integrated minorities like the Stasians and Sifalamis. While the state promotes a civic-national identity, it allows cultural diversity to exist privately, without enforcing assimilation or granting special group-based recognition.

The country has one of the most formidable militaries in the world, ranking second globally. The People’s Armed Forces of Cusea consist of a powerful army, air force, and navy, though the country maintains no foreign bases, in line with its defensive doctrine. It does, however, regularly participate in joint drills across the Tayanusan Community, underscoring its role as a continental defense pillar.

Economically, Cusea has a GDP of 1.8 trillion DTR, driven by state-led industrialization, resource extraction, and massive infrastructure projects. It is the world’s second-largest oil exporter, with energy revenues funding development, modernization, and social programs. Alongside a growing manufacturing base, strategic agriculture, and a rising service sector, Cusea’s economy is planned through national five-year strategies and protected by strict controls on foreign ownership and trade flows.

In foreign affairs, Cusea is a firm advocate of multilateralism, with membership in all major global and regional institutions. It is the leading promoter of pan-Tayanusanism, seeking integration and cooperation across the continent, but maintains rivalry with Lamberia over regional influence. Globally, its with strong cooperation also extended to Fesia and Suklan. Its most entrenched rival is Valmirica, due to disputes over nuclear capabilities, regional influence, and opposing alliances.

Cusea’s population is young (median age 31.7), increasingly urbanized, and supported by a well-developed welfare system, educational infrastructure, and universal healthcare. The state prioritizes demographic planning, civic unity, and technological development, reinforcing its position as a regional superpower with global reach, guided by centralized governance, disciplined growth, and a vision of sovereign modernity.

Etymology

The name Cusea carries profound historical and symbolic significance, reflecting the region’s long-standing pursuit of unity, identity, and central authority. Its most widely accepted origin traces back to the ancient Tayanusan root “Kusaea”, which is believed to translate to “Land of Unity” in the proto-Cusean dialects once spoken across the riverine valleys and eastern highlands of what is now modern Cusea. This etymology resonates with the founding mythos of the nation: that fractured tribes, religious factions, and agrarian communes gradually consolidated under a single socio-political vision, laying the groundwork for what would eventually become the People’s Republic.

Historical linguists argue that the original term *Kusaea* underwent gradual phonetic simplification due to centuries of oral transmission, dialectal blending, and contact with regional merchant tongues along the Grandic trade corridors. By the late classical period, the term had narrowed into the more concise form “Cusea”, which became formalized in early revolutionary writings and adopted officially with the establishment of the socialist state in 2020. The name thus serves as both a geographic designation and an ideological proclamation, echoing the principles of unity, solidarity, and centralized governance that define the modern republic.

While alternative theories have surfaced—some linking the name to lost dynastic titles or ancient toponyms from neighboring civilizations—these remain speculative and lack the broad scholarly or political consensus that supports the *Land of Unity* interpretation. The government, through both official publications and educational curricula, strongly promotes this etymological reading, presenting it as an affirmation of the collective destiny of the Cusean people to overcome division through disciplined unity and shared purpose. In this way, the name Cusea transcends mere linguistic curiosity—it is a core element of the national identity, reinforced in party rhetoric, cultural narratives, and the very motto of the state: *Workers of Cusea, unite!*

Geography

Cusea’s geography is as strategically vital as it is ecologically diverse, encompassing a dramatic range of environments that shape the nation’s economic structure, demographic distribution, and geopolitical reach. Located in northern Tayanusa, the country spans 148,732 km², and is characterized by its juxtaposition of arid deserts, fertile valleys, and an expansive coastline along the Grandic Ocean, which anchors its maritime influence and trade networks.

At the core of the country lies the Cusean Desert, a vast and imposing expanse of sand dunes, rocky plateaus, and arid basins that dominates the national interior. This region, while harsh in climate, is far from barren. The government has invested heavily in desert reclamation and irrigation projects, transforming parts of this dry heartland into productive oases through advanced hydrological engineering, subterranean reservoirs, and solar-powered desalination. These innovations have allowed for controlled agricultural development, the establishment of sustainable desert settlements, and strategic population dispersal away from coastal congestion.

Interspersed within this desert terrain are several agriculturally rich valleys, which serve as the backbone of the nation's food production system. Most notable among these are the Virendale Valley and the Althar Basin, both situated along fertile alluvial plains nourished by the region’s river systems. These valleys host state-run and cooperative farming zones, producing wheat, legumes, fruits, and fodder crops. Their microclimates and river-fed soils also sustain dense vegetation, biodiversity pockets, and protected natural reserves—unique biomes in an otherwise arid geography.

The hydrographic network of Cusea is defined by several rivers that originate in the eastern highlands, cutting through desert plateaus before spilling into agricultural basins and lowland deltas. The most significant of these is the Arvista River, which flows through the Virendale Valley and supports a chain of settlements, irrigation channels, and hydro-agricultural infrastructures. It is considered a lifeline of the interior, and its controlled flow is managed through a system of dams, reservoirs, and seasonal redistribution schemes. Other smaller rivers and wadis provide supplementary water during wet seasons, but the Arvista remains the only year-round navigable and agriculturally indispensable watercourse.

Cusea’s coastline along the Grandic Ocean, stretching along its northern edge, is one of the country’s most geopolitically significant assets. This maritime corridor provides access to key international shipping lanes, energy export terminals, and naval facilities, and is vital for trade with Tayanusa and other global partners. The coast hosts several deep-water ports and industrial zones, as well as naval bases that underpin Cusea’s regional military presence. The oceanic shelf is rich in fisheries and untapped energy reserves, which have drawn both domestic investment and foreign interest, particularly in offshore drilling and undersea cable infrastructure.

Off the coast, a number of island territories extend Cusea’s influence further into the Grandic maritime space. These include the Verdant Isle, known for its subtropical climate and state research centers; the Solara Archipelago, which hosts both fishing communities and naval outposts; Aquil Island, a transport and refueling hub; and Oasis Island, famed for its coral-rich waters and marine biodiversity sanctuaries. Collectively, these islands enhance Cusea’s naval logistics, environmental monitoring, and coastal security network, solidifying the country’s position as a maritime gatekeeper in northern Tayanusa.

Climatically, Cusea experiences a hot desert climate in its interior, with high diurnal temperature variation, minimal rainfall, and intense seasonal winds. In contrast, the coastal and valley regions exhibit semi-arid to mite conditions, making them more habitable and agriculturally favorable. Environmental management is a core aspect of national policy, with major programs focused on desertification control, renewable energy deployment, and eco-conservation in sensitive zones.

Politics

Government

The Government of the People’s Republic of Cusea operates under a unitary parliamentary one-party framework, founded on the principle of state-above-party discipline, where the sovereignty of the Republic is vested not in political factions but in the collective will of the people as represented through the state. The Constitution of 2020 defines Cusea as a secular, centralized, and developmental state, dedicated to order, progress, and social justice under a model of guided democracy.

At the apex of the system stands the President of the Republic, who serves simultaneously as Head of State, Commander-in-Chief of the People’s Armed Forces, and Guarantor of National Unity. The President represents the continuity of the state, appoints the Prime Minister, and oversees national defense, foreign policy, and long-term strategic planning. His authority symbolizes the unity of the nation above class or partisan division, ensuring that all branches of government operate in harmony with constitutional objectives and socialist legality.

The executive power is exercised by the People’s Council, chaired by the Prime Minister, who coordinates the ministries and leads the implementation of national five-year plans, economic directives, and social programs. The Council functions as a technocratic body focused on administrative efficiency, industrial management, and infrastructural modernization. Its composition reflects a balance between senior party officials and professional administrators, ensuring the integration of political guidance with bureaucratic expertise.

The legislative authority resides in the People’s Congress, a unicameral assembly presided over by Chairman. The Congress debates and enacts laws, approves the national budget, and supervises the performance of the executive. Deputies are elected through a structured national list within the framework of the ruling organization, representing workers, youth, farmers, professionals, and local communities. The Congress operates under principles of participatory centralism, where deliberation precedes decision, and decision binds all.

The judiciary is unified under the People’s Court System, headed by Chairman, which guarantees justice through constitutional discipline, administrative impartiality, and public accountability. The courts interpret the Constitution, safeguard citizens’ rights, and ensure that state organs act within the boundaries of law. Judges are selected through meritocratic evaluation and serve as instruments of lawful governance rather than instruments of political struggle.

Together, these institutions form the tripartite architecture of Cusean governance, where leadership, administration, and justice operate as coordinated pillars of the Republic. The system aspires to harmonize authority with representation, efficiency with legality, and ideological guidance with professional governance, embodying the Cusean model of centralized democracy and sovereign modernity.

Political parties

The Cusean Communist Party (CCP) stands as the sole legal and ruling political organization of the People’s Republic of Cusea, serving as the vanguard of the working class and the principal guardian of the state’s ideological, political, and moral unity. Founded upon the doctrine of socialist modernization and national sovereignty, the CCP operates under the guiding motto “Workers of Cusea, unite!”, symbolizing its mission to integrate the strength of labor, science, and statecraft into a unified national purpose.

The Party’s structure follows the principles of democratic centralism, combining broad participation at the grassroots level with firm central leadership. The Central Committee, led by the Secretary General, directs policy through the Politburo and Secretariat, ensuring that all governmental, military, and social institutions act in accordance with the Party’s strategic line. Below it, provincial, municipal, and workplace committees transmit decisions, mobilize the population, and oversee implementation across every sector of society.

The CCP functions not merely as a political organization but as the ideological and moral compass of the nation, responsible for shaping education, cultural policy, labor relations, and civic ethics. It upholds a secular and progressive socialist vision, promoting industrial advancement, social equity, and national independence while rejecting both religious extremism and external political interference. Within the state, the Party and government are distinct yet inseparable: the Party defines the direction of policy, while the government executes it through the constitutional framework. Every major institution—from the People’s Congress to the People’s Armed Forces—operates under Party guidance, ensuring cohesion, loyalty, and discipline.

Membership in the CCP is considered a mark of civic merit and ideological dedication, open to citizens who demonstrate integrity, competence, and service to the Republic. The Party maintains internal schools and academies to train cadres in administration, law, economics, and defense studies, cultivating a professional elite loyal to the principles of the revolution and the authority of the state. Through this system, the Cusean Communist Party embodies the unity between people, state, and ideology, serving as the permanent engine of national development, the guardian of socialist legality, and the architect of Cusea’s path toward sovereign modernity and collective progress.

Administrative subdivisions

The People’s Republic of Cusea is organized as a unitary state, with all administrative authority emanating from the central government in Virindal. The country is divided into a hierarchical structure of provinces, districts, and municipalities, designed to ensure effective governance, national cohesion, and uniform implementation of the state’s development strategies. This system reflects the Cusean principle of centralized unity with delegated administration, allowing local bodies to execute national directives while maintaining strict adherence to constitutional discipline and party oversight.

At the first level, Cusea is composed of 18 provinces, each headed by a Governor (Hakim) appointed by the People’s Council upon recommendation from the Ministry of Interior and approval by the President. These governors act as the representatives of the central state, supervising regional security, economic coordination, infrastructure projects, and the enforcement of national policy. Provincial governments are not autonomous but operate as extensions of the central executive, ensuring administrative uniformity across the nation.

Each province is subdivided into districts (madinat), directed by District Commissioners, who manage local services, resource allocation, and civil administration under the supervision of the provincial governor. At the base level, municipalities and communes serve as the administrative link between citizens and the state, coordinating education, healthcare, and social programs in accordance with the national plan.

Local councils exist in an advisory capacity, composed of representatives from the Cusean Communist Party, trade unions, youth organizations, and community leaders. These councils provide feedback and assist in implementing the Five-Year Development Plans, but final authority always rests with the provincial and central ministries.

The administrative framework of Cusea is thus deeply integrated and vertically structured, reflecting the nation’s doctrine of efficient governance, social mobilization, and disciplined unity. Each level of administration functions as both a bureaucratic mechanism and an instrument of civic participation, ensuring that every province and community contributes to the collective mission of national development and socialist modernization.

Foreign relations

The foreign relations of the People’s Republic of Cusea are guided by the doctrine of Pan-Tayanusanism, sovereign equality, and collective development, emphasizing cooperation among nations while upholding the absolute independence of the Cusean state. Rooted in the principles of non-alignment, multilateral engagement, and defensive realism, Cusea projects itself as both a regional pillar of stability and a global advocate of balanced multipolarism, seeking to construct an international order based on mutual respect, economic justice, and technological self-reliance.

At the continental level, Cusea plays a leading role in the Tayanusan Community (TC), promoting economic integration, infrastructure connectivity, and regional security coordination. Its initiatives within the TC focus on joint energy networks, counter-terrorism cooperation, and sustainable industrial growth, making Cusea a central actor in shaping the political and economic destiny of northern Tayanusa. The Cusean model of centralized planning and social development has inspired several neighboring states, reinforcing its soft power and ideological influence across the continent.

Globally, Cusea is a full member of all major international organizations, including the World Union, Islamic Cooperation Organization, Global Reserve Fund, World Trade Network, and the International Energy Agency, where it advocates for equitable trade systems, fair resource distribution, and South-South cooperation. Its diplomacy combines pragmatism and principle, supporting peaceful conflict resolution, non-interference in domestic affairs, and a strong role for developing nations in global governance.

Cusea maintains close relations with Fesia and Suklan, based on technological exchange, energy security, and defense coordination. These partnerships underpin Cusea’s external modernization drive and enhance its strategic depth in space, cyber, and advanced manufacturing sectors. Relations with Lamberia remain marked by strategic rivalry, reflecting competition for regional leadership and influence over Tayanusan integration frameworks.

Its most entrenched geopolitical opponent is Valmirica, due to enduring disputes over nuclear capabilities, maritime control, and opposing alliance systems. Despite recurring diplomatic tensions, Cusea adheres to a policy of controlled deterrence, balancing firmness with dialogue to prevent escalation. Toward Destenia, Cusea maintains a stance of measured economic, political and miltiary cooperation, recognizing its economic and scientific importance and recognizing it efforts in the respect toward the Global South.

Through its global outreach, humanitarian programs, and technical aid to developing nations, Cusea seeks to embody the vision of a responsible socialist power — one that defends sovereignty, promotes equitable globalization, and strengthens the collective advancement of the Global South. Its foreign policy thus reflects the synthesis of realism, solidarity, and revolutionary pragmatism, asserting Cusea’s role as a regional superpower with global reach and moral leadership in the twenty-first century.

Military

The People’s Armed Forces of Cusea (PAFC) serve as a central pillar of the state’s sovereignty, national unity, and strategic deterrence. Organized under a unified command structure, the armed forces are tasked with defending the territorial integrity of the republic, safeguarding national interests, and maintaining readiness in a region marked by dynamic geopolitical shifts. The military operates under the authority of the People’s Council, with the President of the Republic serving as Commander-in-Chief, and overall strategic planning managed by the General Staff of National Defense. The PAFC is composed of three primary branches:

  • The People’s Army, the largest and most historically grounded branch, is responsible for ground defense, border security, internal stability, and logistical coordination. It consists of armored divisions, mechanized infantry, mountain brigades, and missile units, all structured for rapid deployment across Cusea’s varied geography—from the desert interior to coastal defense zones. The army places a heavy emphasis on combined-arms operations, mobile artillery systems, and domestic troop self-sufficiency, reinforced by a robust reserve force and high readiness levels.
  • The People’s Air Force maintains a fleet of interceptor jets, multirole fighters, tactical bombers, and unmanned aerial systems, supported by a growing satellite and radar network. It is tasked with aerial defense, reconnaissance, precision strikes, and strategic surveillance, particularly over the Grandic coastline and inland corridors. The air force has undergone rapid modernization in recent years, with notable advancements in drone warfare, early warning systems, and domestic aircraft production, often developed in cooperation with Cusea’s technological partners, including Comussania and Fesia.
  • The People’s Navy operates primarily within the Grandic Ocean zone, focusing on coastal protection, maritime patrol, anti-submarine operations, and search-and-rescue missions. While it does not possess blue-water power projection capabilities, it maintains frigates, fast-attack craft, patrol ships, and amphibious units capable of defending the homeland and supporting amphibious operations near the coast. The navy is particularly active in the defense of Cusea’s strategic islands—such as Verdant Isle and Aquil Island—and plays a key role in monitoring sea lanes, enforcing fishing rights, and protecting offshore infrastructure.

Cusea does not maintain any overseas military bases, consistent with its non-interventionist doctrine and emphasis on territorial defense rather than global projection. However, it is highly active within the Tayanusan Community, participating regularly in joint military drills, defense forums, and regional strategic planning exercises. These joint operations enhance interoperability, deterrence coordination, and shared regional security protocols, positioning Cusea as the leading defense contributor within the continent.

Conscription exists in the form of universal military service, with all eligible citizens undergoing basic training, followed by a multi-tiered national service model that supports rapid mobilization in times of crisis. The armed forces are highly professionalized, with elite academies and technical institutes feeding a steady pipeline of officers, engineers, cyber-specialists, and intelligence analysts into national service.

While Cusea has no declared nuclear weapons, it has developed advanced conventional missile systems, electronic warfare capabilities, and strategic deterrents aimed at preserving national autonomy and dissuading foreign aggression. The state invests heavily in defense research, military infrastructure, and self-reliant arms production, making its defense sector a cornerstone of its industrial strategy.

Law enforcement

Law enforcement in the People’s Republic of Cusea is managed through a centralized and hierarchical system, designed to ensure public order, legal compliance, and the unquestioned authority of the state across all provinces and urban districts. The system is overseen by the Ministry of Internal Security, which coordinates national policing, internal intelligence, and civil protection under a unified doctrine of discipline, social stability, and prevention of disorder.

The primary national agency is the Cusean Public Security Force (CPSF), a well-structured and professional police service with responsibilities that include crime prevention, investigation, border enforcement, traffic control, civil monitoring, and protection of state institutions. The CPSF operates in all nine provinces and the capital district, with specialized directorates for urban policing, rural patrol, anti-theft operations, and strategic surveillance. Officers are trained in national academies and selected through meritocratic exams, with continued assessments of loyalty, efficiency, and legal performance.

Supporting the CPSF is the Special Investigation Bureau (SIB), a federal agency tasked with handling economic crimes, corruption, cyber offenses, and high-profile cases involving state infrastructure or national security. The SIB works closely with provincial governors and the People’s Court to prosecute organized crime, disrupt smuggling networks, and monitor illicit financing. The Bureau is known for its discretion and extensive reach, operating both overtly and covertly across strategic sectors. Internal border checkpoints, ports, and transportation hubs are secured by the Territorial Border Directorate, which works alongside the military and customs to ensure that movement across provinces is monitored, and that entry and exit points comply with national regulations. This agency also handles immigration enforcement, residency permits, and the surveillance of foreign nationals within Cusean territory.

The People’s Court system serves as the final legal authority over law enforcement actions. All arrests, detentions, and prosecutions must comply with national legal standards, and the Office of Judicial Review provides legal counsel to citizens during proceedings. However, the judiciary and police collaborate closely, with no institutional rivalry, ensuring swift enforcement and minimal procedural delays.

Economy

The economy of the People’s Republic of Cusea is a state-directed mixed economy driven by a strategic blend of resource extraction, industrial production, and infrastructure development, all orchestrated through central planning mechanisms. With a GDP of 1.8 trillion DTR, Cusea ranks as the fifth-largest economy in the world, and among the fastest-growing in the Tayanusan continent, bolstered by its vast natural wealth and emphasis on national self-reliance.

At the heart of Cusea’s economic strength is its oil sector, where the country stands as the second-largest oil exporter in the world. The Cusean National Energy Corporation (CNEC), a state-owned enterprise, oversees exploration, extraction, refining, and export of petroleum resources. Oil reserves are concentrated in the Althar Basin, Eastern Plateaux, and select offshore blocks in the Grandic Ocean, where Cusea has built a network of pipelines, refineries, and deep-water terminals. Revenue from oil exports forms the backbone of the national budget, funding infrastructure, welfare programs, military development, and subsidies for industrial expansion.

In parallel with oil, Cusea possesses significant reserves of natural gas, phosphates, and rare minerals, supporting a robust mining and energy cluster under strict environmental and labor regulation. These industries are vertically integrated, with profits reinvested into high-priority sectors like manufacturing, research, and urban development.

The industrial sector is expansive and diversified, encompassing steel production, machinery, petrochemicals, construction materials, and most notably, state-managed arms manufacturing. The government has placed considerable emphasis on building a heavy industrial base capable of supplying domestic needs and meeting export demands within the Tayanusan Community. This includes military-grade equipment, electronics, and railway infrastructure components, much of which is exported to allied nations such as Suklan and Verdania. Industrial zones are concentrated in provinces such as Rashtan, Virindal, and South Altharia, where economic zones benefit from special investment regimes and dedicated logistics corridors.

Agriculture, while less dominant than in the past, remains essential for food security and rural employment. Thanks to state irrigation programs, valleys such as Virendale and Karnathi yield high volumes of wheat, barley, vegetables, and livestock products, often using technologically assisted farming techniques to overcome the country’s arid climate. Cusea maintains strict food price controls and strategic grain reserves to ensure affordability and stability, particularly during global supply shocks.

The tertiary sector is steadily growing, driven by investments in transportation, education, digital infrastructure, and state-owned financial institutions. National banks manage investment portfolios aligned with development priorities, while the government has launched large-scale projects in high-speed rail, digital communications, and renewable energy to diversify the economy and reduce long-term oil dependency. Additionally, Cusea is becoming a regional hub for logistics and trade finance, particularly through its well-developed ports on the Grandic Ocean.

Foreign trade plays a major role in sustaining Cusea’s economic performance. Its primary export partners include Comussania, Fesia, Suklan, and several Tayanusan states, with whom it maintains favorable long-term contracts. While largely self-sufficient in energy and industry, Cusea imports select high-tech components, medical equipment, and specialized machinery, often in exchange for oil and minerals. Trade is regulated through state monopolies, and all foreign investment is channeled through government-supervised industrial zones, ensuring national control over key sectors.

Despite its strengths, the Cusean economy faces challenges related to climatic stress, regional inequality, and overdependence on resource exports. In response, the state has launched the Vision 2035 Plan, aimed at accelerating economic diversification, expanding renewable energy, and nurturing technological innovation, especially in space technologies, water desalination, and civilian AI applications.

Demography

The People’s Republic of Cusea, with a population of approximately 78.4 million, is one of the most populous nations in Tayanusa, and its demographic profile reflects a society marked by youthful energy, steady population growth, and a carefully managed balance between urban development and rural continuity. Cusea’s population density varies significantly across regions, with the highest concentrations found in the Virindal Valley, the coastal provinces, and several inland cities that have become hubs of industrial and administrative activity.

The median age in Cusea is 31.7 years, placing it among the younger nations of the industrialized world. This youthfulness is a result of relatively high birth rates, strong public health services, and a national emphasis on family support and reproductive care. The fertility rate stands at approximately 2.2 children per woman, slightly above replacement level, sustained by generous maternity benefits, child allowances, and housing incentives for young families. The government views population growth as a strategic asset, aligning with national development goals and long-term workforce sustainability.

Cusea’s demographic structure features a broad base of working-age citizens, with a rapidly expanding segment of technically trained youth entering fields such as manufacturing, civil engineering, education, and national defense. The education system, which is compulsory until age 18, ensures near-universal literacy and a consistent stream of skilled workers and civil servants. The labor force participation rate exceeds 67%, with large state employment programs and vocational placement systems guiding career pathways from adolescence onward.

Urbanization is a major demographic trend. Over 69% of the population now lives in urban areas, up from 52% two decades ago. Key cities such as Virindal, Solara, Althar, and Karnath have experienced rapid expansion, supported by massive public investment in housing, transportation, and public services. Despite this growth, the government has retained a strong rural population in the agricultural valleys and desert-edge provinces, supported by development programs aimed at discouraging overconcentration in major cities.

Life expectancy in Cusea averages 76.4 years, with women typically living longer than men. This is attributed to effective public health infrastructure, universal access to medical services, and government-led campaigns in preventive care, vaccination, and maternal health. Infant mortality has dropped significantly in recent years, and major diseases are controlled through widespread screening and treatment initiatives.

Migration into Cusea is minimal, as the country maintains strict control over immigration, allowing only select foreign professionals, technical experts, or workers from allied nations such as Suklan and Comussania. There is little emigration, as economic security and state support retain the population domestically. Refugee intake is managed on a humanitarian basis but kept limited to avoid social strain or disruption to national planning.

Language

The official and universally spoken language of the People’s Republic of Cusea is Tayanusan, a linguistic cornerstone of national identity, cultural continuity, and institutional cohesion. Tayanusan is used in all areas of public life, including government, education, law, military, media, and commerce. It is the sole official language recognized by the constitution, and its promotion is a matter of national policy and civic duty.

While Cusea does not recognize any other language as co-official, regional dialects and minority languages—spoken by small communities such as the Stasians and Sifalamis—are permitted in informal and domestic settings. However, these languages receive no public funding or institutional support, and their use in education, public signage, or administration is restricted. The government encourages cultural preservation privately, but public life is expected to conform to the Tayanusan linguistic standard, reinforcing national unity.

Religion

Religion in the People’s Republic of Cusea is officially recognized as a private and cultural matter, fully separated from the institutions of the state. The constitution affirms Cusea as a secular republic, guaranteeing freedom of belief and worship while maintaining a strict boundary between religious institutions and political authority. Despite this secular framework, Islam is the dominant religion, practiced by approximately 93% of the population, with the majority adhering to mainstream Sunni traditions shaped by Tayanusan historical schools of thought.

Religious institutions operate freely within the law, but are regulated by the State Commission for Religious Affairs, which oversees mosque licensing, clerical training, and the content of religious sermons to ensure alignment with the principles of social harmony and public order. Religious leaders are not permitted to engage in political advocacy, and religious gatherings must be registered and conducted in approved spaces. The government promotes a vision of “socially integrated faith,” wherein religion is respected as a moral force, but kept entirely outside of governance and legislative influence.

The state also recognizes the presence of a significant Christian minority, comprising roughly 7% of the population, primarily concentrated in urban districts and among certain ethnic communities. Churches and Christian institutions are permitted to operate under the same regulatory framework, with equal access to registration, publication, and assembly. The relationship between Christian communities and the state is generally peaceful and cooperative, with interfaith initiatives encouraged at the local level to reinforce national unity across religious lines.

Religious education is restricted to private institutions, and public schools do not include religious instruction. However, history and philosophy curricula introduce students to the role of religion in Cusean civilization as part of a broader cultural heritage. The state encourages scientific rationalism, secular ethics, and civic loyalty as the foundation of education and public life.

Proselytism, extremism, or the use of religion for political mobilization is strictly prohibited. The National Security Code classifies religious incitement, foreign-funded radicalization, and sectarian propaganda as offenses against national unity. This policy has been effective in maintaining inter-religious peace, though it has drawn criticism from international observers concerned with limits on religious autonomy. Domestically, however, most citizens accept the state’s approach as a balanced model of religious freedom and civic responsibility.

In everyday life, religious practice remains strong—particularly in rural areas, provincial cities, and cultural festivals—where mosques are active, religious holidays such as Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha are widely celebrated, and spiritual traditions are woven into communal life. However, religious identity is treated as personal and apolitical, with public life and institutions firmly rooted in secular law, scientific planning, and the ideological foundations of the republic.

Ethnicities

The People’s Republic of Cusea is an ethnically cohesive nation with a clear Cusean majority, yet it is not without internal diversity. Approximately 95% of the population identify as ethnic Cuseans, a group defined by a shared Tayanusan heritage, cultural traditions, language, and historical continuity within the geographic heartland of the republic. This majority forms the backbone of the country’s institutional identity, linguistic unity, and national symbolism, and is predominant in all spheres of public life, from education and administration to the military and civil service.

The state promotes a civic-national model rooted in legal equality and shared loyalty to the republic, rather than ethnic classification. Official censuses do not record ethnic data beyond broad demographics, reflecting the constitutional principle that all citizens are Cuseans, regardless of background. However, independent academic studies and regional records confirm the presence of several minority communities, making up the remaining 5% of the population.

Among these, the most prominent are the Stasians, an ethnocultural group with ancient highland roots, who maintain distinct dialectal and artistic traditions. They are concentrated in the mountainous provinces of the interior, particularly in small towns and rural communes. Despite linguistic and cultural differences, Stasians are fully integrated into national life and widely respected for their contributions to craftsmanship, agricultural innovation, and military service.

Also notable are the Sifalamis, a coastal minority with ancestral ties to maritime trade routes along the Grandic Ocean. They are known for their unique religious customs, cuisine, and communal governance models. Predominantly Muslim like the majority, the Sifalamis nonetheless maintain distinct social rituals and cultural expressions, especially in cities like Solara and East Virindal, where their merchant communities historically flourished.

Smaller groups include a mix of long-settled Tayanusan nomads, urbanized mixed-heritage families, and a limited number of naturalized foreign workers from allied nations such as Suklan, Verdania, and Comussania. These individuals are generally concentrated in the industrial zones and ports, where they contribute to manufacturing, logistics, and technical services. While their numbers are modest, they are protected under national labor and residency laws, and granted full civil rights if naturalized.

Importantly, Cusea does not enforce assimilation policies. Minority communities are allowed to preserve their customs, languages, and internal structures within the bounds of national law. However, public life—including education, administration, and media—remains entirely centered on Tayanusan language and civic culture, and there is no recognition of minority autonomy or ethnic political representation. This approach ensures cultural pluralism in private life, while preserving a uniform public identity based on shared national belonging.

Interethnic relations are generally peaceful, and the government promotes “unity through civic equality,” rather than through forced integration or multiculturalism. Communal harmony is supported by local councils, interfaith forums, and national campaigns emphasizing common purpose, historical sacrifice, and the collective project of socialist development.
