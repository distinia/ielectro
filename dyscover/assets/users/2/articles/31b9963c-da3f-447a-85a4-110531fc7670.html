Lamberia, officially the Islamic Republic of Lamberia, is a unitary parliamentary republic located in the northern region of Tayanusa. It has a population of 53,047,324 and covers an area of 721,324 km². The capital and largest city is Tarek, while other major cities include Nourat and Ladhrat. The official and universally spoken language is Tayanusan, shared by both the majority Lamberian (87%) and minority Racabi (13%) ethnic groups. The country is overwhelmingly Muslim (95%), with a small Christian minority (5%), and maintains a constitutional system that balances Islamic identity with secular governance.

Lamberia is widely regarded as the only true democracy in Tayanusa, featuring a multi-party system, free elections, and robust civil liberties. The government consists of a President, a Prime Minister , and a unicameral Parliament. The judicial system is independent, headed by Chief Judge. Administratively, the country is divided into 15 provinces and follows a centralized governance model.

Lamberia is the largest oil exporter in the world, and its economy—valued at 3.4 trillion DTR—is driven by the energy sector, industrial manufacturing, and infrastructure development. It maintains strong economic and military ties with Destenia, its closest ally, and sees itself as the leader of the Tayanusan Community, often competing with Cusea for regional influence. Relations with Suklan are tense but cordial, while Lamberia remains skeptical of Edrobean political models despite economic cooperation with the Edrobean Community.

Geographically, Lamberia features a diverse landscape of deserts, mountains, rivers, and coastlines along the Grandic Ocean, Blue Sea, and Sea of Ricene. It includes strategic territories such as the Aletian Islands, and its highest point is Mont Redouzi. The country is largely urbanized, with a young and growing population, high literacy rates, and a strong emphasis on education, public health, and national unity.

Etymology

The name Lamberia is believed to derive from the ancient Tayanusan word “Lambir,” which translates roughly to “land of fortitude” or “the resolute land.” This term has deep roots in the mountainous northern Tayanusan highlands, where early Lamberian tribes resisted both imperial invasions and harsh climatic conditions. Over time, “Lambir” became a symbol of unity and perseverance for the people, especially during the period of national awakening in the late 20th century.

The suffix “-ia”, common among Tayanusan and Edrobean languages for denoting sovereign territories, was later appended to “Lambir” during the unification movement to signify a formal national identity. The full name, Islamic Republic of Lamberia, reflects both its majority-Muslim population and its republican governance, while “Lamberia” alone is used in most international and informal contexts to emphasize national distinctiveness.

Geography

Lamberia, located in the northern part of the Tayanusa region, is a country blessed with a diverse and captivating geography that showcases a stunning variety of landscapes. From vast deserts and majestic mountain ranges to fertile plains and abundant waterways, the nation’s physical terrain reflects striking contrasts and a deep connection to its natural heritage.

To the north, Lamberia extends to the shores of the Grandic Ocean, where it also claims sovereignty over the Aletian Islands, a scattered archipelago that expands the nation's maritime jurisdiction and contributes to its considerable coastline. The southern frontier is defined by the Blue Sea and the Sea of Ricene, providing the country with critical access to coastal trade routes and fostering vibrant port cities along its southern shores. To the east, Lamberia borders Stasia, while the western frontier connects it to the state of Ricene, with whom it shares historical, cultural, and geographic links.

With a total surface area of 721,324 km², Lamberia is among the largest countries in the Tayanusan region. The country’s border perimeter stretches over 10,600 kilometers, encompassing both mainland territory and island holdings. An impressive 85% of the landmass is dominated by desert ecosystems, notably the Lamberian Desert, one of the most arid zones on the continent. Rising from these harsh terrains are the Sand Mountains, which characterize much of the eastern landscape. Within this range lies Mont Redouzi, the highest point in Lamberia, whose elevation offers both a symbol of national pride and a challenging frontier for scientific and exploratory missions.

Further enriching the country's topography are two primary mountain systems. In the south, the Allahzi Mountains form a formidable wall of stone and wind-carved peaks, serving as a climatic buffer and a cultural boundary for the southern tribes. In the west, the Ludouzi Mountains rise gently but dramatically, offering breathtaking panoramic vistas and serving as a vital reservoir for biodiversity and mineral wealth. These mountain regions are revered not only for their physical majesty but also for their spiritual significance in Lamberian tradition.

Water remains a crucial element in the country's geography despite the arid climate. The most prominent body of water is Al-Shaimsa Lake, a vast inland basin surrounded by oases and settlements that depend on its life-giving waters. Although other lakes and seasonal rivers dot the territory, none rival the size or influence of Al-Shaimsa. Complementing these freshwater sources are the three seas that embrace Lamberia: the Blue Sea, the Sea of Ricene, and the Grandic Ocean. These maritime domains not only enhance the country’s geostrategic significance but also support its fishing industry, naval mobility, and tourism sector.

Traversing the desert heartland is the Setraki River, the longest river in Lamberia, running 652 kilometers from the western hills to the Sea of Ricene. This river is a vital artery for agriculture, trade, and transportation, and forms the backbone of irrigation systems that sustain key urban and rural populations. Most rivers in Lamberia are seasonal, yet they converge into a network that flows toward the northern or southeastern coasts, shaping the hydrological character of the nation.

Politics

Government

The Islamic Republic of Lamberia operates under a unitary parliamentary republic, blending Islamic values with a constitutional secularism that guides its political framework. Despite its formal designation as an "Islamic Republic," the state structure is grounded in pluralist governance and civil law, where Islam is recognized as the state religion, but freedom of belief and judicial neutrality are protected under the constitution adopted on 13 January 2021. The Lamberian government is built on the principles of popular sovereignty, parliamentary accountability, and institutional balance, and is defined by three core branches: the executive, the legislative, and the judiciary.

At the apex of the executive branch stands the President of the Republic, currently Ahmed Al-Farsi, who serves as head of state, guardian of the constitution, and symbol of national unity. Though largely ceremonial in peacetime, the President holds critical powers in times of emergency, including the authority to dissolve Parliament, declare war, and veto legislation pending parliamentary review. The President also appoints key national officials, including the Prime Minister, based on parliamentary alignment.

The Prime Minister, presently Lachen Hassan, is the head of government and wields substantial executive authority. As the leader of the Council of Ministers, the Prime Minister is responsible for formulating domestic and foreign policy, overseeing national ministries, and ensuring the execution of laws passed by Parliament. The Prime Minister must maintain the confidence of the Parliament, and their appointment requires confirmation by a majority vote in the legislature. The government may be dismissed by a vote of no confidence, affirming the parliamentary character of Lamberia’s political system.

The Parliament of Lamberia is a unicameral legislature, composed of elected representatives from all provinces and constituencies. It functions as the supreme legislative body, responsible for enacting laws, approving the national budget, ratifying international treaties, and exercising oversight over the executive branch. The Speaker of Parliament, currently Omar Abdelrahman, presides over parliamentary sessions and plays a key role in mediating legislative debates and guaranteeing procedural order. Parliamentary elections are held every four years, and the system is based on proportional representation to ensure broad political participation across ethnic and regional lines.

The judicial branch is headed by the Chief Judge of the Republic, Hassan Al-Mutairi, who presides over the Supreme Court of Lamberia, the highest constitutional and appellate authority in the land. The judiciary is independent by law and charged with upholding the constitution, interpreting legislation, and guaranteeing civil liberties. Special religious and customary courts operate in parallel with civil courts for personal status issues, especially in matters involving marriage, inheritance, and family law, but always within the constitutional framework.

Political parties

Lamberia is widely regarded as the only true democracy of Tayanusa, a distinction rooted in its pluralistic political system, vibrant electoral culture, and firm constitutional protections for freedom of speech, association, and press. In contrast to many neighboring states—often marked by authoritarian tendencies, dynastic rule, or theocratic dominance—Lamberia has emerged as a beacon of representative governance, where multi-party competition, peaceful power transitions, and institutional accountability are the norm.

The country’s political landscape is defined by a diverse party system, structured around ideological, ethnic, and regional identities, with a legal framework that guarantees the right to form political parties, participate in elections, and criticize government actions. All registered parties must adhere to the Constitution of the Republic, respect national sovereignty, and operate within a democratic framework. The Electoral Commission of Lamberia, an independent constitutional body, oversees all elections and ensures their transparency and fairness. Among the major political parties in Lamberia:

  • The Lamberian Democratic Party (LDP) is the largest and most influential party, advocating for moderate Islamic values, economic modernization, and regional integration. It has governed in several coalitions since 2020 and is known for its technocratic leadership and diplomatic foreign policy.
  • The Racabi Justice Movement (RJM) represents much of the Racabi minority and focuses on minority rights, decentralization, and social equity. It often aligns with broader progressive coalitions in Parliament and has a strong base in the western provinces.
  • The National Conservative Front (NCF) is a right-wing party that promotes traditionalist Islamic governance, cultural preservation, and national defense. Though occasionally accused of sectarian rhetoric, it remains within constitutional boundaries and participates fully in democratic life.
  • The Social Renewal Bloc (SRB) is a secular leftist party pushing for labor reforms, education expansion, and state-led economic development. It garners significant support among youth, trade unions, and urban workers.
  • The Green Crescent Party (GCP), a newer but growing force, centers its platform around environmental protection, anti-corruption, and sustainable development, reflecting increasing ecological awareness across the country.

Elections in Lamberia occur on a fixed four-year cycle for Parliament, with the Prime Minister chosen from the majority or ruling coalition. Presidential elections, held every six years, are often symbolic but feature active national campaigns. Voter turnout in recent cycles has exceeded 76%, a testament to the country's engaged electorate and trust in the democratic process. Freedom of the press, judicial independence, and a politically active civil society reinforce the democratic culture of Lamberia. Debates in Parliament are often lively, with televised sessions and vibrant political discourse extending into universities, local councils, and mosques.

Administrative subdivisions

Lamberia is administratively structured as a unitary state divided into 15 provinces, known in Tayanusan as wilayat. This subdivision system is designed to facilitate efficient governance while preserving national unity across a diverse and geographically vast country. The provincial structure reflects both historical regions and modern administrative considerations, enabling the central government to implement policies effectively while accommodating local needs and identities.

Each province functions as the primary administrative unit below the national level, serving as a regional extension of the central government. Provinces vary significantly in terms of geography, population density, economic activity, and ethnic composition, yet all operate under a standardized framework set forth by the Ministry of Interior and Territorial Affairs. At the head of each province is a Governor (Wali), who is appointed directly by the central government in Tarek, typically upon the recommendation of the Prime Minister and with oversight from Parliament. The governor acts as the state’s highest representative in the province, tasked with implementing national laws, coordinating security, and managing public services. Although governors are not elected, their administrative staff and policy decisions are closely monitored by local advisory councils.

Below the provinces are districts, or mudiriat, which serve as secondary divisions and are responsible for more localized governance. Each district has its own administrative director and staff, tasked with overseeing municipal services, infrastructure, education, and local economic development. Within districts, there are municipalities (baladiyat), which manage city-level administration, particularly in urbanized areas.

While the structure is centralized, each province has a Provincial Council, composed of representatives either elected or appointed from local stakeholders, religious leaders, and civil society figures. These councils have advisory authority and provide feedback to the national government on provincial priorities, cultural matters, and development needs, fostering a limited but meaningful consultative process. The administrative division into 15 provinces ensures a balanced territorial organization, allowing the national government to maintain cohesive control while granting room for regional variation and cultural representation, especially in provinces with significant Racabi or mixed populations. This structure also plays a critical role in election logistics, infrastructure planning, security coordination, and disaster response, making it foundational to Lamberia’s internal governance.

Foreign relations

Lamberia’s foreign policy is shaped by a firm commitment to diplomacy, multilateralism, and the principle of sovereign equality among nations. As a nation with a rising regional profile and a long-standing tradition of non-aggression, Lamberia positions itself as a bridge between cultures, blocs, and continents, particularly between the Tayanusan, Edrobean, and Litherian spheres. Its diplomatic doctrine emphasizes strategic neutrality, regional leadership, and pragmatic alliances, reflecting a desire to preserve both ideological independence and economic security in a shifting global order.

At the heart of Lamberia’s regional strategy lies its self-perception as the natural leader of the Tayanusan Community (TC), a stance that has elevated its role within the organization but has also fostered a subtle but growing rivalry with Cusea, another influential power in Tayanusa. While both countries are founding members of the TC and collaborate on trade and infrastructure integration, their competition for political influence over smaller Tayanusan states—especially those with fragile institutions or mixed religious demographics—has generated a quiet but persistent contest for hegemony in the region.

Lamberia’s closest and most strategic ally on the global stage is Destenia, with whom it shares deep and expanding ties. The relationship is grounded primarily in economic cooperation, particularly in the areas of energy, infrastructure development, and technological exchange. However, the cornerstone of this alliance lies in the military-industrial domain, as Destenia serves as Lamberia’s main weapons supplier, enabling the latter to modernize its armed forces while maintaining technological parity in a volatile region. The two countries often coordinate positions in global forums, particularly within the United Nations, where they promote a multipolar order, developmental sovereignty, and collective security frameworks.

Relations with Suklan remain complex. While no direct hostilities have occurred in recent decades, political and religious tensions linger beneath the surface. Lamberia views Suklan’s isolationist posture and sectarian undertones with caution, while Suklan remains wary of Lamberia’s expanding influence in maritime Tayanusa. Nevertheless, both countries maintain cordial diplomatic relations, conduct bilateral dialogues, and collaborate within multilateral settings such as the Global Reserve Fund and the International Energy Agency. These engagements help prevent friction from escalating into open rivalry.

Lamberia maintains a skeptical yet pragmatic posture toward the Edrobean world and Valmirica, often criticizing what it calls the hypocrisy of Edrobean democracies, particularly on issues of interventionism, human and migrant rights. Despite these ideological differences, Lamberia is an active cooperating partner of the Edrobean Community and Valmirica, participating in economic initiatives, climate talks, and counterterrorism frameworks, as long as such cooperation respects Lamberian sovereignty and avoids political conditionalities.

The country’s multilateral diplomacy is also evident in its active membership in global organizations such as the United Nations, the Islamic Cooperation Organization, the Global Reserve Fund, and the International Energy Agency. Lamberia uses these platforms to promote fairer trade terms, energy equity, and non-aligned security cooperation. It consistently advocates for reform of global governance structures, calling for greater voice and representation for Tayanusan and Global South nations.

Military

The military of Lamberia, formally known as the Lamberian National Defense Forces (LNDF), is a professional, modern, and highly disciplined armed force tasked with defending the sovereignty, territorial integrity, and strategic interests of the Islamic Republic of Lamberia. As a non-aggressive but highly vigilant nation, Lamberia maintains a defensive military doctrine rooted in deterrence, rapid mobilization, and close cooperation with key strategic allies—most notably Destenia, from which it sources a significant portion of its advanced weaponry and defense technologies. The LNDF is composed of four primary branches:

  • The Lamberian Army is the largest component of the military, responsible for land-based defense, border protection, and internal security support. It maintains a combination of mechanized divisions, infantry brigades, and desert warfare units, with strong emphasis on training in arid environments and mountain operations due to the country's demanding terrain. The Army is structured around three regional commands, each overseeing deployments in the north, center, and southern provinces.
  • The Lamberian Navy, while comparatively modest in size, plays a crucial role in securing the Blue Sea, Sea of Ricene, and Grandic Ocean maritime borders. It operates a fleet of frigates, patrol vessels, and coastal defense units, many of which have been acquired or co-developed in partnership with Destenia. The Navy also maintains rapid-reaction marine detachments, particularly for defending the Aletian Islands and conducting anti-smuggling operations.
  • The Lamberian Air Force is a growing and technologically advancing arm of the military. It controls a range of fighter aircraft, transport planes, and surveillance drones, and plays a pivotal role in border patrol, airspace sovereignty, and strategic reconnaissance. Through its partnership with Destenia, Lamberia has gained access to 4.5-generation combat aircraft, advanced radar systems, and electronic warfare capabilities.
  • The Republican Guard, a highly trained and politically loyal elite corps, is tasked with the protection of national institutions, the President, and key government facilities. The Guard is also deployed in special internal operations, counterterrorism, and national emergencies, and is considered the most ideologically committed and best-equipped force in the country.

The military is commanded by the Supreme Council of National Defense, chaired by the President of the Republic, with operational control exercised by the Chief of the General Staff, who oversees strategic planning, resource allocation, and coordination among branches. Civilian oversight is guaranteed through the Ministry of Defense, which answers to Parliament and ensures alignment with national policy.

Lamberia maintains a professional volunteer force with no compulsory conscription, although the constitution permits a universal mobilization clause in times of war or existential threat. The total number of active personnel stands at approximately 260,000, with an additional 150,000 in reserve formations. The defense industry is steadily growing, focusing on light arms manufacturing, aerospace maintenance, and desert-adapted armored vehicles. However, for high-end military technology, Lamberia remains closely dependent on imports from Destenia, which has become not only its main weapons supplier but also a partner in training, doctrine development, and cyber defense.

Strategically, the LNDF has adopted a forward-defense model, deploying early-warning systems and rapid-response battalions near vulnerable maritime and land borders, especially near the Suklan frontier, where historical tensions require heightened readiness. Despite those tensions, Lamberia maintains a strict policy of non-intervention, rejects the establishment of foreign military bases on its soil, and refuses to participate in offensive coalitions, reinforcing its image as a sovereign, defensive power.

Law enforcement

The law enforcement system of Lamberia is a centralized, multi-tiered structure designed to uphold public order, state security, and the rule of law within the framework of a constitutional republic. While operating under the overarching authority of the Ministry of Justice and Internal Security, Lamberia’s police forces maintain a high degree of professionalism, discipline, and public accountability, reflecting the country’s status as the only true democracy in Tayanusa. Law enforcement agencies are empowered by the Lamberian Penal Code and must act in compliance with the constitutional protections of civil liberties, including freedom of expression, fair trial, and privacy.

The primary force is the Lamberian National Police (LNP), which serves as the general-purpose civil police across the entire territory. The LNP is responsible for crime prevention, public safety, criminal investigations, and traffic enforcement. Structured into provincial commands, the force operates local stations in every district and municipality, ensuring widespread presence and rapid response capabilities. Officers undergo intensive training at the National Police Academy in Tarek, where they receive education not only in law enforcement techniques but also in human rights, community relations, and non-lethal conflict resolution.

In parallel to the LNP, the Judicial Police Directorate (JPD) functions as a specialized investigative agency under the oversight of the Ministry of Justice and the Public Prosecutor’s Office. Its officers work alongside judges and prosecutors in conducting forensic analysis, financial investigations, organized crime probes, and anti-corruption operations. The JPD plays a key role in Lamberia’s efforts to maintain judicial independence and enforce transparency laws, especially in cases involving public officials or transnational crimes.

The Border and Maritime Security Corps (BMSC), a militarized police force, is tasked with protecting Lamberia’s land borders, ports, and coastlines. Operating in coordination with the Navy, it is responsible for anti-smuggling operations, illegal migration control, and monitoring of restricted zones such as the Grandic Ocean islands. The BMSC also conducts joint drills with Destenian advisors, especially in counter-piracy and maritime interdiction.

For internal threats, terrorism prevention, and intelligence operations, Lamberia relies on the General Directorate for National Security (GDNS), the highest security agency in the country. The GDNS has both domestic and foreign intelligence mandates, with divisions focusing on cybersecurity, counterterrorism, and surveillance of extremist networks. Although secretive in nature, the GDNS is subject to parliamentary oversight through a special security committee, ensuring its actions remain consistent with national law.

Maintaining public confidence in law enforcement is a key concern of the Lamberian state. The Police Ethics Board, an independent civilian-led institution, is responsible for investigating misconduct, abuse of power, and violations of rights by law enforcement personnel. Citizens can submit complaints anonymously, and findings are regularly published to the public to guarantee transparency. In rural and sparsely populated regions—especially in the southern and desert provinces—the Regional Constabulary Forces (RCF) operate as auxiliary units trained in both police and civil affairs. Their role includes conflict mediation, tribal liaison, and emergency response, helping bridge the gap between state institutions and isolated communities.

Lamberia has invested heavily in digital policing infrastructure, including nationwide biometric identification, centralized criminal databases, and real-time surveillance systems in urban centers. However, strict regulations exist to protect against invasive practices, and any surveillance initiative requires judicial authorization. Ultimately, Lamberia’s law enforcement model is defined by a blend of central control, institutional accountability, and local adaptability, making it one of the most advanced and respected policing systems in the Tayanusan region. Its ability to balance state authority with civil liberties reflects the country's broader democratic ethos and commitment to justice through rule of law.

Economy

The economy of Lamberia is a powerful and strategically positioned engine within the Tayanusan region and the broader global market. With a GDP of 3.4 trillion DTR, it ranks as the third-largest economy in the world, driven primarily by its unparalleled dominance in the energy sector, alongside strong performance in infrastructure development, industrial manufacturing, and logistics services. Despite its formal designation as an Islamic Republic, the country follows a market-oriented economic model with state oversight in strategic sectors, especially in energy, mining, and defense.

Lamberia holds the distinction of being the largest oil exporter in the world, with daily exports exceeding 11 million barrels, contributing to over 40% of its GDP and nearly 70% of government revenue. Its vast petroleum reserves—particularly those in the Ricene Basin, the Deshari Desert Fields, and the offshore platforms in the Grandic Ocean and Blue Sea—have made Lamberia a central pillar of global energy supply. The national oil conglomerate, LAMOIL, is state-owned but operates under corporate structures similar to multinational firms, maintaining joint ventures with firms from Destenia, the Edrobean Community, and the Tayanusan bloc.

Natural gas, liquefied petroleum, and petrochemicals also form a significant part of the export portfolio, with Lamberia investing heavily in refining capacity, pipeline infrastructure, and gas-to-liquid (GTL) technologies. The country’s membership in the International Energy Agency and the Global Reserve Fund allows it to exert considerable influence on energy policy, price stabilization, and emergency reserves coordination across multiple continents.

While energy dominates, Lamberia has actively pursued a diversification strategy, led by the Ministry of Economic Development, to reduce dependence on hydrocarbons. This includes significant investments in renewable energy, heavy industries, agriculture modernization, and transportation logistics, especially along the DesTay Corridor—a vast infrastructure project linking Lamberia with Destenia and other Tayanusan economies through railways, ports, and highways. The industrial sector, comprising steel, cement, chemicals, and automotive assembly, accounts for about 25% of GDP. Several industrial zones, particularly around the capital Tarek, have attracted foreign direct investment due to tax incentives, high-skilled labor, and state guarantees. Lamberia also maintains a growing defense manufacturing sector, producing small arms, armored vehicles, and communication systems, many of which are exported to allied nations.

The agricultural sector, though limited by arid geography, remains vital for food security and rural employment. Through extensive irrigation projects supported by the Setraki River and water conservation programs near Al-Shaimsa Lake, Lamberia produces dates, citrus fruits, grains, and legumes, with significant volumes directed to domestic consumption and export to neighboring countries. Lamberia’s services sector—particularly finance, telecommunications, and construction—has grown rapidly in urban areas. The financial capital Nourat is home to regional branches of several global banks, while local fintech startups are beginning to digitize public services and consumer transactions. The Lamberian Riyal (LBR) is a stable, non-convertible currency managed by the Central Bank of Lamberia, which uses sovereign wealth funds to stabilize inflation and cushion budget shocks during oil price fluctuations.

Trade policy is oriented around bilateral agreements with Destenia, the Edrobean Community, and Tayanusan states. Lamberia is a founding member of the Tayanusan Economic Compact, promoting intra-regional trade liberalization and infrastructure harmonization. Despite its skepticism toward Edrobean political models, Lamberia maintains active commercial ties with Edrobean ports and industries, particularly in raw materials, energy, and construction services. Unemployment is officially around 5.8%, though rural underemployment remains a concern. The government has launched massive public works programs and vocational education campaigns to increase workforce participation among youth and women, while maintaining a robust welfare system funded by energy revenues.

Demography

Lamberia’s demography reflects a dynamic and steadily growing nation, shaped by youthful vitality, urban expansion, and a strong emphasis on national cohesion. With a total population of 53,047,324, the Islamic Republic of Lamberia ranks among the most populous countries in the Tayanusan region, serving as a key demographic engine for regional development. Its population density varies significantly by geography, with dense concentrations along coastal areas and river systems, and sparser settlements across the interior deserts and mountain ranges.

The population is predominantly young, with a median age of 28.6 years, a consequence of high birth rates during the early 2000s and effective health campaigns that have reduced infant and maternal mortality. Over 60% of citizens are under the age of 35, giving the country a vast labor pool and substantial human capital for future growth. This youth bulge is both a strategic asset and a policy challenge, driving national investments in education, vocational training, and job creation.

Lamberia's urbanization rate has surged in the last two decades, with approximately 71% of the population now residing in urban centers. Major cities include the capital Tarek, which alone houses nearly 8 million residents and functions as the political, cultural, and religious heart of the nation; Nourat, the economic and financial hub; and Ladhrat, known for its industrial base and vibrant working-class districts. Rapid urban growth has led to the development of modern infrastructure, but it has also placed pressure on housing, transportation, and public services—issues being addressed through ambitious urban renewal programs.

The rural population, comprising the remaining 29%, is primarily engaged in agriculture, pastoralism, and resource extraction, especially in the desert provinces and mountain valleys. Government policies aim to reduce regional inequality through infrastructure expansion, rural electrification, and the digital inclusion of remote communities. Life expectancy in Lamberia has risen significantly in recent decades, currently standing at 76 years for women and 73 years for men. This improvement is largely due to expanded access to public healthcare, nationwide vaccination programs, and improvements in nutrition and water sanitation, particularly in underserved regions. The country boasts a universal healthcare system, with additional private providers operating in urban centers under strict regulation.

In terms of family structure, Lamberia combines traditional and modern values. While extended families remain common in rural areas, nuclear households now dominate in urban regions. Marriage rates are high, and the average household size is 4.2 persons, though this number has been declining slightly due to economic modernization and shifting cultural norms. Fertility rates have declined to an average of 2.5 children per woman, reflecting increased female education, urbanization, and access to reproductive healthcare. Nevertheless, this rate remains above replacement level, ensuring continued population growth well into the mid-21st century.

Lamberia also maintains a relatively small but growing diaspora population, primarily in Destenia, Comussania, and Edrobean countries, where Lamberians pursue education, business, and professional careers. Remittances from the diaspora contribute modestly to the economy and are viewed as an important bridge to the global Lamberian community.

Language

Language in Lamberia serves as a powerful unifying force, reflecting both the country's national identity and its inclusive civic culture. The sole official language of the Islamic Republic of Lamberia is Tayanusan, which is used in government administration, education, media, and legal affairs across all regions and social strata. Despite the country’s ethnic diversity, the linguistic landscape is remarkably cohesive: both Lamberians and Racabi minorities speak Tayanusan fluently, making Lamberia one of the few multi-ethnic nations in the region with a single, shared linguistic foundation.

Tayanusan in Lamberia has evolved into a standardized national dialect, often referred to as Lamberian Tayanusan, distinguished by its clear pronunciation, modern lexicon, and incorporation of administrative and scientific terms borrowed from Edrobean languages. This variant is used in national broadcasts, state education, and official publications, reinforcing a sense of shared civic identity. While Racabi is not recognized as an official or regional language, elements of Racabi culture and oral tradition remain present in poetry, music, and local religious ceremonies—especially in the western provinces where Racabi communities are concentrated. The state supports cultural preservation programs that allow Racabi heritage to thrive within the broader framework of Tayanusan linguistic unity, ensuring that ethnic coexistence is maintained without fragmenting national communication.

All students, regardless of background, are taught in Tayanusan from early schooling, and literacy rates exceed 96%, thanks in part to universal education policies and the widespread availability of public media in accessible dialects. In urban centers such as Tarek, Nourat, and Al-Muharraq, Tayanusan is also used in informal commercial signage, street culture, and digital media, making it not only the language of the state but also the everyday language of the people.

Religion

Religion in Lamberia plays a profound yet constitutionally balanced role in the country’s national identity, social fabric, and institutional life. While the state is officially designated as the Islamic Republic of Lamberia, the constitution is largely secular, ensuring freedom of worship, protection of religious minorities, and the non-compulsory nature of religious observance in public affairs. This delicate balance between Islamic heritage and republican governance has helped make Lamberia one of the most religiously tolerant and stable nations in Tayanusa.

An overwhelming 95% of the population is Muslim, with the vast majority adhering to Sunni Islam, specifically following the Maliki school of jurisprudence. Islam is recognized as the state religion, and many aspects of national life—such as public holidays, cultural values, and moral discourse—are influenced by Islamic tradition. The Ministry of Religious Affairs and Endowments oversees the administration of mosques, the training of imams, and the certification of Islamic charities, ensuring that religious institutions align with national law while preserving their spiritual autonomy.

The state does not impose religious law in civil or criminal matters. Instead, civil courts govern most legal proceedings, with religious courts limited to personal status issues such as marriage, divorce, inheritance, and guardianship—always within the bounds of constitutional protections. Participation in religious courts is voluntary and subject to the consent of all parties involved. The Supreme Court of Lamberia has jurisdiction over religious cases in the event of constitutional conflicts, safeguarding against theocratic overreach.

The remaining 5% of the population is Christian, primarily composed of Catholic communities, concentrated in urban centers such as Tarek, Ladhrat, and parts of western Lamberia, where Racabi Christians maintain vibrant traditions. Christian citizens enjoy full civil rights, and the government supports interfaith councils, protected worship sites, and minority language publications to ensure cultural and religious continuity. Christians serve in public office, the judiciary, and the military without discrimination, reflecting the country’s commitment to religious pluralism. Lamberia’s religious policy explicitly rejects sectarianism and bans the political use of religion. Political parties and religious organizations are prohibited from inciting division or claiming exclusive representation of religious identities. Religious leaders who engage in political speech are subject to judicial oversight, and mosques or churches found violating laws of incitement or hate speech are subject to suspension or dissolution.

In education, religious instruction is offered in public schools as an optional subject, with separate curricula available for Muslim and Christian students. Parents may also opt their children out entirely. Religious content in textbooks is carefully regulated to emphasize tolerance, shared values, and civic responsibility. Public life in Lamberia reflects this equilibrium. Islamic holidays such as Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha are national holidays, but Christian holidays like Christmas and Easter are recognized and respected, especially in Christian-majority neighborhoods. Public fasts and processions are common, but participation is never mandatory, and government workplaces remain inclusive and diverse.

Ethnicities

Lamberia is a multiethnic republic characterized by a strong sense of national unity, fostered through a shared language, civic identity, and inclusive governance. Despite its rich ethnic composition, the country has successfully avoided sectarianism or ethnic marginalization, instead embracing a model of integration without assimilation, where diverse communities contribute to a common national project.

The majority of the population—87%—identify as ethnic Lamberians, who trace their ancestry to the native desert and coastal tribes of northern Tayanusa. Lamberians are culturally tied to the Tayanusan heritage, with deep historical roots in the region’s Islamic scholarship, nomadic traditions, and urban trade networks. They are predominantly Sunni Muslim and form the social, political, and cultural core of the republic. Lamberians are distributed throughout the country but are especially concentrated in the central and eastern provinces, as well as in the capital Tarek and industrial hubs such as Nourat and Al-Muharraq.

The second-largest ethnic group is the Racabi minority, comprising approximately 13% of the population. Racabis are an ancient people originating from the western mountain regions and are distinguished by cultural customs, religious diversity, and communal traditions that set them apart from the Lamberian majority. While most Racabis are Muslim, there is also a significant Christian minority among them, especially in the highland provinces. Despite historical marginalization during pre-republican regimes, the Racabi people have been fully integrated into modern Lamberian society, with strong representation in education, business, civil society, and even national politics—notably through the Racabi Justice Movement, a key political party advocating for regional equity and cultural preservation.

A small but present 3% of the population falls into the tag of Others, which includes Tayanusan migrants, Edrobean expatriates, and descendants of long-settled Litherians. These communities are mostly urban-based and often engage in specialized professions, including academia, international trade, and technology sectors. While not large in number, they contribute to the cosmopolitan character of cities like Tarek and Ladhrat and are protected by national anti-discrimination laws.

Lamberia does not officially categorize citizens based on ethnicity in civil documents, in line with its constitutional commitment to equal citizenship. Instead, the state promotes a concept known as “Unified National Identity”, which emphasizes linguistic unity (Tayanusan), civic participation, and shared historical memory—particularly the unification struggle and the drafting of the 2021 constitution.
