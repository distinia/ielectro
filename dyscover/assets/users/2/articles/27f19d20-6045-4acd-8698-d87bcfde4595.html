The Revolutionaries are the ruling political movement of Destenia, established on 17 July 2020 by Destiny Omagu. Headquartered in Marpoli, Revolutionaries has the largest organizations in Destenia, with a membership of nearly 6 million. Revolutionaries holds a dominant position in Destenian politics and society, controlling the government.

Despite supporting regular free elections, the party operates as the dominant party, maintaining overwhelming influence across the government, media, civil society, and the education system. The party organizes its leadership through the Leaderate, a flexible and informal body composed of the most powerful figures in the party and government, appointed by the elected Leader. It also operates a structured Secretariat to manage daily operations and a powerful Inspectorate to uphold internal accountability. Annual Congresses, held at national and sub-national levels, serve as venues for internal elections, ideological reaffirmation, and strategic planning. Affiliated organizations and various associations extend the party's reach into youth, labor, media, sport, and rural sectors. Membership is open to all citizens willing to undergo a probationary period and take an oath of allegiance, with full members participating in party elections and discussions. Revolutionaries remains central to Destenia’s identity as a multiethnic, meritocratic, and sovereign country, championing a vision of disciplined governance, economic independence, and social unity.

Ideology and factions of Revolutionaries center on Destenism, the official doctrine of the Revolutionaries, formulated by Destiny Omagu as a comprehensive political–philosophical framework integrating centralized authority, disciplined democratic legitimacy, civic nationalism, enforced meritocracy, and absolute sovereignty. Grounded in the Nation of the Forgotten, Destenism interprets exclusion and marginalization as structural and cyclical realities, rejects utopian finality, and elevates loyalty and merit as the forces that transform fragmentation into collective power. Institutionally, it advances the New State, prioritizing order as the precondition of freedom, assimilation over fragmentation, and competence over entitlement, while politically expressing itself through totalitarian democracy, where elections, rights, and republican institutions coexist with centralized authority, bounded pluralism, and executive primacy under legality and accountability. Economically, Destenism supports a state-led mixed economy, rejecting neoliberal globalism and collectivist planning, defending strategic public control, robust welfare, and patriotic reinvestment by elites; in foreign affairs, it embraces non-alignment, anti-imperialism, a multipolar order, and a clash of civilizations perspective; underpinning all is adaptive sovereignty, affirming continuous adaptation, permanent renewal, and survival through realism. Within the Revolutionaries, these principles generate fluid internal factions united by Destenism rather than rigid ideology: Liberals emphasize social justice, inclusion, welfare, and participatory reform; Conservatives stress discipline, cultural order, patriotism, militarism, and sovereignty; Realists prioritize stability, efficiency, and institutional power, valuing hierarchy over doctrine; and Ideologues act as purists of Destenism, demanding permanent revolution, denouncing stagnation, and enforcing ideological vigilance—together forming a mass movement in which plural tendencies coexist.

Political stance

Ideology

Destenism is the official ideological doctrine of the Revolutionaries, formulated by Destiny Omagu as a comprehensive political–philosophical framework rather than a single policy doctrine. It synthesizes theories developed across social analysis, governance, democracy, geopolitics, and survival theory, combining centralized authority, structured democratic legitimacy, civic nationalism, enforced meritocracy, and absolute sovereignty into a coherent system of statecraft. Rejecting the binary opposition between liberal democracy and authoritarianism, Destenism advances a model in which strong state leadership coexists with popular participation, asserting that democracy must be disciplined, purposeful, and institutionally protected in order to function as a tool of nation-building rather than a source of fragmentation, paralysis, or foreign manipulation.

At the philosophical core of Destenism lies the concept of the Nation of the Forgotten, a realist interpretation of history and society that views exclusion, humiliation, and marginalization as recurring structural conditions rather than moral anomalies. According to this framework, individuals and groups become “Forgotten” not through a single act of injustice, but through repeated and systemic exclusion across multiple domains of life, producing cycles of resentment, reaction, and upheaval. Destenism rejects utopian promises and final historical outcomes, emphasizing instead that history is cyclical: systems generate exclusion, exclusion produces reaction, and reaction reshapes power, often creating new forms of exclusion in turn. Loyalty, rather than identity or ideology, is identified as the binding force capable of transforming fragmented exclusion into collective power, while merit is treated as the only sustainable basis for legitimacy and advancement.

Building upon this foundation, Destenism articulates the concept of the New State, a governance paradigm grounded in realism, cohesion, and structural continuity. The New State is not defined by a fixed institutional form, but by principles that prioritize order as the precondition of freedom, assimilation over fragmentation, and competence over entitlement. Citizenship is conceived as inseparable from belonging to the nation, with integration understood as a process of participation, contribution, and adoption of a shared language and civic culture. Ethno-nationalism is explicitly rejected as divisive and destabilizing, yet the notion of a culturally neutral state is equally dismissed as unsustainable. Instead, Destenism advances a civic monoculture in the public sphere, within which private diversity may exist, ensuring unity without racial doctrine and inclusion without relativism.

Politically, Destenism is expressed through the model of totalitarian democracy, a theoretical configuration in which democratic forms—elections, constitutions, rights, and representative institutions—coexist with concentrated authority and bounded pluralism. Democracy is defined not as unlimited individual autonomy, but as majority rule exercised through stable institutions, with legitimacy continuously renewed through elections. Opposition and political pluralism are permitted in principle, but conditioned on loyalty to the constitutional order, national unity, and institutional stability. Power is understood as real rather than symbolic, centralized for effectiveness but constrained by legality, accountability, and results. Leadership is conceived as first among equals, rejecting both cults of personality and hereditary authority, while affirming the necessity of strong direction during periods of transformation.

Destenism places particular emphasis on meritocracy as both a moral and structural imperative. Nepotism, corruption, and clientelism are treated not as incidental flaws, but as systemic threats that inevitably reproduce exclusion and domination. Advancement in administration, justice, education, and economic life must therefore be based on competence, discipline, and proven value. This principle extends to the role of the military, which is regarded as an essential instrument of sovereignty and deterrence, integrated into society as a school of responsibility and cohesion, yet firmly subordinate to civilian authority. Military strength is framed as a condition for peace and independence, not as a vehicle for conquest or political rule.

Economically, Destenism advocates a state-led mixed economy that rejects both collectivist central planning and neoliberal globalism. Private enterprise, competition, and innovation are encouraged, but within a framework where the state retains decisive control over strategic sectors such as energy, infrastructure, telecommunications, resources, and food security. Markets are treated as instruments serving national development rather than autonomous forces dictating policy. A robust welfare system is upheld not as charity, but as a pillar of justice, cohesion, and productivity, with universal access to healthcare, education, housing, and essential services understood as civic guarantees. Progressive taxation and compulsory reinvestment by elites are justified not through moral guilt, but through patriotic obligation, while tax evasion and corruption are framed as direct assaults on national sovereignty.

In foreign affairs, Destenism is guided by a realist understanding of power relations and a clash of civilizations perspective, which views global politics as enduring competition between value systems rather than a linear movement toward convergence or universal norms. It rejects imperialism in all its forms—military, economic, and ideological—and denounces what it characterizes as liberal or moral imperialism masked in the language of rights and progress. Destenism promotes strict non-alignment, the refusal of foreign military bases, and resistance to dependency on external powers, while supporting a multipolar world order based on equal-footing cooperation, technological exchange, and sovereign development.

Underlying all dimensions of Destenism is the principle of adaptive sovereignty, which holds that survival and continuity depend on constant adaptation to reality. Adaptation is treated as a foundational law of existence, preceding morality or ideology, while idealism and realism are integrated instrumentally rather than absolutized. Systems that fail to adapt are not morally condemned but structurally replaced or erased. For this reason, Destenism defines itself as a living doctrine, committed to permanent renewal and institutional correction rather than historical finality. In its own self-conception, Destenism is neither utopian nor static, but a power-conscious framework that seeks to unite the Forgotten into a disciplined national force, preserve sovereignty through competence and cohesion, and enable the state to endure, reform, and shape its own destiny in an uncertain and competitive world.

Factions

Since Revolutionaries is fundamentally a mass movement rather than a traditional political party, its internal structure reflects a broad ideological spectrum unified by the ideas and values of Destenism. The lines between factions are fluid, not rigid; members may sympathize with different tendencies depending on context, policy, or personal experience. It's from Destiny Omagu’s writings, speeches, and wartime leadership that the four dominant factions of the Revolutionaries emerged—each emphasizing different aspects of it associated with their ideologies, yet all claiming legitimate descent from it. All factions—whether liberal, conservative, realist, or ideological—are part of the same party, and all members participate equally in the Revolutionaries’s democratic and organizational life.

The liberal faction is the most left-leaning, focusing heavily on social justice, inclusion, anti-discrimination, and political freedom. They emphasize the original revolutionary call to uplift the Forgotten, defend minority rights, strengthen the welfare state, and ensure economic freedoms for workers, immigrants, and marginalized communities. While loyal to the state and party discipline, liberals often push for reforms to make institutions more transparent, responsive, and participatory. Their support base includes immigrants, low-income workers, social workers, who see Revolutionaries as their protector and path to dignity.

The conservative faction leans right, drawing inspiration from Destiny Omagu’s emphasis on discipline, order, and the moral authority of the nation. They see Destenia not just as a political project, but as a sacred cultural mission—to protect traditional values from what they call woke degeneracy, cultural decay, and foreign ideological intrusion. For conservatives, the Revolution must be defended at all costs, especially against the internal rot of moral weakness. They champion patriotism, militarism, religious freedom, and national sovereignty, believing these form the backbone of a united and proud Destenian culture. Their base includes Christians, active-duty and retired military, nationalists, who value cultural rootedness and civic order.

The realist faction occupies a more pragmatic center, not driven by ideology, but by loyalty, ambition, and institutional trust. They may lean liberal or conservative on different issues, but their main concern is stability, efficiency, and access to the mechanisms of state power. Many realists enter Revolutionaries not out of revolutionary zeal, but because it is the engine of governance, influence, and national transformation. They are often the most loyal to the party as an institution, precisely because their own futures depend on it. Without Revolutionaries, their bureaucratic, business, or political ambitions would wither. Their support base includes businessmen, high-level bureaucrats, local politicians, and even segments of the military who prefer order and hierarchy over abstract ideology.

The ideologue faction is the most intensely ideological, yet the least institutionally bound. They believe that the Revolution is not permanent unless it is continuously renewed, and they are the first to denounce internal stagnation, moral compromise, or betrayal of the Forgotten. Ideologues are not loyal to any ideology, but to Destenism only, often interpreting it in purist or radical terms. They are totalitarian in impulse, not out of a lust for control, but from the belief that the mission must be carried through with uncompromising will. They see themselves as keepers of the flame, ready to criticize the party if it strays, and to purge the complacent if necessary. They tend to be free-thinking, often bypassing formal party lines, and using Revolutionaries as a platform for ideological combat rather than bureaucratic careerism. Their base includes youth radicals, elderly veterans of the Revolution, political extremists, and citizens who have rejected cancel culture and prefer confrontation over conformity.

Leaderate

The Leaderate represents the supreme leadership body of the Revolutionaries, composed of the most powerful and influential figures within the movement, led by the Leader. It serves as the core strategic nucleus of the organization, where key decisions are shaped, political direction is refined, and internal unity is maintained. Unlike other organizations structures, the composition of the Leaderate is determined by influence, and strategic importance, rather than by specific titles or positions within the movement hierarchy. The Leaderate is not an executive committee, nor does it follow procedural voting rules or bureaucratic structures. At the center of the Leaderate is the Leader, the Deputy Leader, the chairs of all the affiliated organizations and the Chairman of the Inspectorate. Other key individuals deemed essential to the movement's strategic direction are included as well, —appointments are based not solely on institutional role, but on influence, proven leadership, and personal trust.

Inspectorate

The Inspectorate functions as the oversight organ of the Revolutionaries, entrusted with preserving organizational integrity, enforcing internal accountability, and upholding the Statue of each organizations. As the guardian of discipline, it ensures that all levels of the organization—from local branches to the Leaderate itself—adhere to the organizations’s statutes, principles, and operational norms.

It composed by an representative of each organizations, and add a undefine members, both elected by the Leaderate. At its helm is the Chairman of the Inspectorate appointed by the Leader, a role of considerable authority and independence, appointed directly by the members of the Inspectorate. The Inspectorate’s functions include:

  • Reviewing the decisions made by the Leaderate, providing a check against procedural overreach or deviation from the organization's ideological framework.
  • Resolving internal conflicts and disputes, whether between members, branches, or the organizations. These interventions are based on impartial investigation, structured mediation, and restorative disciplinary measures.
  • Investigating allegations of misconduct, including corruption, abuse of power, or violations of movement ethics. Upon conclusion of investigations, the Inspectorate may issue expulsion, recommend reforms, or refer matters to the appropriate legal authorities if needed.
  • the protector of members’ rights, offering a formal mechanism through which grievances can be addressed. Members have the right to petition the Inspectorate when they believe that due process has been violated or arbitrary decisions have been made against them.

Party

Secretariat

The Secretariat serves as the administrative and operational core of the party, tasked with managing the daily affairs of the party and ensuring the implementation of policies and directives established by the Secretary General and the Leaderate. It functions as the engine of execution, translating the party’s strategic vision into coherent programs, internal coordination, and nationwide mobilization.

At the head of the Secretariat is the General Secretary, a central figure appointed directly by the Leader, responsible for overseeing the entire structure. The General Secretary ensures that the party’s agenda, organizational initiatives, and messaging are consistently and efficiently deployed across all levels of the party. This role requires not only administrative skill but also political discipline, serving as the vital link between the Leader's vision and the organization's apparatus.

The Secretariat is composed of a number of specialized departments, each charged with a distinct functional mandate—ranging from communications, logistics, and membership mobilization to public relations, ideological education, election coordination, and organizational security. These departments form the backbone of the party's bureaucratic infrastructure, maintaining order, discipline, and synchronization across the party’s vast national and territorial network.

All department secretaries within the Secretariat are appointed by the Secretary, not elected or independently selected. This centralized appointment system guarantees that the Secretariat’s leadership remains aligned with the party’s strategic orientation and the Leader’s directives. It reinforces ideological cohesion, operational loyalty, and hierarchical accountability.

Territorial organization

At the regional level, the core governing body is the Regional Committee, which oversees the implementation of party strategy across a Region. The Regional Secretary is elected when needed at the Regional Congress. Upon election, the Regional Secretary appoints the members of the Regional Commitee. This leadership is responsible for coordinating all Local Committees within their Region, ensuring alignment with national priorities and the directives of the Leaderate, while also adapting strategy to local realities.

At the local level, the core governing body is the Local Committee, which oversees the implementation of party strategy across the Municipailty. The Local Secretary is elected when needed at the Local Congress. Upon election, the Local Secretary appoints the members of the Local Commitee. This leadership is responsible for coordinating all Local Committees within their Municipality, ensuring alignment with national priorities and the directives of the Leaderate, while also adapting strategy to local realities.

These committee directs the daily functioning of the party, including the mobilization of local members, and the addressing of local concerns and grievances. The structure is intentionally designed to be highly responsive, ideologically disciplined, and deeply embedded in community life. Through this bottom-up engagement, the Revolutionaries ensures that every neighborhood, village, and town becomes a direct participant in the revolutionary process and a living embodiment of movement ideals.

Governance

Within the party, the Revolutionaries functions as both a mass movement and a political party, encompassing diverse factions and a vast membership base of nearly six million. It is built on the principle of strong leadership, embodied by the Leaderate and chaired by the Leader. Unlike many political organizations, this leadership is viewed as essential for maintaining cohesion in both administration and mass mobilization, since the Revolutionaries simultaneously governs the state and commands a nationwide political movement. The Leader is elected directly by the membership—the only internal election within the party—reflecting the Revolutionaries’s belief in grassroots legitimacy. The party does not impose strict ideological discipline or punitive measures against those diverging from its general direction; however, loyalty to the movement is paramount. This balance between centralized leadership and the autonomy of a large, mobilized membership has made the Revolutionaries internally more participatory than many traditional parties, despite its dominance in national politics.

Within the government, the Revolutionaries adheres to a doctrine of collective leadership. Many individuals hold party roles without state offices, and vice versa, to preserve institutional balance. The government is constitutionally supreme, meaning party positions alone do not grant formal state power. The Leaderate includes members with state authority but no party office, others with party authority but no state role, and some with influence in both spheres. While the party acknowledges the existence of legal opposition, in practice it maintains a dominant party system, with Revolutionaries members occupying the overwhelming majority of government posts. This is justified on the grounds that national cohesion and stability require unified leadership, particularly in a totalitarian democratic framework.

Within society, the Revolutionaries exercises influence beyond the machinery of state through a vast network of affiliated organizations—including youth, student, labor, women’s, veterans’, and cultural associations—and through partnerships with key social institutions. Operating independently of formal state structures allows the party to pursue its goals without the procedural constraints of governance. The Revolutionaries has deeply penetrated Destenian civil life, to the extent that nearly every major social figure, economic elite, and influential institution is either a member, a close affiliate, or aligned with its objectives.

Foreign relations

Revolutionaries maintains an unparalleled position within the Destenian political reality, functioning as the dominant political force while coexisting with legally recognized opposition. As the leading party of Destenia, it is embedded in the state apparatus and permeates nearly all levels of governance, while also maintaining a formal separation between party and government institutions. Beyond formal party politics, the Revolutionaries maintains extensive relationships with a network of affiliated mass organizations, including youth movements, trade unions, veterans’ associations, cultural councils, and professional guilds. These entities operate both independently and in coordination with the party, enabling the Revolutionaries to shape public opinion, mobilize communities, and integrate social groups into the national project. Through these channels, the party exerts significant influence over civil society, extending its reach far beyond parliamentary activity.

The Revolutionaries also collaborates with military, intelligence, and law enforcement structures under the framework of the state, ensuring that the state’s security apparatus remains loyal to the constitutional order and the national unity. This alliance between political authority and the security sector reinforces the Revolutionaries’s position as the guardian of national sovereignty and political stability. Although formally committed to pluralism, the Revolutionaries’s dominance over political institutions, social organizations, and strategic sectors has made it the central pillar of Destenia’s political order, ensuring that all major actors, whether cooperative or oppositional, operate within a framework ultimately shaped by the party.

In international relations, the Revolutionaries actively engages with both regional and global political networks. In Edrobe, it collaborates with a range of political parties, movements, and civic organizations that support the vision of a strong Edrobe composed of sovereign nations, resisting any drift toward supranational federalism or erosion of national decision-making. This approach is consistent with the Revolutionaries’s stance within the Edrobean Sovereigntist Forum, where it champions intergovernmental cooperation over political integration, working closely with parties that value national sovereignty and strategic independence.

In the Global South, the Revolutionaries aligns with forces that are anti-imperialist and reject what it views as the hypocritical liberal human rights discourse used as a pretext for interventionism. It strongly advocates for cooperation, multipolarity, and genuine multilateralism, promoting equitable international engagement without ideological coercion. Through this lens, the Revolutionaries actively supports economic self-determination, non-alignment in military blocs, and cultural sovereignty for developing nations.

The peak expression of this orientation is the United Alliance, an international organization uniting sovereignist political parties from multiple continents that are anti-liberal, anti-imperialist, and committed to non-alignment. The United Alliance functions as a forum for exchanging strategies, coordinating global political campaigns, and defending the rights of the Forgotten—marginalized peoples across the world who face systemic exclusion in both developed and developing countries. The Revolutionaries is a founding and leading member, using the platform to strengthen alliances with like-minded movements in Tayanusa, Litheria, and Edrobe, and to counterbalance the influence of liberal democratic coalitions led by powers historically opposed to Destenia’s rise.
