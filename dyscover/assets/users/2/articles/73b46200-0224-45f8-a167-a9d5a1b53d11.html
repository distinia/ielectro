The 2026 WFF World Cup was the fourth edition of the World Football Federation's premier international football tournament. It was held in Valmirica from 12 June to 12 July 2026, with sixteen national teams participating in the final tournament.

The competition was the largest WFF World Cup ever held at the time, both in terms of attendance and international participation. The tournament brought together teams from several different regions of the world, with the final draw deliberately creating mixed continental groups rather than separating nations according to geographical origin.

Valmirica hosted the tournament across eight venues, with matches being played in major cities distributed throughout the federation. The country's extensive transport infrastructure, including its high-speed railway network, allowed supporters and national teams to move between the host cities throughout the month-long competition.

A total of 32 matches were played and 89 goals were scored. The tournament attracted 2,184,760 spectators, making it one of the most attended sporting events in the history of the WFF.

The tournament ended with Comussania winning its first WFF World Cup, defeating Azaria 2–1 in the final at the Montigon National Stadium. The host nation Valmirica finished in third place, while Metosia finished fourth.

Host selection

Valmirican bid

Valmirica was selected as the host country for the 2026 WFF World Cup after presenting one of the most ambitious hosting proposals in the federation's history.

The Valmirican bid focused heavily on the country's transport infrastructure, stadium capacity, economic strength and geographical diversity. Rather than concentrating the tournament in a small metropolitan area, the organizing committee proposed a nationwide tournament involving eight major host cities.

The proposal received particular attention because of Valmirica's extensive railway system. The country possesses one of the most developed transportation networks in the world, with high-speed rail connecting many of its principal urban centers.

The bid also emphasized Valmirica's experience in hosting major sporting competitions and international conferences. The federal government committed substantial resources to stadium modernization, public transportation, security and telecommunications infrastructure.

The WFF Congress subsequently awarded the tournament to Valmirica. The decision was widely described as a major achievement for the country and an opportunity to present Valmirican culture, technology and infrastructure to the international Union.

Venues

Stadiums

Eight stadiums were selected for the tournament.

The venues were distributed between the country's major population centers and strategically important regions. Montigon National Stadium was selected to host the opening match and final because of its size and symbolic importance as the principal national stadium.

Infrastructure

Valmirica's existing infrastructure greatly reduced the need for temporary construction.

The country's federal authorities coordinated with the WFF to establish dedicated transport corridors for participating teams. High-speed railway services were expanded during the tournament, while airports in the principal host cities operated additional international and domestic services.

Public transport systems in the host cities also operated extended schedules on match days.

The organizing committee established dedicated WFF transport zones, allowing accredited players, officials and staff to move between airports, hotels, training grounds and stadiums under controlled security arrangements.

Draw

Group draw

The final draw was held in Montigon before the beginning of the tournament.

The WFF divided the sixteen teams into four seeding pots according to their international ranking and previous competitive performances. Valmirica was automatically placed in Group A as the host nation.

The draw was designed to create mixed groups containing teams from different continents and footballing regions. The WFF considered geographical diversity one of the defining characteristics of the tournament.

Group A consisted of Valmirica, Azaria, Jarnovia and Fesia.

The host nation began the tournament strongly and remained unbeaten throughout the group stage. Valmirica's attacking football and home support made it one of the tournament's early favorites.

Azaria recovered from an opening defeat and secured second place after a decisive victory in its final group match.

Knockout stage

Quarter-finals

The quarter-finals marked the beginning of the knockout stage.

The eight qualified teams were paired according to their positions in the group standings. Every match was played as a single elimination fixture, with extra time and penalties available if necessary.

MatchResult

Valmirica defeated Destenia 1–0 in a tightly contested match in which the hosts controlled possession for long periods.

Comussania defeated Verdania by the same score, with a late goal deciding a match in which both defenses performed strongly.

Metosia defeated Sifalam 1–0, while Azaria produced the most open quarter-final by defeating Lamberia 2–1.

Semi-finals

The first semi-final was contested between Comussania and Valmirica.

The match attracted one of the largest crowds of the tournament. Valmirica entered the match as the host nation and had significant support from the home crowd, but Comussania's defensive organization prevented the hosts from creating clear opportunities.

Comussania eventually won 2–0, qualifying for the final.

The second semi-final between Azaria and Metosia was more closely contested. Both teams created opportunities, but Azaria eventually scored the only goal of the match and secured a place in the final.

Third-place match

The third-place match was contested by the host nation Valmirica and Metosia.

Valmirica entered the match determined to finish the tournament on the podium after losing to Comussania in the semi-final.

The hosts scored the decisive goal during the second half and maintained their advantage until the final whistle.

Valmirica therefore finished third, while Metosia ended the tournament in fourth place.

Final

The final was played on 12 July 2026 at the Montigon National Stadium.

The match between Comussania and Azaria was one of the most anticipated fixtures in WFF history. Comussania entered the final unbeaten, while Azaria had reached the championship match after eliminating Metosia in the semi-final.

Comussania opened the scoring during the first half. Azaria responded after the interval and equalized, creating a tense final period in which both teams searched for the winning goal.

With the match approaching its final stages, Aleksandar Veyrin scored the decisive goal for Comussania.

The final ended 2–1, making Comussania the 2026 WFF World Cup champion.

Statistics

Goals

A total of 89 goals were scored across the 32 matches, producing an average of approximately 2.78 goals per match.

Comussania was among the highest-scoring teams in the tournament and recorded nine goals during the group stage alone.

The tournament's leading scorer was Aleksandar Veyrin, who scored seven goals for Comussania.

Attendance

The tournament recorded a total attendance of 2,184,760 spectators.

The average attendance was approximately 68,274 spectators per match, with the largest crowds recorded during the semi-finals and final.

The high attendance was attributed to the combination of strong domestic interest, international supporters and Valmirica's extensive transportation network.

Organization

World Football Federation

The World Football Federation was responsible for the organization and administration of the tournament.

The federation coordinated the competition regulations, refereeing, disciplinary procedures, broadcasting standards, commercial rights and international accreditation.

The WFF also established an independent tournament organizing committee in Valmirica to coordinate local operations.

Security

Security was coordinated between the Valmirican federal authorities, state governments and the WFF.

Security measures were increased around stadiums, railway stations, airports and official fan zones.

Accredited supporters and tournament staff were required to pass through controlled security zones before entering stadiums.

Technology

The tournament used several modern football technologies.

Video assistant refereeing, goal-line technology and semi-automated offside technology were available throughout the competition.

Stadiums were also equipped with high-capacity telecommunications systems designed to support international broadcasting, mobile connectivity and real-time tournament statistics.

Culture

Opening ceremony

The opening ceremony was held at the Montigon National Stadium before the first match.

The ceremony presented Valmirica's cultural and geographical diversity through music, choreography and large-scale visual projections.

Representations of the country's fifteen states were incorporated into the performance, with particular attention given to the country's forests, rivers, plains and mountainous regions.

The central theme of the ceremony was international unity through football.

Fan festivals

Official fan festivals were organized in all eight host cities.

The largest fan zone was established in Montigon, where supporters could watch matches on large screens, attend concerts and purchase official merchandise.

The festivals remained open throughout the tournament and became particularly crowded during the knockout stage.

Cultural program

The organizing committee established a cultural program alongside the football competition.

Museums, public institutions and cultural organizations in the host cities organized exhibitions concerning Valmirican history, Edrobean culture and the development of international football.

Foreign supporters were also encouraged to participate in cultural events outside match days.

Broadcasting

Television

The WFF distributed broadcasting rights to national and international broadcasters.

All 32 matches were broadcast live, with coverage including pre-match analysis, live commentary, interviews and post-match analysis.

The final attracted the largest television audience of the tournament.

Digital media

Digital platforms were used extensively throughout the tournament.

The WFF provided live statistics, match reports, player information and official highlights through its digital services.

Social media activity reached its highest levels during the semi-finals and final, particularly following Comussania's victory.

Legacy

The 2026 WFF World Cup was considered a major success for both the WFF and Valmirica.

The tournament demonstrated that a sixteen-team international format could produce a compact competition while maintaining geographical diversity and high attendance.

Valmirica benefited from long-term investments in stadiums, railway infrastructure, telecommunications and tourism.

The tournament also strengthened the international reputation of the WFF, particularly after the successful organization of the final and the high attendance figures.

Comussania's victory established the country as a new major force in international football, while Azaria's run to the final marked one of the strongest performances in its WFF history.

The tournament also created several new international football rivalries that were expected to influence future WFF competitions.
