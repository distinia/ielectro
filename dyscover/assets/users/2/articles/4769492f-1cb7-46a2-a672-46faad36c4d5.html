Alveria, officially the Republic of Alveria, is a unitary semi-presidential republic in northwestern Edrobe, bordered by Metosia, Boravia, and Laocitia, and flanked to the east and north by the Northern Sea. With a population of approximately 16.47 million and an area of 128,474 km², it is a highly centralized yet politically pluralistic state, governed through a unicameral Parliament, a President serving as head of state, and a Prime Minister leading the government. The capital, Veridia, serves as the administrative, political, and cultural center.

Alveria is ethnically homogeneous, with 90% of the population being Alverian, alongside Metosian and Laocitian minorities. The official language is Alverian, used exclusively in government, education, and media. Catholicism, practiced by 95% of the population, dominates the religious landscape, playing a prominent cultural role within a constitutionally secular framework.

The economy, valued at 400 billion DTR, is driven by a robust agricultural base, a competitive industrial sector—notably in automotive and light machinery—and an expanding tertiary sector centered in urban regions. Alveria is fully integrated into the Edrobean Community and supports multilateral cooperation, with active participation in the United Nations, World Trade Network, and Global Health Organization.

The Alverian Armed Forces, comprising the Army, Air Force, and Navy, are professional, defensive in doctrine, and integrated with regional security structures. Law enforcement is centralized under the National Police Directorate, with specialized agencies handling financial crimes, cyber security, and border control.

Administratively, Alveria is divided into eight regions, with municipalities providing local governance under national oversight. Demographically, the country is aging but stable, with moderate urbanization, a high median age, and increasing reliance on skilled migration to sustain labor and innovation. The state remains focused on infrastructure modernization, demographic resilience, and preserving national unity through civic integration and institutional continuity.

Etymology

The etymology of the name Alveria traces its origins to the ancient Alverian word *“Alver,”* meaning "virtue" or "noble conduct," a concept deeply embedded in the cultural and philosophical traditions of the early highland tribes that once inhabited the region. This term, associated with honor, moral clarity, and civic duty, formed the linguistic and ideological foundation of the ancient Alveric codes—proto-legal customs that guided communal life prior to formal statehood. The suffix *-ia*, added during the unification period of the 12th century, follows a common Edrobean linguistic convention denoting territory or land, thus forming “Alveria,” literally understood as “land of virtue.”

Historical chronicles dating back to the First Kingdom of Veridia describe the term *Alver* not only as a word but as a social ethos, a framework through which individuals were judged and communities organized. Over centuries, as tribal alliances coalesced into duchies and later centralized into a sovereign entity, the notion of “Alveria” was adopted as a unifying name. It symbolized a collective aspiration to establish a polity rooted in moral uprightness, justice, and unity, ideals that would later become formalized in the national motto: *Unity in Virtue.*

The enduring cultural resonance of the name is evident in its continued use across various domains of Alverian life—from constitutional language and educational curricula to literature, civic institutions, and ceremonial rituals. Unlike many states whose names were shaped by geography or military conquest, Alveria’s name is a direct invocation of principle, designed to bind its citizens not by blood or territory alone, but by a shared ethical identity embedded in both language and law.

Geography

Alveria occupies a geographically diverse and strategically situated portion of northwestern Edrobe, where it commands both maritime access and inland connectivity. The country spans 128,474 square kilometers, bordered by the Northern Sea to the east and north, while land boundaries are shared with Metosia and Boravia to the west, and Laocitia to the south. This configuration grants Alveria a dual character: both a coastal nation with a rich seafaring tradition and an inland power whose mountainous and agrarian heartlands have long shaped its internal development and external posture.

The most prominent coastal features are the Draškovac Peninsula and the Plamenjak Peninsula, each projecting into the Northern Sea and contributing distinct ecological and cultural profiles. The Draškovac Peninsula, located in the southeastern quadrant of the coast, is defined by rocky inlets, steep cliffs, and small natural harbors historically used by merchant and naval fleets. Its terrain is rugged and lightly forested, dotted with fishing villages and fortified towns that trace their origins to the medieval maritime leagues. Further north, the Plamenjak Peninsula presents a more temperate environment, characterized by fertile coastal plains, moderate elevation, and an abundance of wind-swept meadows ideal for small-scale farming and wind energy development. The peninsula serves as a traditional stronghold of Alverian agriculture and continues to support a mix of dairy, grain, and root crop production.

The Velikograd Mountains, forming a continuous barrier along the southern frontier with Laocitia, are the country’s most imposing topographical feature. This range consists of sharp peaks, deep alpine valleys, and glacial formations, rising sharply from the lowland transition zone and extending across several prefectures. The mountains serve both as a natural defense line and a climatic barrier, shaping local weather patterns and providing critical watersheds. Though not heavily populated, the Velikograd region contains rich deposits of iron ore and rare minerals, making it a resource base for the national mining and metallurgy industries. The range is also a center of ecotourism, winter sports, and mountain monasticism, with isolated monasteries preserved as part of Alveria’s cultural heritage.

To the north, the Prostranac Plains represent the country’s agricultural core. These wide, gently undulating lowlands benefit from a moderate continental climate, making them ideal for wheat, barley, and vegetable cultivation. The plains function as a logistical axis between the capital Veridia, located at the edge of the uplands, and the coastal export terminals. They are also home to some of Alveria’s most populous municipalities, supported by an extensive network of railways and rural highways that facilitate internal trade and labor mobility.

Water resources are limited, and the country is notably deficient in major river systems. The only significant river is the Dubravica, which runs along the western border with Metosia and Boravia. While not navigable throughout its length, the Dubravica has historically provided a natural defensive boundary and a secondary trade route, particularly for agricultural goods and small-scale industrial exports. A series of artificial reservoirs and aqueduct systems have been developed in response to seasonal water scarcity, especially in the south and interior regions.

Alveria’s geographic diversity has cultivated a regionalized cultural and economic identity, with distinct coastal, mountainous, and inland traditions shaping the national character. This environment has fostered adaptability and regional pride, particularly in the face of geographic constraints and historical isolation. Rather than rely on abundance, the country’s development has been defined by efficient land use, environmental stewardship, and strategic positioning, enabling Alveria to punch above its weight within the Edrobean geopolitical landscape.

Politics

Government

Alveria functions as a unitary semi-presidential republic, combining elements of both presidential and parliamentary systems within a framework designed to ensure institutional balance, legal continuity, and democratic accountability. The Constitution, outlines the distribution of power between the executive, legislative, and judicial branches, all operating under the principles of the rule of law and popular sovereignty. While power is centralized at the national level, the state structure provides avenues for local governance and participatory representation.

At the apex of the executive branch is the President of the Republic, who serves as head of state and symbol of national unity. Elected by popular vote for a five-year term, the President holds constitutionally defined powers, including the authority to appoint the Prime Minister, promulgate laws, veto legislation (subject to parliamentary override), represent Alveria in international affairs, and serve as Commander-in-Chief of the Armed Forces. Although the role is largely ceremonial in daily governance, the President retains significant prerogatives in times of national crisis, foreign policy decisions, and the formation of government coalitions.

The Prime Minister, appointed by the President with the approval of Parliament, acts as the head of government and exercises the full range of executive responsibilities. These include managing the day-to-day affairs of the state, coordinating the activities of ministries, proposing legislation, and drafting the national budget. The Prime Minister presides over the Council of Ministers, a cabinet composed of ministers and state secretaries who oversee portfolios such as defense, finance, health, agriculture, infrastructure, and education. The Council operates on a collegial basis, though the Prime Minister holds final authority in administrative decisions and crisis response.

The legislative branch is embodied in the Parliament of Alveria, a unicameral assembly composed of members elected through a mixed proportional and majoritarian voting system. Parliamentary elections are held every four years, with political parties and independent candidates competing in both constituency-based and party-list formats. The Speaker of Parliament, currently the third-ranking official in the state hierarchy, presides over sessions, oversees legislative procedures, and ensures the neutrality and integrity of parliamentary operations. Parliament is responsible for enacting laws, approving the national budget, ratifying international treaties, and exercising oversight of the executive through motions of inquiry and no-confidence votes.

The judiciary functions independently of the political branches, with its authority rooted in the constitution and codified civil law. The apex judicial body is the Supreme Constitutional Court, tasked with reviewing legislation for compatibility with the constitution, adjudicating electoral disputes, and interpreting fundamental rights. Below it are appellate courts, regional courts, and magistrates’ tribunals that handle civil, criminal, and administrative cases. Judges are appointed through a merit-based system managed by the National Judicial Council, an autonomous body composed of legal professionals and academics.

Alveria also maintains a framework for local governance, with municipalities and regions governed by elected councils and mayors, though their powers are limited in scope compared to the central government. Local authorities manage urban planning, primary education, waste collection, and certain cultural and social programs, all within budgets allocated by national ministries.

Political pluralism is a constitutionally protected principle, and Alveria operates a multi-party democratic system, with regular competitive elections, legal protections for civil liberties, and a media environment subject to press freedom regulations. The Electoral Commission of Alveria, an independent oversight body, ensures the transparency and legality of elections and referenda.

Political parties

Alveria operates within a multi-party democratic system, characterized by a competitive electoral environment, ideological diversity, and a culture of coalition-building. The country’s political scene is structured around several dominant parties and a range of minor or regional groups, all functioning within the bounds of a constitutional framework that guarantees political pluralism, free elections, and equal access to public office. While no party has maintained prolonged single-party dominance, certain formations have consistently shaped government policy and national discourse since the adoption of the 2020 constitution.

The leading force in contemporary Alverian politics is the Civic Democratic Union, a center-right party grounded in liberal conservatism, pro-European values, and economic pragmatism. The CDU draws strong support from the urban middle class, business sectors, and rural conservatives, advocating for market liberalization, fiscal discipline, and national unity under democratic principles. It has led multiple coalition governments and remains influential in both Parliament and local administrations.

On the center-left stands the Alverian Social Democratic Alliance, traditionally the main rival of the CDU. The ASDA promotes social justice, labor rights, and state intervention in health, education, and infrastructure, aligning itself with moderate progressivism and cooperative internationalism. Its electoral base includes industrial workers, public sector employees, students, and socially liberal professionals. Although its influence has fluctuated in recent years, the ASDA remains a major parliamentary presence and a frequent coalition partner.

The National Renewal Front occupies the populist-nationalist space, with a platform focused on sovereignty, anti-immigration policy, cultural preservation, and skepticism toward supranational governance, particularly regarding the Edrobean Community. Its rhetoric is sharply critical of elite institutions and often appeals to voters in rural and border regions, especially those concerned with identity politics and perceived economic neglect.

More centrist and technocratic in orientation is the Progress for Alveria party, a reformist group emphasizing digital innovation, education reform, and environmental modernization. Though smaller in parliamentary size, PfA has played a pivotal role in multiple coalition governments and enjoys support from urban youth, tech professionals, and segments of the academic community.

The Green People's Party, representing the environmentalist and eco-socialist current, advocates for climate justice, renewable energy transition, and anti-corruption reforms. While it maintains a modest electoral presence, particularly in university towns and environmentally conscious prefectures, the GPP often influences public discourse and legislative priorities related to sustainability and biodiversity.

All registered political parties are subject to oversight by the Electoral Commission of Alveria, which enforces transparency in campaign financing, monitors media access, and ensures compliance with electoral law. Party membership remains open and voluntary, with internal governance structures required to follow democratic norms.

Alveria’s political environment is generally stable, though coalition governments are the norm due to the proportional nature of parliamentary representation. While ideological divides persist, most parties operate within the constitutional consensus, respecting the rule of law, separation of powers, and democratic procedure. Political debates are vigorous but largely institutionalized, reflecting a maturing democracy with active civic participation.

Administrative subdivisions

Alveria is administratively divided into eight regions (regije), each serving as a principal subnational unit of governance under the centralized authority of the national government. These regions function as territorial-administrative entities rather than federated units, consistent with the country’s unitary structure, and their authority is delegated by statute rather than guaranteed by the constitution. The regions are: Veridia, Draškovac, Plamenjak, Prostranac, Velikograd, Istrovia, Boravska, and Metostan. Each region varies in geography, population, and economic focus, reflecting the country’s spatial diversity and historical settlement patterns.

At the head of each region is a Regional Governor (Regijski guverner), appointed by the central government upon confirmation by the regional assembly. The Governor functions as the chief representative of the national administration at the regional level, tasked with overseeing the implementation of national legislation, coordinating disaster response, managing regional development funds, and ensuring compliance with state regulations. The Governor is supported by a Regional Administrative Council, composed of appointed officials from various ministerial departments.

Each region also hosts a Regional Assembly (Skupština Regije), composed of members elected by popular vote every four years. These assemblies have the authority to deliberate and adopt secondary legislation within the limits established by national law. They manage regional budgets, plan infrastructure projects, and oversee public services such as transportation, waste management, and vocational training. However, all regional decisions remain subordinate to national policy and can be overridden by the central authorities in matters of national interest.

Below the regional level, Alveria is further subdivided into municipalities (općine) and urban districts (gradski kotari), the latter applicable in larger cities such as Veridia and Plamenjak. Municipalities are the basic unit of local self-government and are governed by elected municipal councils and mayors, who exercise authority over local public services, land use planning, cultural affairs, and primary education. While municipalities enjoy a degree of autonomy, their fiscal capacity is limited, with the majority of their funding derived from centrally allocated transfers and regulated local taxation.

The capital city, Veridia, holds a special administrative status as a metropolitan district directly subordinate to the national government. It hosts all major federal institutions, including the Parliament, Supreme Court, Presidential Office, and national ministries. As such, it is administered by a Metropolitan Council under the supervision of a nationally appointed Prefect of the Capital, separate from the Governor of the Veridia Region. Veridia also contains multiple urban boroughs with elected councils that manage neighborhood-level services.

Administrative divisions are overseen nationally by the Ministry of Regional Development and Local Administration, which coordinates intergovernmental relations, allocates development funding, and monitors compliance with national standards across regions and municipalities. While Alveria remains a highly centralized state, administrative subdivisions serve to enhance service delivery, facilitate regional planning, and promote balanced socio-economic development across its diverse territorial landscape.

Foreign relations

Alveria pursues a multilateralist foreign policy, positioning itself as a committed advocate of international cooperation, institutional integration, and peaceful diplomacy within a rapidly evolving global order. Anchored in the strategic goal of safeguarding national sovereignty while expanding economic and security guarantees, the country emphasizes regional solidarity, rule-based international relations, and the promotion of democratic norms. Alveria views active engagement in international organizations not merely as a diplomatic tool, but as a core element of its national strategy, reflecting both historical pragmatism and ideological orientation.

A central pillar of Alverian foreign policy is its deep alignment with the Edrobean Community, of which it is a full member. The government views the EC as the principal framework for promoting economic integration, collective security, energy cooperation, and shared infrastructure development among the nations of Edrobe. Alveria has been a consistent proponent of expanding the EC's mandate, particularly in areas of climate governance, digital standardization, and joint border management, and regularly contributes personnel and funding to Edrobean institutions. It also participates in joint Edrobean parliamentary forums, customs harmonization bodies, and regional research initiatives.

Beyond the EC, Alveria is an active member of the United Nations. Although not a founding member, Alveria has pursued full participation with enthusiasm, seeing the UN as a counterweight to unilateralism and great-power rivalry. It contributes to humanitarian aid programs, and has signed all major UN charters relating to arms control, civil liberties, and environmental sustainability. Through its role in the Global Reserve Fund, World Trade Network, and International Energy Agency, Alveria promotes open markets and sustainable development, while carefully safeguarding national regulatory autonomy in sensitive sectors.

Relations with neighboring states are guided by a doctrine of proximity diplomacy—prioritizing stability and shared interests in the region. Alveria enjoys close bilateral ties with Metosia, underpinned by a long-standing friendship treaty, a cross-border railway corridor, and defense coordination agreements. With Boravia, relations are pragmatic and trade-focused, often channeled through trilateral Edrobean forums. Ties with Laocitia remain cautious but functional, reflecting historical frictions over border demarcation and minority rights; however, both countries have agreed to maintain dialogue through EC mediation mechanisms and military deconfliction protocols.

Alveria’s strategic calculus is informed by the need to balance national interest with international law, particularly in defense and digital sovereignty. The country maintains a non-aggressive military doctrine, and adheres to nuclear non-proliferation and cyber security frameworks. It does not host foreign military bases, but allows participation in joint intelligence coordination centers and logistical hubs under Edrobean operational control.

Diplomatically, Alveria maintains embassies in all major global capitals and consulates across the Edrobean continent. The Ministry of Foreign Affairs, headquartered in Veridia, oversees diplomatic missions, treaty negotiations, international legal arbitration, and multilateral engagements. Alverian diplomats are known for their legalistic, negotiation-oriented approach, frequently acting as mediators in regional disputes or consensus-builders within complex multilateral bodies.

Military

The military of Alveria is formally designated as the Alverian Armed Forces, a professional and constitutionally regulated institution tasked with the defense of national sovereignty, territorial integrity, and the fulfillment of international obligations under collective security frameworks. Operating under civilian command, the military is subordinated to the executive authority of the President, who serves as Commander-in-Chief, while day-to-day operational leadership is exercised by the Chief of the General Staff, appointed by the President on the advice of the Minister of Defense. The Armed Forces are composed of three principal branches: the Alverian Army, Air Force, and Navy, each structured to perform distinct roles within a unified defense doctrine centered on deterrence, territorial defense, and multilateral interoperability.

The Alverian Army forms the backbone of the military and is responsible for land-based operations, internal defense, border security, and civil support during emergencies. It is organized into several regional brigades, including mechanized infantry units, armored divisions, combat engineers, and logistical battalions. The Army places significant emphasis on mobility, tactical readiness, and rapid deployment, operating within a framework that integrates conventional forces with specialized mountain warfare and reconnaissance units. The proximity to the Velikograd Mountains has given rise to a long tradition of alpine training and high-altitude operations, making the Army particularly adept at defending difficult terrain.

The Alverian Air Force is a compact but technologically advanced branch, tasked with maintaining airspace sovereignty, conducting aerial reconnaissance, and supporting both offensive and defensive joint operations. Its fleet includes a combination of multirole fighter aircraft, tactical transport planes, unmanned aerial vehicles, and early warning systems. The main airbases are strategically located near Veridia and in the northern plains, ensuring quick response times across the national territory. The Air Force also participates in Edrobean Community air patrols and contributes to UN joint exercises, reflecting its commitment to collective defense frameworks.

Though Alveria lacks a traditional blue-water navy, it maintains a specialized Navy, primarily focused on coastal defense, maritime surveillance, and port security along the Northern Sea. This branch operates a fleet of patrol boats, corvettes, and fast-response vessels equipped for anti-smuggling, search-and-rescue, and maritime law enforcement missions. The Navy is also tasked with protecting offshore infrastructure, particularly energy terminals and communication lines, and coordinates closely with civilian maritime authorities during peacetime operations. Port facilities at Draškovac and Plamenjak serve as its logistical and command hubs.

Alveria does not possess nuclear weapons and adheres strictly to international arms control agreements. Defense spending remains moderate at approximately 1.8% of GDP, reflecting a doctrine centered on smart defense, digital modernization, and regional coordination rather than force projection or global reach.

Military service is entirely voluntary, with conscription suspended indefinitely. Recruitment is conducted through merit-based entrance into military academies and technical schools, with a strong emphasis on professional training, ethical conduct, and technological proficiency. The Armed Forces maintain joint training programs with Edrobean partners, and Alverian officers frequently attend multinational command courses abroad.

Civil-military relations are governed by a robust legal framework that prohibits the armed forces from engaging in partisan politics or domestic law enforcement, except in clearly defined emergency situations. The Parliamentary Defense Committee exercises oversight over military expenditures, strategic planning, and arms procurement, while the Court of Military Justice adjudicates all disciplinary and legal matters within the force.

Law enforcement

Law enforcement in Alveria is organized under a nationally centralized but functionally diversified structure, operating under the oversight of the Ministry of Interior. The country’s policing framework is designed to ensure public order, enforce criminal and civil law, protect individual rights, and uphold constitutional authority across all regions of the state. Guided by the principles of professionalism, legality, and non-politicization, Alverian law enforcement operates with a strong emphasis on civilian oversight, internal accountability, and interagency cooperation.

The primary policing body is the National Police Directorate, which is responsible for general law enforcement duties across the country. The NPD maintains territorial stations in all eight administrative regions and is organized into specialized divisions including criminal investigation, public security, traffic enforcement, anti-narcotics, and juvenile protection. Officers are trained at the Alverian Academy of Policing and Internal Security, where emphasis is placed on constitutional rights, procedural justice, community engagement, and non-lethal intervention tactics. The NPD is led by the National Police Commissioner, appointed by the Minister of Interior and confirmed by Parliament.

In addition to the NPD, Alveria operates a Gendarmerie, a militarized policing force with jurisdiction over rural areas, strategic infrastructure, border zones, and regions affected by civil unrest or natural disaster. The Gendarmerie works in parallel with the Armed Forces during national emergencies but remains a civilian service under the Interior Ministry during peacetime. Units are equipped for riot control, mountain patrol, and infrastructure protection, and frequently participate in joint exercises with regional security agencies under Edrobean coordination mechanisms.

Major urban centers, particularly Veridia, also maintain Municipal Police Departments, which function under local authorities but coordinate with the NPD for information sharing, emergency response, and policy enforcement. Municipal police are responsible for parking regulation, minor infractions, urban traffic control, and local code enforcement, and serve as first responders for non-violent incidents. In Veridia, the capital’s Metropolitan Police includes an intelligence and cybercrime unit tasked with protecting diplomatic missions and critical government installations.

Specialized national services operate under distinct mandates. The Financial Crimes Unit is tasked with investigating corruption, embezzlement, tax evasion, and money laundering, working in tandem with the judiciary and national audit authorities. The Cybercrime Division investigates offenses related to hacking, digital fraud, and data breaches, and maintains close cooperation with the private sector and international partners. Organized crime, human trafficking, and narcotics are handled by the Directorate for Criminal Operations, which has transnational jurisdiction and operates field offices in cooperation with InterEdro and UN security networks.

Border and migration control fall under the Alverian Border Authority, which oversees entry points, visa compliance, refugee screening, and customs enforcement. ABMA officers are deployed at land crossings, ports, and international airports, and are trained in both security protocol and international humanitarian standards.

Oversight and accountability mechanisms are robust. The Internal Affairs Inspection Service conducts investigations into misconduct, abuse of power, or unlawful use of force within law enforcement agencies. The Judicial Police, a specialized arm of the prosecution service, ensures procedural integrity in criminal investigations and monitors compliance with court orders.

Economy

Alveria possesses a developing yet structurally balanced economy, ranked 16th globally with a gross domestic product of approximately 400 billion DTR, and marked by a dynamic interplay between its agricultural, industrial, and emerging service sectors. The country’s economic model is grounded in export-oriented production, strategic public investment, and a high degree of integration into regional trade frameworks, particularly the Edrobean Community. While Alveria does not command the economic scale of its larger neighbors, it has consistently leveraged its geostrategic location, natural resources, and disciplined fiscal policy to maintain macroeconomic stability and attract foreign investment.

The primary sector continues to play a foundational role in national output and employment, particularly in the central plains and northern peninsulas. Agriculture remains highly productive, owing to fertile land, favorable climate, and mechanized farming techniques supported by state subsidies and cooperative associations. Key crops include wheat, maize, potatoes, and sunflowers, along with dairy, poultry, and pork production, which supply both domestic consumption and regional exports. The Prostranac Plains in particular function as Alveria’s agricultural heartland, supplying urban markets and sustaining the country’s agri-industrial value chain.

Alveria is also endowed with iron ore, bauxite, and limestone deposits concentrated along the Velikograd Mountains, which fuel its metallurgy and construction sectors. While not a mineral superpower, the state has invested in environmentally compliant extraction and strategic partnerships with regional smelters and manufacturing plants. Forestry contributes modestly to GDP but plays a greater role in local economies, especially in mountainous and coastal areas, where wood processing and paper production are long-standing industries.

The secondary sector forms the core of Alveria’s export capacity. The country has a well-developed automotive and light machinery industry, centered in the industrial zones of Boravska and Veridia, with both domestic and foreign firms producing compact vehicles, agricultural machinery, and electric components. Alverian car manufacturers are competitive within Edrobe due to cost efficiency, standardized quality, and growing investment in hybrid and electric vehicle technology. The textile and apparel industry also maintains a strong domestic base, exporting garments under both national and foreign labels. Government incentives for industrial automation, green manufacturing, and vocational training have further enhanced productivity and attracted mid-scale foreign direct investment, particularly from Destenian and Metosian firms.

The tertiary sector is growing rapidly, although it lags behind the industrial and agricultural sectors in terms of total GDP contribution. Services are concentrated in urban centers like Veridia, where finance, information technology, tourism, education, and healthcare form the backbone of economic modernization. The financial sector is stable and moderately regulated, dominated by domestic banks with expanding digital platforms and limited foreign penetration. Fintech startups and insurance cooperatives are emerging, supported by favorable regulation and participation in Edrobean financial harmonization programs.

Tourism plays a secondary but expanding role, with increasing arrivals to Alveria’s historic cities, coastal peninsulas, and mountain resorts. The government promotes ecotourism and heritage tourism through targeted marketing campaigns and investment in hospitality infrastructure. However, the sector remains underdeveloped relative to its potential, hampered by regional disparities and logistical bottlenecks.

Alveria’s infrastructure is functional but uneven. Major urban centers are connected by a modern road and rail network, including electrified railway corridors to Metosia and Boravia, which are essential to the country’s export logistics. Ports in Draškovac and Plamenjak handle bulk and container traffic, though expansion is needed to meet rising trade volumes. Energy infrastructure is undergoing transition, with a national plan to reduce dependency on imported fossil fuels by expanding hydropower, wind, and solar capacity, particularly in coastal and highland areas. The state maintains ownership of key utilities, though gradual liberalization is underway in telecommunications and electricity distribution.

The Alverian Dollar is a freely convertible currency, managed by the National Reserve Bank, which maintains inflation within target ranges and ensures financial sector liquidity. Public debt is moderate, and fiscal policy remains conservative, with emphasis on infrastructure development, education, and innovation subsidies. Labor laws support collective bargaining, though the union movement remains fragmented and varies significantly across sectors.

Trade policy is strongly pro-integration. Alveria’s accession to the World Union and membership in the World Trade Network and Edrobean customs zone have provided tariff-free access to regional markets and increased foreign investor confidence. Key trading partners include Destenia, Metosia, and Verdania, with ongoing negotiations to expand digital services and environmental technology exchanges with the Global South. While challenges persist—particularly in terms of regional inequality, brain drain, and limited high-tech export capacity—Alveria’s economy is marked by resilience, expanding diversification, and steady convergence with higher-performing Edrobean economies.

Demography

Alveria’s demographic profile reflects the characteristics of a mature, moderately urbanized society, shaped by post-industrial trends, stable population growth, and a steadily aging population. As of the most recent census, the total population stands at approximately 16.47 million, making Alveria a medium-sized nation by Edrobean standards. The demographic distribution reveals a well-developed social infrastructure, high life expectancy, and relatively low fertility, balanced by gradual urban expansion and moderate net migration.

The urban population accounts for nearly 69% of the total, concentrated in key metropolitan centers such as the capital Veridia, the coastal cities of Plamenjak and Draškovac, and the inland industrial hubs of Boravska and Velikograd. Urbanization has been driven by economic centralization, educational opportunities, and housing modernization, with municipal authorities managing public services, transport, and healthcare through centralized planning frameworks. Nonetheless, a substantial 31% of Alverians still reside in rural areas, particularly in the agricultural zones of Prostranac and the highland valleys of Istrovia, where small towns and farming communities maintain traditional lifestyles supported by state investment in rural development.

Alveria has a median age of 41.2 years, indicative of a slowly aging population typical of developed societies. This trend is the result of consistently low fertility—estimated at 1.74 children per woman—and rising life expectancy, which currently stands at 81.6 years for women and 76.9 years for men. The country’s demographic transition has led to increasing attention on pension reform, elder care infrastructure, and incentives for family formation, including child allowances, subsidized housing, and parental leave policies implemented in recent years to moderate the aging curve.

Population growth remains modest but positive, largely driven by immigration and naturalized residents. Annual net migration has ranged between 15,000 and 25,000 persons, consisting primarily of skilled laborers and students from neighboring countries such as Metosia, Laocitia, Ricene, and select UN partner states. Many of these migrants settle in industrial zones or university cities, contributing to labor market flexibility and the expansion of service-oriented employment. Integration programs, language courses, and civic orientation initiatives are coordinated by the Ministry of Social Affairs and Integration, and immigrants with long-term residence are eligible for naturalization after five years, provided they demonstrate Alverian language proficiency, civic knowledge, and economic independence.

Household structures have evolved in line with European norms. The average household size is 2.4 persons, with a growing share of single-person and dual-income households, especially in urban areas. Traditional multi-generational family arrangements persist in rural regions but are declining steadily. Marriage rates have declined moderately, while divorce and cohabitation have become more socially normalized. National surveys indicate rising demand for flexible family policies, accessible childcare, and generational housing solutions.

The education level of the population is high, with nearly 98% adult literacy and strong enrollment in secondary and tertiary education. Public universities are tuition-free for citizens, and vocational training is well integrated with labor market needs, contributing to a highly skilled workforce. Youth demographics, while shrinking in relative proportion, remain politically and culturally engaged, with high levels of participation in civic organizations and cross-border educational exchange programs, especially within the Edrobean Community.

The gender balance of the population is stable, with women making up slightly over 51% of residents. Female participation in the labor force has steadily increased, supported by anti-discrimination laws, maternity protections, and equal pay regulations. Demographic forecasts predict a gradual rise in the dependency ratio, prompting long-term adjustments in healthcare, taxation, and automation strategy to sustain productivity and social stability.

Alveria’s demographic trajectory suggests continued slow growth, aging, and urban consolidation, accompanied by modest diversification through immigration. The state’s proactive planning and social infrastructure have allowed it to adapt to these shifts while preserving cohesion and maintaining a high standard of living across regions and generations.

Language

The official and overwhelmingly dominant language of Alveria is Alverian, a South Edrobean language that functions as the primary vehicle of national identity, legal authority, and civic cohesion. It is the exclusive language of government, education, public administration, and the judiciary, and is spoken natively by over 96% of the population. The status of Alverian is enshrined in the constitution, which recognizes it as not only a linguistic medium but a fundamental pillar of the cultural and political unity of the Alverian Republic.

Language policy in Alveria is centralized and assertively unilingual. All public education is conducted in Alverian, beginning at the preschool level and continuing through university, with the national curriculum placing heavy emphasis on language proficiency, grammar, literature, and oratory. Government publications, official signage, and legal documents are issued exclusively in Alverian, and proficiency is a prerequisite for naturalization, public employment, and electoral candidacy.

Although Alveria is ethnically and linguistically homogeneous, minority languages are present in limited contexts, particularly among Metosian and Laocitian communities residing near the western and southern borders. These groups maintain their heritage languages within private households, cultural associations, and religious institutions, but they do not possess official status or state support for public use. Educational accommodations for minority languages are permitted only in private institutions, and national legislation prohibits their use in formal state proceedings. Despite this, the government upholds constitutional protections for freedom of linguistic expression, and minority-language media, publications, and events are allowed without interference.

The NLC also oversees the promotion of linguistic heritage, including the publication of standardized dictionaries, linguistic journals, and a national corpus of literature and historical documents. Efforts to preserve dialectal variation, particularly in rural regions of Velikograd and Istrovia, are limited but ongoing, primarily in academic and folkloric contexts. While dialects are generally intelligible with the standard language, public life is dominated by standardized Alverian, and dialect use is increasingly rare among younger generations.

Religion

Religion in Alveria occupies a deeply rooted yet institutionally moderated role in public life. The country is officially secular, with the freedom of religion guaranteed under the constitution, which prohibits the establishment of a state religion while affirming the right of individuals to practice their faith without interference. Nonetheless, Catholicism holds a central place in Alverian society, shaping cultural traditions, national rituals, and moral discourse. Approximately 95% of the population identifies as Christian, with the vast majority adhering to the Catholic Church, which in this world is referred to simply as the *Catholic Church*, without Roman qualifiers.

The historical dominance of Catholicism in Alveria dates back to the early medieval unification period, when monastic orders and episcopal authorities played a formative role in literacy, state formation, and legal development. Over time, the Church became deeply intertwined with the state, influencing education, family law, and social norms. While direct Church control over state functions was abolished in the early 20th century, the cultural legacy of Catholicism remains pervasive, especially in rural communities, national holidays, and ceremonial life.

The Alverian Episcopal Conference, headquartered in Veridia, oversees ecclesiastical affairs within the country. It manages the country's dioceses, coordinates Catholic education, and represents Alveria in global synods. Though the Church no longer receives direct state subsidies, it retains privileges in the areas of religious instruction, healthcare chaplaincy, and heritage preservation. Public funding may be granted for the maintenance of historically significant churches and monasteries, as part of broader cultural conservation initiatives.

Catholic holidays such as Easter, Christmas, and All Saints’ Day are recognized as national public holidays, and Catholic rites—particularly baptism, confirmation, and marriage—are widely practiced across all regions and social classes. While civil marriage is required for legal recognition, many couples continue to hold religious ceremonies in churches, and funeral masses remain the dominant form of commemorative ritual.

The Catholic education system operates alongside the public school network, with private Catholic schools permitted to function under state regulation. These institutions must adhere to the national curriculum but may include religious instruction and pastoral services. In public schools, religious education is optional, offered with parental consent and implemented under guidelines issued by the Ministry of Education in cooperation with the Episcopal Conference.

Despite its cultural prominence, the Church holds no formal political authority and is barred from direct involvement in electoral politics or party activity. The clergy are expected to remain non-partisan, though they often voice positions on social issues such as bioethics, family policy, and refugee care. These interventions, while sometimes controversial, are framed in moral rather than partisan terms, and are generally respected by the public as part of the Church’s traditional social mission.

Religious minorities in Alveria, including small Orthodox Christian, Protestant, Muslim, and Jewish communities, account for approximately 5% of the population. These groups are legally protected and allowed to establish places of worship, publish religious materials, and operate private schools. While there have been occasional public debates regarding minority religious visibility and recognition, overt discrimination is rare, and interfaith dialogue initiatives have been supported by both civil society and segments of the clergy.

The state maintains a clear legal boundary between religious institutions and public governance through the Office for Religious Affairs, a non-partisan agency under the Ministry of Justice, which manages the registration of religious organizations, monitors compliance with public law, and mediates disputes involving religious rights.

Overall, Catholicism in Alveria is not enforced, but it is widely embraced, functioning as both a personal faith and a cultural framework. It remains one of the most influential institutions in Alverian civil society—respected for its moral voice, historical continuity, and role in shaping the national conscience, while operating within a modern secular republic defined by religious tolerance and legal neutrality.

Ethnicities

Alveria is among the most ethnically homogeneous countries in Edrobe, with approximately 90% of the population identifying as ethnic Alverians, a group unified by a shared language, cultural tradition, and historical lineage tracing back to the early Edrobean highland civilizations. This overwhelming demographic majority has been a central factor in shaping the cohesive national identity that underpins the political and social fabric of the Alverian Republic. Ethnic Alverians are defined not only by ancestry but by participation in a common civic culture centered on the Alverian language, Catholic heritage, and republican values.

The remaining 10% of the population consists of ethnic minorities, primarily Metosians (5%), Laocitians (3%), and a small aggregate of other communities (2%), including individuals of Boravian, Cusean, and Verdic origin. These minorities are concentrated in specific regions—Metosians along the western border in Metostan, and Laocitians in the southern districts near Velikograd—where historical migration, redrawn boundaries, and cultural interlinkage have produced durable enclaves. While these groups maintain distinctive cultural practices, including traditional festivals, cuisine, and informal linguistic continuity within families, they are generally well integrated into the broader civic structure.

Alveria’s constitution prohibits ethnic discrimination and enshrines the principle of equal citizenship regardless of origin. In practice, the state does not collect official data on ethnicity in censuses or administrative documents, in line with its civic-unity model. However, academic and municipal studies consistently affirm the continued presence of cultural and linguistic diversity, particularly in urban areas, where intermarriage and demographic fluidity are increasingly common.

Ethnic minorities are granted the freedom to maintain cultural organizations, publish in their own languages, and operate private educational institutions, provided these comply with national educational and legal standards. Public education, government proceedings, and state media operate exclusively in Alverian, but minority groups often supplement formal education with after-school cultural programs, religious schooling, and heritage associations. The state provides modest support to registered minority associations through the Office for Cultural Inclusion, which allocates annual grants for festivals, publications, and language preservation projects.

Relations among ethnic communities in Alveria are generally stable, though isolated incidents of cultural tension—particularly during electoral periods or policy debates on immigration—have occasionally surfaced. In such cases, national authorities and community leaders have prioritized dialogue, mediation, and civic education, avoiding systemic polarization. Political representation of minority groups exists primarily through local governance and municipal councils, with occasional representation in Parliament by candidates running on minority-interest platforms or within broader coalition lists.

Alveria does not follow a model of ethnic pluralism or affirmative action, but rather promotes integration through shared institutions and civic participation, encouraging all citizens to engage with the national language, legal system, and public life. This approach has historically succeeded in maintaining social cohesion, while allowing limited space for cultural differentiation within a firmly defined national framework.

Overall, while ethnic Alverians dominate demographically and institutionally, the country accommodates its minority populations within a legal and cultural order that emphasizes unity without forced assimilation. The state’s policies aim to preserve internal stability while respecting individual identity, achieving a careful balance between majoritarian cultural continuity and democratic inclusiveness.
