Comussania, officially the Comussan Confederation, is a country located in northeastern Edrobe, bordered by Jarnovia, Verdania, and Agaritia, with its eastern and southern coasts along the Grandic Ocean. The country spans 637,214 km², with a population of 47.86 million, making it one of the most populous and powerful states in the region. Its capital is Sandropos, and the official language is Comussan.

The state is built around a dominant ethnic Comussan majority (89%), yet is also home to one of the most ethnically diverse populations in Edrobe (11%), with communities from Verdania, Jarnovia, Ricene, Tayanusa, and beyond. While Comussania does not enforce assimilation, it maintains a unifying civic structure centered around language, law, and national identity, with Catholicism as the dominant religion (87%) and a powerful cultural influence in public and private life.

Governance operates under a federal structure of nine autonomous regions, each with its own government, while federal authority is shared between a President, and a bicameral legislature—the House of Deputies and House of Regions. The country has a stable liberal democratic system, dominated since its founding by the Union Party and the Labor Party, forming a bipartisan regime.

Economically, Comussania ranks 4th in the world by GDP (3.2 trillion DTR). Its strengths lie in mining (diamonds, gold, iron) from the Dalacchian Mountains, a powerful industrial sector (automobiles, semiconductors, heavy machinery), and export-driven trade supported by an advanced infrastructure and skilled labor force. Agriculture is constrained by terrain but produces gum, carrots, and vast quantities of livestock, especially sheep. The country imports much of its food and invests in technological solutions for self-sufficiency.

Comussania is the third most powerful military power in the world. Its global influence is projected through massive arms exports, making it the largest weapons supplier in Edrobe, and through joint military drills, technical assistance, and strategic defense cooperation. It maintains military-industrial partnerships with Fesia and a strategic, but not allied, relationship with Cusea, focused on energy, finance, infrastructure, and intelligence.

In foreign policy, Comussania supports global multilateralism, beign member of many international organizations. Within Edrobe it strongly defends national sovereignty, opposing efforts to transform the Edrobean Union into a supranational union.

With a median age of 38.5, rising urbanization, strong education, and controlled immigration, Comussania is a disciplined, militarized, and strategically self-assured nation, managing diversity under a strong civic-national model while asserting itself as one of the great powers of its world.

Etymology

The name Comussania derives from the ancient word Komusen, a term rooted in the Old Comussan linguistic substratum that once dominated the coastal and forested regions along the Harpoks Sea. In its earliest recorded use, Komusen referred to a confederation of semi-nomadic clans inhabiting the inland basins and estuarial corridors of what is now central Comussania. The term itself is believed to mean "people of the convergence"—a reference to the numerous tribal assemblies and seasonal gatherings held at river junctions, where early social and trade networks formed among diverse ethnic and linguistic groups.

As these tribal alliances grew more complex and institutionalized, the word evolved from a descriptor of people into a marker of territory. The Latinized variant, Comussan, was adopted during the late medieval period by chroniclers of the Federated Ports Pact, a forerunner of modern Comussan statehood that united merchant cities along the Gulf of Adainal. The suffix -ia, denoting territorial designation, was added during the Enlightenment-era constitutional movements that aimed to standardize national identities across Edrobe. Thus, the modern form Comussania came to denote both a place and a political idea: a union of regions, peoples, and traditions under a shared legal and cultural framework.

The etymology reflects the historical trajectory of the Comussan Confederation, whose federal structure and cultural pluralism are mirrored in the very name of the state. Unlike ethnically monolithic states, Comussania has long viewed its national identity as a synthesis of diverse regional and communal elements, making the term "Comussan" as much a civic as an ethnic designation. Today, the word remains central in national iconography, official literature, and public institutions.

Geography

Comussania occupies a strategically significant and geographically diverse position in the northeastern quadrant of the Edrobean continent, encompassing an area of approximately 637,214 km². It shares land borders with Jarnovia to the north, Verdania to the northwest, and Agaritia to the west, while its southern and eastern frontiers are maritime, opening onto the Grandic Ocean, including the Sea of Harpoks and the Gulf of Adainal. This coastal access has historically facilitated maritime commerce, naval development, and cultural interchange, reinforcing Comussania’s geopolitical orientation as both an inland and a seafaring nation.

Topographically, the country is defined by its rugged mountainous terrain, particularly along its southern and eastern coastal margins, where the Dalacchian Mountains rise dramatically from the shoreline. These mountains are among the highest and most geologically complex in Edrobe, featuring snow-covered peaks, deep glacial valleys, and active tectonic formations. The range forms a formidable natural barrier and has played a pivotal role in shaping Comussania’s regional identities, defense strategies, and infrastructure planning. The mountain corridors host a network of tunnels and passes that connect inland industrial centers to coastal ports, serving as vital arteries of internal commerce.

Beyond the highland belt, the interior of Comussania transitions into vast expanses of taiga, a biome characterized by coniferous forests, permafrost pockets, and seasonal marshlands. These taigas dominate much of the central and northern landscape, especially in the provinces of Grodensia and East Valerik, where they support a rich ecosystem of native wildlife, including bears, elk, lynxes, and migratory birds. The taiga zones are sparsely populated but hold ecological and economic value through forestry, regulated hunting, and eco-tourism. Seasonal variation in these regions is extreme, with long, snow-covered winters and brief, lush summers that contribute to the country’s biodiversity and environmental distinctiveness.

The hydrographic system of Comussania, while not as dense as in riverine nations, is defined by several major waterways that serve both ecological and geopolitical functions. Chief among them is the Manchel River, which originates in the central highlands and flows southeastward, forming part of the natural boundary with Agaritia. It is the principal source of irrigation, hydroelectric power, and freshwater supply, and supports riverine transport in some navigable stretches. The Saffer River, flowing in a more southerly direction, complements this function and similarly helps delineate the western frontier. Both rivers contribute to shared water management regimes with neighboring states and are subject to environmental agreements under the Edrobean Union’s resource protocols.

Coastal geography is no less dramatic. The eastern shoreline along the Sea of Harpoks is marked by cliffed coasts, narrow fjords, and rocky inlets, interspersed with small fishing settlements and naval outposts. The southern coast along the Gulf of Adainal, though still rugged, offers more navigable bays and hosts the country’s largest ports, including Port Elvensk and Adainalgrad, which serve as economic gateways for Comussania’s maritime exports and naval operations. The coastal terrain limits extensive urban development but provides ample opportunities for strategic defense, marine research, and energy infrastructure, including offshore wind and tidal power projects.

Climatically, Comussania spans subarctic to temperate zones, with cold winters, moderate summers, and significant snowfall in the highlands. This environmental profile influences agricultural viability, energy demand, and transportation logistics, especially in remote provinces. The government actively invests in weather-resilient infrastructure, including underground transport, heated pipelines, and smart-grid energy distribution systems designed to withstand climatic extremes.

Politics

Government

Comussania functions as a federal presidential republic, established by its Constitution, and built upon principles of regional autonomy, legal pluralism, and a strong commitment to institutional democracy. Its governance model is characterized by a dual balance of power—between the central federal authorities and the constituent regions, and between the executive and legislative branches—reflecting the historical evolution of the confederation from a coalition of autonomous provinces into a modern, unified polity.

At the federal level, the President serves as both head of state and head of government, elected through a direct national vote every five years, and vested with broad executive powers. The President is assisted by a Vice President, who acts as deputy head of the executive and assumes duties in the President’s absence. Together, they lead the Federal Cabinet, composed of ministers responsible for defense, finance, justice, education, environment, infrastructure, and interregional cooperation. The President directs national policy, commands the armed forces, appoints federal officials, proposes the budget, and represents Comussania internationally. Executive authority is centralized, but tempered by legal limits and legislative oversight.

The legislative branch is embodied in the Federal Assembly, a bicameral legislature consisting of the House of Deputies and the House of Regions. The House of Deputies is elected directly by citizens through a mixed electoral system combining proportional representation and constituency mandates. It holds primary lawmaking authority, with control over national legislation, budget approval, and treaty ratification. The House of Regions represents regional governments and safeguards regional interests in federal policymaking. Each region appoints its delegates according to its own laws, and the chamber holds veto power over constitutional amendments and laws affecting federal-regional relations.

The judiciary is anchored by the Federal Constitutional Court, led by the Chief Justice, and tasked with interpreting the constitution, resolving interregional disputes, and reviewing the constitutionality of legislation and executive actions. Beneath it operate the Federal Supreme Court, appellate courts, and specialized tribunals for civil, criminal, and administrative matters. Judicial independence is constitutionally guaranteed, with judges selected through a transparent process involving parliamentary vetting and expert council recommendations.

Comussania is composed of twelve constituent regions, each with its own regional parliament, government, and judicial system. These regions enjoy wide autonomy in areas such as education, local policing, infrastructure, cultural policy, and internal economic affairs. Legal diversity is permitted as long as regional laws align with federal principles and respect fundamental rights. Fiscal equalization mechanisms are in place to reduce regional disparities, overseen by the Federal Commission for Interregional Solidarity.

Political parties

Comussania is a multiparty liberal democracy, shaped by a deeply entrenched two-party system that has defined its political landscape. While the constitution guarantees free elections, civil liberties, and multiparty participation, political power is heavily concentrated between two dominant blocs. Structural advantages, state-aligned media, and electoral mechanisms ensure their sustained dominance, while smaller parties remain marginal and largely excluded from meaningful national influence.

The currently dominant political force is the Union Party, a nationalist, right-wing, Christian conservative organization that has governed Comussania for the majority of its modern history. It promotes centralized federal authority, a strong military, religious traditionalism, and a strict interpretation of Comussan national identity rooted in Christian morality. The Union Party maintains close ties with the clergy, military leadership, and industrial elites, advocating for state-led economic planning, restrictive immigration policies, and a defense-oriented foreign policy. Though formally committed to democratic procedures, the party exercises tight control over regional administrations and federal appointments, favoring loyalists and consolidating authority. The majority of presidents have come from the Union Party.

Opposing it is the Labor Party, a center-left, social democratic, and liberal formation advocating for regional autonomy, secularism, social justice, and institutional reform. It draws support from urban professionals, students, labor unions, and pro-Edrobean reformists, promoting decentralization, minority rights, civil freedoms, and an open foreign policy aimed at deeper integration with the Edrobean Union. While the Union Party has consistently dominated the presidency and federal executive, the Labor Party has achieved narrow majorities in the legislature on occasion, playing a key role in shaping federal policy and maintaining the appearance of pluralistic balance.

Administrative subdivisions

The Comussan Confederation is organized as a federal state composed of nine autonomous regions, each functioning as a semi-sovereign entity within the broader constitutional framework of the confederation. These regions—Grunenia, Silberstado, Himmelstado, Rothenia, Schwaregia, Spadania, Tonosia, Keleschna, and Harmile—form the primary administrative and political units of the state, reflecting Comussania’s historical emphasis on regional identity, cultural autonomy, and local governance.

Each region possesses its own regional government, typically composed of a Regional Assembly (Landtag or equivalent), a Governor (or First Minister), and a series of regional ministries responsible for domains such as education, health, internal affairs, transport, and culture. These institutions operate under regional constitutions or charters, which must remain in harmony with the federal constitution but allow wide discretion in policymaking, taxation, and legal interpretation. This framework empowers regions to tailor legislation to the needs of their populations while ensuring uniformity in areas of federal competence such as defense, foreign policy, and currency.

The regional governments exercise control over primary and secondary education, regional infrastructure, housing policy, local policing, and environmental regulation, among other areas. They are also authorized to manage regional development funds, negotiate interregional compacts, and issue administrative decrees. The judiciary in each region includes provincial courts, which operate under regional law but are integrated into the national judicial hierarchy through federal appellate courts.

The House of Regions, the upper house of the Parliament, is composed of representatives appointed by each regional government, ensuring that regional interests are institutionally embedded in federal legislation and constitutional amendments. This structure provides a direct mechanism for regional influence over federal decision-making, reinforcing the confederation’s identity as a union of self-governing territories rather than a unitary state.

Fiscal relations between the federation and its regions are governed by the Federal Fiscal Equalization Act, which guarantees minimum service standards across all regions while preserving the right of wealthier regions—such as Silberstado and Grunenia—to retain surplus revenues. The Federal Commission for Interregional Affairs mediates disputes over funding, competence, and administrative coordination, ensuring cohesion without eroding autonomy.

Despite strong federal oversight in areas of national interest, the regions retain the ability to pursue divergent policy paths, leading to noticeable variation in education systems, healthcare management, and social services across Comussania. This decentralization has fostered a climate of regional innovation and cultural preservation, but has also prompted periodic debates about efficiency, duplication, and national unity.

Comussania’s administrative model is thus defined by a deliberate balance between decentralization and integration, where regional self-rule coexists with a strong federal core, allowing for both local responsiveness and collective governance in one of the most structurally complex republics in Edrobe.

Foreign relations

Comussania maintains a complex and assertive foreign policy, combining a firm commitment to multilateral cooperation with the strategic use of its military-industrial influence and a consistent ideological defense of liberal democratic systems. As a full member of the United Nations, the Edrobean Union, the Global Reserve Fund, the World Trade Network, and the International Energy Agency, Comussania positions itself as a central actor in global governance, particularly in matters of collective security, trade regulation, and technological coordination.

Through joint military exercises, defense contracts, and technical training programs, Comussania has cultivated an extensive network of strategic and defense partnerships across the region. This approach enables the country to exert geopolitical influence and promote alignment among its allies without direct military intervention, consolidating its image as a cornerstone of continental defense cooperation.

Its most significant partnership is with Valmirica, founded on mutual interests in finance, energy, intelligence sharing, manufacturing, and infrastructure development, as well as in defense procurement and arms cooperation. The two nations frequently coordinate their positions within international institutions, leveraging their combined economic strength and logistical capabilities. The partnership is often described as one of strategic complementarity, balancing Valmirica’s industrial capacity with Comussania’s technological and organizational expertise.

A defining feature of Comussania’s foreign doctrine is its geopolitical rivalry to Destenia. Relations between the two countries remain limited to minimal economic coordination, with Comussania maintaining a stance of principled rivalry, rejecting Destenia’s political model as incompatible with Edrobean democratic values.

Within the Edrobean Union, Comussania opposes efforts toward federalization, firmly defending national sovereignty and regional autonomy against centralizing trends. While it supports functional cooperation in sectors such as border management, climate policy, and scientific research, it consistently resists the establishment of supranational authorities that could erode the independence of member states. This stance aligns Comussania with other sovereignty-oriented EC members, placing it in contrast with the federalist bloc.

Although its relationship with Agaritia has cooled in recent years, Comussania continues to pursue selective strategic partnerships across Edrobe and beyond, especially in cyber-defense, transport connectivity, and technical standardization. Its growing collaboration with Fesia, a major global power, includes space surveillance, arms development, and digital security programs, further reinforcing Comussania’s international stature through advanced civil-military technological cooperation.

Military

Comussania maintains the third most powerful military in the world. Its military doctrine is rooted in strategic assertiveness, rapid deployment capability, and regional projection, making it one of the few countries with a permanent overseas military presence. The Comussan Armed Forces operate under the centralized command of the Ministry of Defense, with overall strategic coordination led by the Chief of the General Staff, and executive policy shaped by the President of the Confederation which serves as the Commander-in-Chief. The military is structured into four core branches:

  • Comussan Army, responsible for land operations and territorial defense, are composed of mechanized infantry divisions, heavy armor units, mountain warfare brigades, and special operations groups. Their presence is strongest in the interior highlands and border regions, and they are trained for both high-intensity conflict and regional stabilization missions. The Ground Forces operate advanced artillery systems, integrated battlefield drones, and hybrid terrain vehicles adapted for both taiga and mountain deployment.
  • Comussan Navy plays a dominant role in coastal security, amphibious operations, and regional maritime dominance across the Sea of Harpoks and the Gulf of Adainal. It fields a fleet of guided missile frigates, stealth corvettes, attack submarines, and amphibious landing craft.
  • Comussan Air Force, a technologically advanced component of the armed forces, maintains fleets of multirole fighter jets, strategic bombers, surveillance drones, and heavy transport aircraft. The air force is fully networked into satellite-guided systems, allowing for high-precision strike capabilities and intercontinental logistics. It also cooperates extensively with Cusea in areas such as joint aircraft development and orbital reconnaissance platforms.
  • Comussan Strategic Force, the newest and most classified branch, oversees cyber-warfare, electronic intelligence, anti-satellite weapons, missile systems, and strategic deterrence infrastructure. While Comussania is not a nuclear-armed state, its conventional missile capabilities are advanced, and its defense infrastructure includes hypersonic systems, EMP-hardened command centers, and long-range cruise missiles developed.

The armed forces are entirely professional and volunteer-based. Military academies, technical colleges, and elite training centers maintain a steady stream of officers and specialists, many of whom train abroad in joint programs with Jarnovia and Verdania. Annual defense spending stands at 4.1% of GDP, one of the highest in Edrobe, reflecting both political consensus and strategic necessity. it has since rebuilt and modernized its military capabilities, focusing on deterrence, alliance-building, and non-traditional warfare.

Law enforcement

Law enforcement in the Comussan Confederation operates under a federated and multilayered structure, reflecting the country’s commitment to regional autonomy, internal security, and centralized coordination of national law enforcement standards. The system is anchored by a strong emphasis on public order, surveillance infrastructure, and internal cohesion, with security services playing a central role not only in crime prevention, but also in preserving the ideological stability of the confederal order.

At the federal level, the primary agency is the Federal Police, which functions under the authority of the Ministry of Internal Affairs. The FPD is responsible for combating organized crime, cybercrime, high-level corruption, and terrorism. It oversees border protection, interregional investigations, and manages national databases on criminal activity. With jurisdiction across all nine autonomous regions, the FPD operates elite investigative units, rapid-response teams, and an advanced surveillance network linked to traffic, banking, and communications systems. The agency also administers the National Surveillance Grid, a coordinated data-sharing platform that includes facial recognition and predictive behavior algorithms.

Each of the nine regions maintains its own Regional Police, which operates independently in most routine law enforcement matters. These agencies are controlled by regional ministries of the interior and vary in size, training standards, and equipment depending on the political orientation and budget of each regional government. The RPS handles local crime prevention, patrol duties, Union policing, and enforcement of regional ordinances. In conservative regions like Rothenia and Silberstado, the RPS is often closely aligned with federal priorities, while in more liberal regions such as Spadania and Tonosia, the local police tend to emphasize civil rights, Union oversight, and judicial transparency.

In addition to these forces, Comussania maintains the National Guard, a militarized police force tasked with riot control, counter-insurgency, protection of critical infrastructure, and intervention in politically sensitive areas. The Gendarmerie reports directly to the Ministry of Defense in times of emergency and can be deployed rapidly across regional boundaries without local authorization. It is especially active in border zones, urban centers experiencing unrest, and areas with a history of political dissent. Critics of the Gendarmerie argue that it functions as a tool of state intimidation, though supporters claim it is necessary for national stability.

Internal intelligence is managed by the Comussan Internal Security Directorate, a powerful agency with broad authority to conduct surveillance, detain individuals suspected of undermining the constitutional order, and coordinate with military intelligence in times of national threat. Law enforcement officers across all branches are trained at the Federal Institute for Security Sciences, where curricula focus on tactical discipline, behavioral analysis, ideological security, and legal operations under confederal law. While Comussan police forces are nominally nonpartisan, many senior commanders are politically aligned with the Union Party, especially at the federal level.

Economy

Comussania's economy is a diversified and export-oriented system, ranking fourth globally by GDP at approximately 3.2 trillion DTR. Structured around a balance between natural resource exploitation, industrial production, and technological advancement, the Comussan economic model reflects the nation's strategic use of geography, state coordination, and global trade integration to sustain its large population and geopolitical weight. While facing structural limitations—such as terrain-related agricultural constraints—Comussania has compensated through mineral wealth, industrial innovation, and a commitment to infrastructure and education as drivers of long-term competitiveness.

The primary sector, while no longer dominant in GDP contribution, remains essential for food security and rural employment. Comussania’s mountainous terrain and limited arable land restrict large-scale crop cultivation; nevertheless, the country has become a specialized producer of gum and carrots, both grown intensively in designated agricultural zones supported by subsidies and state-run cooperatives. These commodities are cultivated using greenhouse technology and terraced farming systems, which mitigate land constraints and allow for year-round production in controlled environments.

In the livestock sector, Comussania boasts the largest sheep population in Edrobe, a cornerstone of its wool, dairy, and meat processing industries. Livestock farming is concentrated in alpine pastures and taiga margins, supported by strong veterinary regulation and domestic demand. Despite these strengths, the country remains dependent on food imports, particularly cereals, fruits, and oils, due to its limited plains. This has made agricultural trade policy a critical component of national planning, with significant investments in food logistics, international supply agreements, and agricultural research, including initiatives into vertical farming and hydroponic technologies.

The secondary sector is a pillar of Comussania’s economic strength, particularly due to its strategic mineral resources and industrial base. The Dalacchian Mountains, rich in diamonds, gold, and iron, serve as the backbone of a powerful mining industry, tightly regulated and partially state-owned to control environmental impact and ensure sovereign resource management. These minerals are refined domestically and exported in both raw and processed forms, forming a major source of foreign currency.

Comussania has also emerged as a regional leader in advanced manufacturing, with key industries including automobiles, semiconductors, aerospace components, and heavy industrial machinery. Major industrial zones in Harmile, Grunenia, and Silberstado host vertically integrated supply chains, supported by robust energy systems and high-speed logistics. The government incentivizes industrial innovation through tax exemptions, export credits, and strategic public-private partnerships, resulting in strong international demand for Comussan-manufactured goods. The state’s dual-use industrial policy also allows for close cooperation between civilian and defense industries, particularly in electronics and automation.

The tertiary sector has experienced rapid modernization, driven by investments in education, infrastructure, and public services. Comussania’s transportation network—including high-speed railways, trans-regional highways, and port complexes on the Gulf of Adainal—is among the most advanced in Edrobe. Its energy grid, built for efficiency and redundancy, is powered by a mix of hydroelectric, geothermal, and imported fossil fuels, ensuring reliable supply even in remote mountainous regions.

Comussania is firmly embedded in global trade networks, exporting its mineral resources, industrial goods, and precision technologies to markets across Edrobe, Tayanusa, and the Global South. Strategic trade relationships with Cusea and Fesia, alongside a complex but functioning economic engagement with Destenia, ensure diversified export routes and financial resilience. The Comussan Pound is freely convertible and supported by a technocratic central bank, which maintains a conservative monetary policy and stable inflation.

However, the country faces persistent challenges, including agricultural vulnerability, environmental degradation in mining zones, and regional economic disparities between industrial cores and mountainous peripheries. The government has responded with a new national plan focused on digital transformation, green technology, and food system innovation, with targeted investment in automated agriculture, AI-driven manufacturing, and sustainable logistics.

Demography

Comussania, with a population of 47.86 million, ranks among the most populous nations in Edrobe and exhibits a complex demographic profile shaped by a combination of high internal cohesion, urban concentration, and international diversity. Despite being a strongly centralized state with a dominant national culture, Comussania’s population structure reveals regional variation, slow but steady growth, and a gradual shift towards urbanization and demographic aging, tempered by modest immigration and internal mobility.

Approximately 72% of the population resides in urban areas, a figure that continues to rise due to steady internal migration from mountainous and rural provinces toward industrial and administrative centers such as Sandropos, Silberstado, Harmile, and Tonosia. These cities have become hubs for employment, higher education, infrastructure, and services, often reflecting the country’s social stratification, where ethnic Comussans dominate public administration while minority communities concentrate in logistics, construction, and private entrepreneurship. Rural regions—especially Schwaregia, Spadania, and the alpine fringes of Himmelstado—remain sparsely populated but culturally influential, preserving traditional lifestyles and local governance patterns.

The median age in Comussania is 38.5 years, positioning the country within the transitional band between youthful and aging societies. The fertility rate hovers around 1.91 children per woman, slightly below replacement level but higher than many other industrialized Edrobean nations. This is due in part to generous family welfare policies, including paid parental leave, state-subsidized childcare, and cultural support for large families in rural Catholic communities. Life expectancy stands at 80.2 years for women and 75.6 for men, with public health outcomes differing slightly between regions due to disparities in infrastructure and medical staffing.

Household size averages 2.7 persons, with dual-income nuclear families becoming the norm in urban areas, while multi-generational households persist in rural and traditionalist regions. Marriage and divorce rates are stable, with a growing tendency toward later family formation and childbearing, especially among urban professionals. The education system—compulsory until age 18—is highly centralized, and literacy rates approach 99%, supported by a network of state schools, Catholic institutions, and elite technical academies linked to industrial sectors.

Comussania's labor force is aging but robust, supported by strong vocational training systems and internal mobility programs. The unemployment rate remains low, fluctuating between 4.5% and 5.2%, though economic disparities between northern and southern regions persist. Strategic workforce planning includes increasing reliance on foreign technicians and specialists, particularly in energy, semiconductors, logistics, and health care.

Looking forward, demographic forecasts suggest a gradual population plateau, with aging pressures offset by controlled immigration, pro-family incentives, and automation. The central government has introduced long-term plans to expand elderly care infrastructure, promote balanced regional development, and sustain population growth through education and employment access.

Language

The official language of the Comussan Confederation is Comussan, a distinct national language spoken natively by the vast majority of the population and used exclusively in all public, legal, administrative, military, and educational contexts. Comussan is a central pillar of the country's identity, promoted as the sole unifying linguistic standard across the nine autonomous regions, despite the country’s ethnic and regional diversity. It is constitutionally designated as the only official language, and its mastery is a prerequisite for citizenship, public employment, and higher education.

All federal institutions, including the Federal Assembly, judiciary, and armed forces, operate exclusively in Comussan. Likewise, all primary and secondary education across the Confederation is conducted in Comussan, regardless of region or local ethnic composition. Public signage, broadcast media, tax forms, and official identification use Comussan only. The language is taught from early childhood through adulthood with a strong emphasis on linguistic purity, cultural pride, and state unity.

Minority languages are present, particularly in regions bordering Verdania and Jarnovia, where small communities still speak local dialects with roots in those neighboring cultures. These include Verdanic, Jarnovic, and a few isolated dialects unique to rural border areas. However, these languages have no official status, and the state does not provide public services, education, or media in any language other than Comussan. Private use of minority languages is allowed, especially in homes and cultural associations, but they are steadily declining due to assimilation and a lack of institutional support.

Religion

Religion in the Comussan Confederation is deeply embedded in the nation’s cultural fabric, with the overwhelming majority of the population—87%—identifying as Christian, and nearly all of these adhering to the universal Catholic Church. Although the federal constitution upholds religious freedom and secular governance, Catholicism exerts a dominant social, moral, and symbolic influence throughout Comussanian society, shaping both public rituals and private life across all nine regions.

The Catholic Church in Comussania operates through a highly centralized national structure under the Comussan Episcopal Authority, which manages dioceses, seminaries, and religious schools, and plays an active role in guiding moral debates, public education content, and cultural affairs. While the state does not legally endorse any religion, successive Union Party governments have maintained close informal ties with the Church, often consulting it on matters of family policy, bioethics, and civic education. Catholic holidays such as Holy Week, Christmas, Ascension Day, and Saints’ Vigil are recognized as national public holidays, and religious imagery remains prevalent in many public institutions, especially in rural regions and traditionalist municipalities.

Mass attendance is high compared to regional averages, particularly in the regions of Schwaregia, Silberstado, and Rothenia, where the Church maintains a strong pastoral presence and Union role. In more liberal areas such as Spadania and Tonosia, Catholicism remains influential, though practice tends to be more private and culturally symbolic than doctrinally strict. The Church also plays an active role in education and health care, managing a network of Catholic schools, hospitals, and charities, many of which receive indirect public funding through service agreements or cultural preservation grants.

The non-religious population accounts for about 12%, concentrated mainly in younger urban populations and academic sectors. Although legally protected, open secularism and atheistic activism are often met with social resistance, particularly in traditionalist regions. Public criticism of the Church is uncommon in mainstream media, and political figures are expected—if not legally required—to maintain respectful relations with the clergy. Religious education remains optional in public schools, but most students participate due to strong parental and institutional pressure.

Other religions constitute less than 1% of the population and include small communities of Verdanic spiritual minorities, Jarnovian sects, and a limited number of agnostic philosophical societies. These groups are allowed to operate private places of worship and publish religious material, but they receive no public support and are largely absent from national discourse. The Religious Affairs Registry, an office within the Ministry of Justice, manages the licensing of religious associations, ensuring compliance with national law and monitoring activities deemed incompatible with Comussan civic values.

Ethnicities

Comussania is a pluralistic yet nationally cohesive society, characterized by a strong ethnic Comussan majority alongside a remarkably diverse array of minority communities. Ethnic Comussans constitute approximately 89% of the population, forming the cultural, linguistic, and institutional backbone of the Confederation. Their dominance in public life, education, administration, and political leadership reflects a centuries-long continuity rooted in the historical development of the Comussan regions, particularly along the coastal heartland and the Dalacchian mountain corridor.

The remaining 11% of the population is composed of non-Comussan minorities originating from across the world, making Comussania the second most ethnically diverse nation in Edrobe, surpassed only by Destenia. These communities include long-settled populations from neighboring countries such as Verdania, Jarnovia, and Laocitia, as well as more recent immigrants and descendants of settlers from distant regions—particularly Fesian industrial workers, Ricenic entrepreneurs, Tayanusan professionals, and economic migrants from the Edrobean South and Global East. Their presence is most visible in the metropolitan regions of Sandropos, Tonosia, and Harmile, where multicultural urban centers have emerged, housing ethnic enclaves, bilingual schools, foreign-owned businesses, and cultural associations.

Unlike many other states with comparable diversity, Comussania does not pursue a policy of forced integration or cultural assimilation. Instead, the state adopts a model of managed coexistence, where all citizens are expected to adhere to Comussan civic and legal norms, but are otherwise free to maintain their native languages, religions, customs, and internal Union structures in private life. There are no mandatory “Comussanization” programs, and the government does not attempt to erase minority identities, provided they do not contradict the constitutional order or public morality.

Public life, however, remains heavily centered around Comussan language, history, and Catholic traditions, which limits access to higher positions of influence for those outside the majority group unless they voluntarily integrate. Naturalization laws require language fluency and civic knowledge, but not ethnic identification. This system allows for peaceful multiculturalism under a dominant national culture, where minorities may thrive economically and socially, but are not expected to dilute the foundational identity of the state.

Ethnic tensions are rare, as minority communities are generally stable, respected, and integrated into the labor market. Nevertheless, social stratification along ethnic lines does exist, particularly in sectors such as housing, public administration, and law enforcement, where ethnic Comussans are overwhelmingly represented. Some critics argue that this creates a soft cultural hierarchy, while defenders of the system claim it maintains national cohesion without infringing on personal freedoms.

The state does not keep ethnic statistics in public censuses, reflecting a policy of colorblind legal equality, but academic studies, municipal records, and migration data confirm that Comussania is home to dozens of ethnic and national groups, many of which operate their own schools, religious institutions, and cultural centers with minimal interference.
