Kashiria, officially the Federal Republic of Kashiria, is a country located in southwestern Edrobe, bordered by Metosia to the north, Laocitia to the northeast, the Grandic Ocean to the south, the Gulf of Kosher to the east, and the Sea of Kasher to the west. Covering 501,324 km², it ranks as the second-largest country in Edrobe and has a population of 48 million, predominantly ethnic Kashirians (96%), with small foreign communities tied to business and investment. The official language is Kashirian, and the population is primarily Muslim (87%), with a significant Catholic minority (13%).

Kashiria is a federal presidential republic composed of five constituent states, each subdivided into provinces and municipalities. Its government features a bicameral Parliament—the Chamber of States and Chamber of Deputies—with strong executive power vested in the President. The country functions under a de facto two-party system, dominated by the Kashirian National Party and the Democratic Party. The judiciary is independent and led by the Supreme Court, ensuring constitutional oversight.

Economically, Kashiria ranks 8th globally by GDP at 950 billion DTR, driven by its role as the world’s fourth-largest oil exporter, vast infrastructure—including high-speed rail and federal highways—and active participation in global trade. While the country exports energy and industrial goods, it also attracts massive foreign investment and maintains trade partnerships across Edrobe and Tayanusa, notably with Destenia in the economic sphere.

In foreign affairs, Kashiria follows a policy of neutrality, multilateralism, and strategic balance, maintaining close cultural ties with Cusea and Lamberia due to shared religious heritage, while playing an active role in the Edrobean Community. It enjoys excellent bilateral relations with Laocitia and sustains robust economic cooperation with Destenia, all while avoiding military alliances and refraining from foreign interventions.

Kashiria is also a member of many international and regional organizations like the United Nations, Global Reserve Fund, International Tribunal for Justice, Edrobean Community, World Trade Network, Islamic Cooperation Organization, Global Health Organization, International Energy Agency. Altogether, Kashiria presents itself as a disciplined, resource-rich, and strategically neutral nation, balancing tradition and modernity, central authority and federal structure, while remaining firmly anchored in its cultural identity and global economic relevance.

Etymology

The name Kashiria carries a profound etymological significance, deeply embedded in the linguistic and cultural fabric of the Kashirian people. Derived from the native word Kasheriz, which means “prosperous people”, the name reflects a self-perception rooted in abundance, resilience, and collective advancement. It encapsulates both historical memory and aspirational identity, linking the modern republic to centuries of cultural continuity and territorial stewardship.

This meaning is not merely symbolic. Kashiria’s immense wealth in natural resources, particularly its globally significant reserves of gold and petroleum, has long contributed to its economic foundation and national pride. The association between the country’s name and its material prosperity has become a defining element of its civic narrative—one that celebrates natural abundance as both a gift and a responsibility. In this sense, the etymology of “Kashiria” does more than describe its people; it articulates a worldview in which prosperity is inseparable from unity, endurance, and strategic statecraft.

Geography

Kashiria, located in western Edrobe, is a country of remarkable geographical diversity, combining vast natural landscapes with an abundance of environmental and mineral wealth. Covering an area of 501,324 square kilometers, it is the second-largest nation in the Edrobean region, and its territory stretches from mountainous interiors to expansive coastal zones, offering a striking array of climates, ecosystems, and topographical contrasts.

To the north, Kashiria borders Metosia, while the northeastern frontier connects it to Laocitia, reinforcing its geopolitical position within central Edrobe. The southern coastline opens into the Grandic Ocean, granting Kashiria access to major maritime routes, whereas the eastern shoreline is lapped by the Gulf of Kosher, a calm inland sea rich in biodiversity and maritime trade potential. To the west, the country meets the Sea of Kasher, whose serene shores form part of Kashiria’s historical and cultural heartland.

The country's interior geography is defined by significant elevation contrasts. In the Neglandia region, the imposing Neges mountain range dominates the skyline, with peaks that form natural barriers and influence local microclimates. Moving eastward, the Newvalla uplands in Piotitia present a landscape of rolling hills and moderate altitude, blending agriculture with sparse woodland. In the north, the Spetrino Plain, one of the largest lowland areas in Kashiria, supports dense population centers and extensive transport networks, while the Giusvalla highlands in Nokara offer a cooler, rugged environment favored for mining and forestry.

Several major river systems flow through Kashiria’s terrain, crucial for both ecological balance and human settlement. The Ludra River meanders through fertile valleys and supports some of the nation's most productive agricultural zones. The Nursery River, flowing from the highlands toward the east, feeds into the Gulf of Kosher and sustains key urban regions along its banks. Meanwhile, the Jebel River cuts across central Kashiria, playing a strategic role in irrigation, freshwater supply, and hydroelectric generation.

Politics

Government

The Federal Republic of Kashiria is governed as a federal presidential republic, blending a strong executive leadership with a bicameral legislative framework and a clearly defined separation of powers. The country’s political system is rooted in the Constitution, which laid the foundation for democratic governance, federalism, and the protection of civil liberties. The system reflects Kashiria’s commitment to both national unity and regional representation, ensuring that the interests of its diverse federal constituents are balanced within a unified state structure.

At the apex of the executive branch stands the President, who serves as both head of state and head of government. The President is directly elected by the people and holds considerable authority over national policy, foreign affairs, defense, and administration. The President is supported by a cabinet of ministers, who oversee various governmental departments and implement executive policies in coordination with federal and state-level institutions.

The legislative power is vested in the Parliament of Kashiria, which is composed of two chambers: the Chamber of Deputies (Lower House) and the Chamber of States (Upper House). The Chamber of Deputies represents the population directly, with members elected proportionally from across the country. The Chamber of States, on the other hand, represents the five constituent federal states, ensuring that regional perspectives are reflected in national lawmaking. This bicameral system allows for both popular and territorial representation, maintaining a balance between demographic majorities and federal interests.

The Speaker of Deputies presides over the lower chamber and plays a critical role in organizing legislative sessions, managing debates, and liaising with the executive. The Speaker also represents the legislative branch in ceremonial and intergovernmental functions. Laws passed by the Parliament must be approved by both chambers and then signed into law by the President, who has limited veto power.

The judicial system operates independently, with the Supreme Court at its highest level, headed by the Supreme Judge. The judiciary is responsible for interpreting the constitution, overseeing legal compliance, and resolving disputes between federal and state authorities. Judges are appointed based on merit and serve to uphold the rule of law, human rights, and the constitutional order.

Political parties

Kashiria operates under a multi-party democratic framework, but in practice, it has evolved into a de facto two-party system, dominated by two major political forces that represent contrasting ideological currents within the country’s political landscape. While smaller parties exist and occasionally win regional seats or form part of local governments, national politics is largely shaped by the alternating influence of the Kashirian National Party and the Democratic Party.

The Kashirian National Party represents the center-right and moderately Islamist segment of the population. It emphasizes national sovereignty, traditional values, federal cohesion, and a moderate integration of Islamic principles into the cultural and moral fabric of society. While it does not advocate for theocratic governance, the KNP supports public recognition of religious identity, particularly in education, social policy, and public ethics. Economically, the party favors market liberalism with national safeguards, and its voter base is strong in rural regions, religious communities, and conservative urban districts. The KNP has historically positioned itself as the guardian of Kashirian unity, patriotism, and cultural heritage.

On the other end of the spectrum, the Democratic Party is a center-left, secular formation rooted in liberal democratic ideals, progressive social policy, and institutional reform. It advocates for strict separation of religion and state, civil liberties, gender equality, and stronger federal protections for minority groups. The DP supports a mixed economy, public investment in education and healthcare, and environmental sustainability. It garners strong support from younger voters, urban professionals, academic circles, and more liberal segments of society. The party sees Kashiria’s future in greater regional cooperation, social inclusivity, and modernization of governance.

These two parties have alternated in power at the federal level since the adoption of the constitution, with elections marked by high voter participation and competitive campaigns. Despite their ideological differences, both the KNP and DP are committed to the constitutional order and democratic institutions, and transitions of power have remained peaceful and lawful. While smaller parties—such as the Social Reform Bloc, the Green Federation, or regionalist movements—maintain a presence, especially at the state level, they have limited impact on national policymaking. Their influence is often absorbed through coalitions or issue-based cooperation with the dominant parties.

Administrative subdivisions

Kashiria is structured as a federal republic, composed of multiple layers of territorial administration designed to balance national unity with regional autonomy. At the highest subnational level, the country is divided into five constituent States: Piotitia, Neglandia, Vestgalia, Nokara, and Sokara. These States form the core pillars of the federation, each with its own constitutional authority, government, and legislative assembly. While all states adhere to the national constitution, they possess the power to legislate on a range of internal matters such as education, local infrastructure, public health, and cultural affairs. Each State government is headed by a State Governor and supported by a locally elected State Assembly, which operates under a state-specific legal framework aligned with national principles.

Beneath the state level, each constituent State is further divided into provinces, which function as administrative coordination units. Provinces serve primarily as intermediaries between the state and municipal governments, facilitating the implementation of public policies, managing inter-municipal projects, and overseeing provincial-level services such as regional roads, environmental oversight, and emergency management. Unlike the states, provinces do not possess legislative authority but are managed by provincial administrators, typically appointed by the state government. These administrators ensure that provincial planning aligns with both state and federal priorities.

At the local level, municipalities represent the most immediate layer of governance for Kashirian citizens. Municipalities are responsible for essential public services, including water distribution, waste management, local policing, primary education, and community welfare. Each municipality is governed by an elected mayor and a municipal council, which exercise limited legislative and fiscal authority within their jurisdiction. The size and scope of municipal governments vary widely, ranging from large urban centers with extensive bureaucratic structures to small rural towns with more limited functions. Municipalities enjoy a degree of autonomy in managing local affairs, but they must operate within the legal and budgetary frameworks defined by their respective state governments.

Foreign relations

Kashiria’s foreign policy is defined by a commitment to multilateralism, neutrality, and the principles of a multipolar world, reflecting both its geopolitical positioning and cultural identity. As a founding member of the United Nations, the country has consistently advocated for international cooperation, legal diplomacy, and balanced global governance, favoring institutions that reflect diverse voices rather than hegemonic dominance. Despite being an active participant in global forums and a prominent voice in Edrobe, Kashiria maintains a strictly neutral stance in geopolitical rivalries, preferring to act as a bridge-builder and mediator rather than aligning with power blocs.

Its foreign policy neutrality is not passive but strategic, rooted in a belief that sovereignty, religious respect, and regional stability must be preserved above alliance politics. This posture has enabled Kashiria to cultivate strong and cordial relations with all major global powers, while remaining free from binding military entanglements or ideological alignments. The country has avoided foreign bases or participation in armed coalitions, choosing instead to leverage diplomacy, trade, and regional integration as its primary tools of influence.

As a Muslim-majority nation, and part of the Islamic Cooperation Organization, Kashiria enjoys close cultural and political ties with several Islamic states, particularly Cusea and Lamberia, with whom it shares religious affinity and engages in bilateral dialogue on issues such as education, civil society, and interfaith cooperation. These relationships are marked by mutual respect and frequent diplomatic exchanges, though Kashiria refrains from participating in religious blocs, emphasizing instead intercivilizational dialogue.

At the same time, as a country located in southwestern Edrobe, Kashiria is a fully committed member of the Edrobean Community, where it plays an active and constructive role in shaping regional policy, promoting infrastructure connectivity, energy cooperation, and economic harmonization. Kashiria is viewed as a moderate, reliable voice within the EC, often mediating between larger continental players and smaller members. Its dual identity—as both a Muslim nation and a federal Edrobean republic—positions it uniquely to promote cross-cultural understanding and cooperative diplomacy within the wider global framework.

Kashiria also enjoys strong bilateral relations with Laocitia, its northeastern neighbor, with whom it shares historical ties, stable borders, and joint development initiatives. These relations have expanded in recent years to include cultural exchange programs, joint research projects, and military coordination on humanitarian operations. Economically, Kashiria maintains significant trade and investment relations with Destenia, especially in infrastructure development, energy logistics, and raw materials processing. While aligned politically with Destenia, Kashiria benefits from Destenian technology, military cooperation, capital, and industrial expertise, making the relationship an essential part of its foreign economic policy.

Military

Kashiria maintains a professional, well-structured military oriented toward defensive self-reliance, territorial integrity, and regional humanitarian support, rather than offensive projection or interventionism. The country’s armed forces operate under strict civilian oversight, with the President serving as Commander-in-Chief, and strategic planning coordinated by the Ministry of Defense in close consultation with the Supreme Military Council. The constitutionally enshrined doctrine of strict neutrality is reflected in the military’s structure and purpose: Kashiria does not maintain foreign military bases, does not participate in foreign wars, and refrains from joining military alliances, focusing instead on domestic security and peacekeeping readiness.The Kashirian Armed Forces are composed of three primary branches: the Kashirian Ground Forces, Naval Forces, and Air Defense Forces.

  • The Ground Forces are the largest component, responsible for land-based defense, border protection, and national emergencies. They are equipped with modern infantry systems, armored vehicles, and a growing capability in unmanned systems and logistics.
  • The Naval Forces, though modest in size, play a crucial role in securing Kashiria’s extensive coastline along the Grandic Ocean, Gulf of Kosher, and the Sea of Kasher. They maintain patrol ships, surveillance capabilities, and a small but efficient marine corps, with a focus on maritime security, anti-smuggling operations, and humanitarian response.
  • The Air Defense Forces are primarily tasked with safeguarding national airspace and supporting ground operations. They operate a fleet of multipurpose aircraft, drones, and surface-to-air defense systems designed for rapid response and homeland security. The air force also contributes to disaster relief and medical evacuation during natural crises.

Kashiria’s military is characterized by its technocratic professionalism and strict legal limitations, including clear rules of engagement, civilian command structures, and constitutional limits on deployment. The country maintains compulsory military service, with conscription periods adjusted based on educational background and federal needs. Training emphasizes defense, emergency response, and cyber defense, with growing investment in domestic defense industries and technological innovation, particularly in surveillance, communication systems, and terrain-based mobility platforms.

While remaining outside formal defense alliances, Kashiria does engage in regional security cooperation, especially with trusted neighbors such as Destenia and Cusea, participating in joint drills, intelligence exchanges, and border coordination. It also contributes troops to international humanitarian and peacekeeping missions, often under coordination with the Edrobean Community’s civilian response units.

Law enforcement

Law enforcement in Kashiria is a central component of the country’s internal security framework, structured to uphold the rule of law, public order, and civil rights in accordance with the national constitution. The system is governed under the authority of the Ministry of Interior, which oversees coordination between federal and state-level police institutions across the republic’s five constituent states. The law enforcement apparatus is decentralized in practice but unified in standards, ensuring that legal norms are applied consistently throughout the federation while allowing for administrative flexibility in diverse regions.

The core of Kashiria’s policing structure is the Kashirian National Police, a civilian force responsible for general law enforcement duties, crime prevention, traffic regulation, and emergency response. The KNP operates throughout the country and maintains specialized divisions such as criminal investigations, cybercrime units, narcotics enforcement, and organized crime intelligence, all of which are trained under national protocols and supervised by federal inspectors. Officers of the KNP are subject to strict codes of conduct and professional standards, with a strong emphasis on community-oriented policing and non-lethal conflict resolution.

Each constituent state has its own State Police Directorate, which works under the authority of the State Ministry of Security but in close alignment with federal guidelines. These state forces are empowered to handle local crime, coordinate with municipal governments, and maintain day-to-day public safety in both urban and rural areas. In regions with distinct cultural or geographic characteristics—such as mountainous Nokara or coastal Sokara—state police units are granted limited autonomy to adapt enforcement strategies to local conditions, provided they remain within the bounds of constitutional rights.

In addition to regular police forces, Kashiria maintains several specialized law enforcement agencies. The Border Security Corps is tasked with guarding land and maritime borders, preventing smuggling, and managing checkpoints. The Judicial Enforcement Unit assists courts in the execution of warrants, detentions, and property seizures. The Public Order Guard, a rapid deployment force under federal command, is used to respond to riots, large-scale protests, or public emergencies. This unit is trained in de-escalation and crowd control and can only be deployed under presidential or judicial authorization.

Kashiria also places strong emphasis on internal oversight and accountability mechanisms. The Office of Police Ethics and Oversight, an independent civilian body, investigates allegations of misconduct, abuse of power, or corruption within law enforcement agencies. Its findings are made public, and its authority extends to recommending disciplinary actions, criminal prosecutions, or structural reforms. Law enforcement training in Kashiria includes not only tactical and legal education but also modules in human rights, interfaith sensitivity, and de-radicalization, reflecting the country’s pluralistic society and commitment to religious harmony. The use of surveillance technologies and predictive policing tools is legally regulated, with safeguards to prevent unlawful intrusion into private life.

Economy

Kashiria possesses a highly developed and strategically diversified economy, ranking as the 8th-largest in the world with a GDP of 950 billion DTR. Its economic strength is rooted in a unique combination of natural resource wealth, robust infrastructure, and global trade integration, making it one of the most influential financial and industrial hubs in Edrobe. The state has historically adopted a mixed economic model, blending market liberalism with strong federal coordination, particularly in key strategic sectors such as energy, transport, and heavy industry.

One of Kashiria’s most defining economic features is its status as the world’s fourth-largest oil exporter. The country’s petroleum sector plays a dominant role in both national revenues and international trade, with vast oil fields concentrated in the southern coastal regions near the Gulf of Kosher and offshore reserves in the Sea of Kasher. The oil industry is supported by a highly efficient logistical chain, including modern refineries, deepwater terminals, and export pipelines that connect directly to global shipping routes. The energy sector is managed by a mix of state-owned enterprises and private investors, with rigorous environmental standards and production quotas that allow Kashiria to maintain both sustainability and profit.

Beyond oil, the country is deeply embedded in international trade networks, exporting not only hydrocarbons but also chemicals, machinery, electronics, and processed goods, while importing advanced technologies, precision equipment, and certain agricultural products. Its trade openness has attracted significant foreign direct investment from across the globe, with major financial flows coming from Destenia, Laocitia, Cusea, and regional Edrobean partners. Industrial parks, free trade zones, and special investment incentives have helped make Kashiria a regional magnet for manufacturing and service sectors, particularly in finance, logistics, and digital technologies.

A critical enabler of this economic dynamism is Kashiria’s exceptional infrastructure network. The country boasts one of the most modern and integrated transport systems on the continent, including a high-speed rail system that connects all five constituent states, a vast grid of federal highways that enable seamless inland trade, and high-capacity ports along both its eastern and southern coasts. The national railway authority, supported by public-private partnerships, ensures efficient passenger and cargo movement, making Kashiria a continental gateway for trade and transit.

The federal government places strong emphasis on infrastructure maintenance and digital transformation, investing heavily in smart cities, renewable energy development, and public service modernization. Urban centers benefit from well-maintained public utilities, digital governance platforms, and intermodal transport systems that reduce congestion and enhance productivity. These investments have raised the country’s competitiveness rankings and contributed to high standards of living and a steadily growing middle class.

Kashiria’s economic policies prioritize stability, export-driven growth, and investment security, guided by a technocratic economic council and long-term strategic planning. Despite fluctuations in oil prices and global market shifts, the country has managed to maintain strong macroeconomic indicators, low unemployment, and controlled inflation, making it a key player not only in energy markets but also in broader global financial and trade systems.

Demography

Kashiria has a population of 48,042,234 inhabitants, making it one of the mid-sized yet demographically stable nations in the Edrobean region. The country exhibits a balanced demographic structure, with a median age of approximately 35 years, reflecting a society that has moved beyond rapid population growth but retains a productive and dynamic workforce. Population growth is modest, sustained primarily through natural increase rather than migration, due to the country’s strict immigration policies and limited openness to foreign settlement.

The population is evenly distributed across the five constituent states, with higher densities in the urbanized coastal regions and economic centers, especially around the capital Kasher, the industrial zones of Piotitia, and the transport corridors of Vestgalia. In contrast, areas like Nokara and Neglandia, with their mountainous terrain and smaller towns, have lower population densities but retain strong communal and cultural continuity. Urbanization is high, with over 72% of the population living in cities or metropolitan regions, supported by modern infrastructure, public transportation, and digital services.

Kashiria maintains a low birth rate, consistent with its level of development and urbanization, with an average fertility rate of around 1.9 children per woman, slightly below replacement level. However, robust healthcare systems and high living standards contribute to a life expectancy of 78 years, with women living slightly longer than men. Public health is prioritized through universal access to medical care, state-funded prevention programs, and investments in biotechnology and hospital infrastructure.

The country boasts a literacy rate above 97%, the result of a strong and centralized education system, which emphasizes national history, language proficiency, and civic responsibility. Youth enrollment in secondary and higher education is high, and the state actively promotes STEM disciplines and vocational training to align demographic output with economic needs. Gender parity in education and employment is generally respected, though traditional roles persist more visibly in rural areas.

Kashiria is also marked by its remarkably low levels of immigration and ethnic diversity, with 96% of the population belonging to the ethnic Kashirian majority. The remaining 4%—mostly foreign professionals—do not significantly alter the country’s demographic trajectory, as integration is minimal and long-term settlement is rare. Emigration from Kashiria is negligible, with most citizens choosing to remain due to the country’s political stability, high quality of life, and limited international Kashirian diaspora.

Demographically, Kashiria is entering a period of maturity, with a slowing growth rate, aging population segments, and increasing demands for healthcare, pensions, and skilled labor. The government has responded with strategic planning focused on automation, productivity, and youth engagement, aiming to sustain economic performance without relying on population expansion. In doing so, Kashiria maintains a self-sufficient and controlled demographic profile, in line with its broader model of national sovereignty, cultural preservation, and centralized development.

Language

The official language of Kashiria is Kashirian, a distinct and standardized language belonging to the Edro-Tayanusan linguistic family, widely spoken across all five constituent states. Kashirian serves as the sole language of government, education, legislation, and media, and is a central pillar of the country’s national identity and federal unity. Its use is universal in public life, with all official documentation, parliamentary proceedings, and judicial rulings conducted exclusively in Kashirian. The state invests heavily in linguistic education, ensuring full literacy in the national language through compulsory schooling and public communication campaigns.

The country is officially monolingual, and there are no recognized regional or minority languages within federal institutions. However, in practice, small linguistic communities exist, especially among migrant or diasporic populations, who may speak other Edrobean or Tayanusan dialects within their homes or local cultural associations. These languages, while tolerated in informal settings, are not used in public administration or schools. The federal language policy strictly promotes linguistic assimilation, with the aim of reinforcing national cohesion and shared civic identity.

Kashiria's media, literature, and national broadcasting are exclusively in Kashirian, reinforcing its dominance and shaping a unified cultural narrative across the federation. National institutions, such as the Kashirian Language Academy, are tasked with overseeing language development, standardization, and the protection of linguistic integrity. All foreign films, books, and digital content are required to be dubbed or subtitled in Kashirian, ensuring accessibility and cultural coherence.

Religion

Religion in Kashiria plays an important yet constitutionally regulated role in public life, reflecting both the country’s diverse spiritual heritage and its secular institutional framework. The population is composed predominantly of Muslims (87%), with a significant Catholic Christian minority (13%), and religious coexistence is regarded as a cornerstone of national unity. The state does not endorse any particular religion but guarantees freedom of belief and worship as a fundamental right enshrined in the federal constitution.

The majority of Kashirian Muslims adhere to Sunni Islam, which influences cultural norms, festivals, and ethical discourse across much of the country. Islamic values often inform social customs, community life, and aspects of civil law at the local level, especially in the more conservative states such as Nokara and Neglandia. However, Kashiria maintains a strict separation between religion and state, ensuring that religious doctrines do not override constitutional law or civil liberties. Religious leaders are respected figures in their communities but hold no formal political authority, and religious institutions are regulated to prevent politicization.

The Catholic minority, concentrated primarily in urban centers and in the western state of Vestgalia, has deep historical roots in Kashiria, dating back to pre-federal times. Catholic communities maintain their own churches, schools, and cultural associations, and regularly participate in interfaith initiatives promoted by the federal government. Relations between Muslim and Christian populations are generally peaceful, with both groups coexisting under a shared civic framework. Religious holidays such as Eid al-Fitr and Christmas are nationally recognized, and efforts are made to ensure equal access to worship, land for religious buildings, and religious education.

The state oversees religious affairs through the Federal Office for Religious Affairs and Dialogue, which monitors compliance with secular laws, registers religious organizations, and promotes interreligious understanding and harmony. Sermons and religious teachings are subject to review in order to prevent hate speech, extremism, or incitement, with a firm emphasis on religious moderation and pluralism. In public education, religion is taught from a comparative and cultural perspective rather than a doctrinal one, with optional religious studies programs tailored to family preference and state law.

While religion remains a vital part of daily life for many Kashirians, especially in rural communities and among the elderly, the country’s younger, urban population tends to express more individualized or secularized forms of belief. Nonetheless, religious institutions continue to play a major role in charity, community development, and moral guidance, often working alongside government programs in areas such as healthcare, social welfare, and disaster relief.

Ethnicities

Kashiria is an ethnically homogeneous nation, with 96% of its population identifying as ethnic Kashirians, a group defined by a shared language, cultural heritage, and historical continuity across the five constituent states. The Kashirian ethnic identity is deeply tied to the nation’s formation, and it plays a central role in shaping the country’s social cohesion, civic traditions, and national narrative. Ethnic Kashirians form the overwhelming majority across all regions and dominate the political, economic, and cultural spheres of the republic.

The remaining 4% of the population consists of non-Kashirian minorities, the majority of whom are foreign-born professionals, investors, or technical experts who have settled in Kashiria for economic or business-related reasons. These individuals come from a wide range of countries, attracted by the nation's stable economy, advanced infrastructure, and global trade networks. Many reside in designated business zones or major urban centers, particularly in Kasher, Piotitia, and coastal hubs along the Gulf of Kosher. Their presence is tolerated for functional reasons, but they generally do not integrate into Kashirian society beyond the professional sphere.

Kashiria is not considered an immigration-friendly country. Its federal policy is characterized by strict border controls, limited naturalization pathways, and a high standard for residency permits, especially for those who do not possess high-level technical or financial qualifications. Citizenship is rarely granted to foreign nationals, and family reunification policies are highly restrictive. The state views immigration primarily through a strategic and economic lens, allowing entry only when it directly serves national interests.

Culturally, Kashiria promotes ethnic unity and social uniformity, with public discourse emphasizing the preservation of Kashirian traditions, language, and national character. While there is no institutional discrimination against foreign residents, social integration is minimal, and the state does not support multiculturalism or bilingualism. Non-Kashirians are expected to respect local customs, abide by civic norms, and refrain from political activity.
