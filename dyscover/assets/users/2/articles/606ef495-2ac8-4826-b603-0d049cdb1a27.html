Metosia, officially the Republic of Metosia, is a sovereign country in southeastern Edrobe, bordered by Boravia to the north, Kashiria to the south, Laocitia and Alveria to the east, and the Gulf of Silmara to the southeast. With an area of 45,372 km² and a population of approximately 6.46 million, Metosia is a unitary parliamentary republic, known for its liberal democracy, strong institutions, and advanced digital economy. The capital and largest city is Serijegrad, which also serves as the official capital of the Edrobean Community.

Governance in Metosia is structured around a parliamentary system led by a President as head of state and a Prime Minister as head of government. The unicameral State Assembly is presided over by Speaker, and the judiciary is headed by Chief Justice. The political landscape is democratic, pluralist, and coalition-based, with elections regularly rated as free and fair.

Metosia’s economy is one of the most technologically advanced and diversified in Edrobe, with a GDP of 600 billion DTR, ranking it 11th globally. The service sector dominates, particularly in finance, IT, and legal services, while high-tech manufacturing, clean energy, and sustainable agriculture also play key roles. It is a leader in digital governance, green infrastructure, and regional economic cooperation, especially through its membership in the Edrobean Community, United Nations, and other global institutions.

The population is 88% ethnic Metosian, with minorities including Laocitians, Alverians, Boravians, and Tayanusans. The country has a median age of 35, near-universal literacy, and an extensive welfare state that includes free healthcare, public education, and social support programs. Metosia is a secular state, with 70% of the population identifying as Christian, 25% non-religious, and a small percentage practicing other faiths. Religion plays a cultural rather than political role.

Metosian is the official language, spoken by over 95% of the population, and is seen as a unifying civic instrument. Multilingual education is widespread, and fluency in foreign languages is common in academic and diplomatic circles.

Metosia maintains professional armed forces with a defense-focused doctrine, and its law enforcement system is centralized, rights-based, and highly trusted. The country is divided into nine governorates and multiple municipalities, with elected mayors and a centralized but responsive administrative structure.

Internationally, Metosia is known for its neutral diplomacy, pro-Edrobe stance, and frequent role as a mediator in regional affairs. Hosting the capital of Edrobe, it is considered a cornerstone of continental dialogue, peacebuilding, and institutional development—a model of small-state resilience, democracy, and forward-looking governance.

Etymology

The name Metosia is believed to originate from the ancient Edrobean word “Metos,” meaning “center” or “middle land,” reflecting the country’s historic role as a crossroads of cultures, trade routes, and political currents in the central Edrobean region. Early texts from pre-unification city-states referred to the territory as the "Metosian Valley", a reference to its fertile inland basin nestled between the mountains and the coast, where various tribal communities converged.

As urbanization and political unification advanced in the early modern period, the term “Metosia” was formalized as a name symbolizing both geographic centrality and a new national identity forged from diverse ethnic, religious, and ideological roots. During the constitutional founding of the modern republic in 2020, the name was reaffirmed in law as a representation of the country’s unitary, inclusive, and forward-looking ethos.

Geography

Metosia is located in the central-southeastern region of Edrobe, bordered to the north by Boravia, to the south by Kashiria, to the east by Alveria and Laocitia, and to the southeast by the Gulf of Silmara, which forms the country’s primary maritime access point. This strategic location places Metosia at the intersection of northern forested highlands and southern arid lowlands, contributing to its ecological diversity and geopolitical significance. The country's terrain is characterized by three main geographical zones:

  • The northern region, known as the Kovren Taiga, is covered in dense coniferous forests and rolling hills. This area has cooler temperatures and significant natural resources, including timber and freshwater. The taiga stretches toward the River Andarin, a major waterway forming part of the border with Boravia. The Andarin supports hydropower and irrigation systems vital to both agriculture and industry.
  • The central and southeastern areas, referred to as the Liden Savannah, are composed of open grasslands, scattered woodlands, and fertile valleys. This region is the agricultural heartland of Metosia, hosting most of the country’s farms, towns, and infrastructure. Its transitional climate allows for year-round cultivation of grain, fruit, and pasture.
  • The southwestern region, known as the Sarenic Plain, is a dry, sparsely populated zone with semi-desert conditions, sandier soil, and extreme temperatures. Despite its harshness, it holds valuable mineral deposits and is increasingly used for solar energy production and defense testing grounds.

Metosia also features Lake Virela, a large freshwater body located in the northeast near the Boravian border. The lake is a major environmental and economic resource, supporting fishing, tourism, and water storage. To the southeast, the country meets the Gulf of Silmara, which provides access to maritime trade and contains several commercial ports and naval facilities. The Bay of Meristad, a smaller inlet of the gulf, lies near the southern coast and is known for its calm waters and growing resort industry.

Politics

Government

Metosia is a unitary parliamentary republic, governed by a framework that emphasizes democratic accountability, institutional stability, and civic participation. The system is rooted in the 2020 Constitution, which established a clear division of powers between the executive, legislative, and judicial branches, while ensuring a strong central government capable of managing national cohesion across the country’s diverse regions.

The President of the Republic, serves as the head of state. Elected by popular vote every five years, the President has primarily ceremonial functions, including representing Metosia internationally, presiding over state ceremonies, and ensuring the constitutional order. The President may also intervene during moments of political deadlock, appointing a Prime Minister or dissolving the legislature if necessary, in accordance with the Constitution.

The Prime Minister is the head of government and holds the central executive authority. Appointed by the President following legislative elections and confirmed by the State Assembly, the Prime Minister leads the Council of Ministers, formulates national policy, oversees the budget, and directs the public administration. The executive operates a professional bureaucracy known for its efficiency, transparency, and emphasis on digital governance.

Legislative authority rests with the State Assembly, a unicameral parliament composed of 120 members, elected every four years through a proportional representation system. The Assembly is presided over by the Speaker who organizes parliamentary debates and ensures procedural integrity. The Assembly passes laws, approves the national budget, ratifies treaties, and can remove the Prime Minister through a vote of no confidence.

The judicial branch is independent and led by the Supreme Court, under the authority of Chief Justice. The judiciary oversees the constitutionality of legislation, adjudicates civil and criminal cases, and protects individual rights and legal fairness. Lower courts are organized into regional circuits, with specialized chambers for commercial, administrative, and family law. Judges are appointed through a merit-based system regulated by the National Judicial Council, ensuring independence and professionalism.

Political parties

Metosia operates under a vibrant liberal democracy, with free, fair, and competitive elections held regularly at both national and local levels. The political system is characterized by pluralism, a proportional representation voting system, and a strong tradition of coalition governance. Civic participation is high, and the country’s democratic culture is reinforced by robust institutions, a free press, and active civil society. There are three main political parties that dominate the national stage:

  • Metosian Democratic Party – A center-left, progressive party that supports social equality, green development, civil liberties, and public investment in healthcare, education, and infrastructure. The MDP often leads coalitions and appeals to urban voters, professionals, students, and middle-income families. It is currently the senior party in government.
  • United Liberal Alliance – A centrist-liberal party advocating for free markets, digital innovation, human rights, and European-style federalism within Edrobe. The ULA plays a frequent kingmaker role in coalition governments and enjoys strong support among entrepreneurs, technocrats, and moderate voters.
  • People’s Conservative Front – A center-right party focused on economic stability, national sovereignty, rural development, and traditional values. While not opposed to liberal democracy, it promotes a more cautious approach to regional integration and social reform. It is strongest in rural areas and among small business owners.

Other smaller parties include the Green Movement of Metosia, the Social Justice Front, and several regional and issue-based parties. While they rarely lead governments, they often influence policy through coalitions, parliamentary committees, and civic mobilization. The State Assembly typically reflects a diverse political composition, requiring cross-party negotiation and consensus-building. Coalition governments are the norm, and the political climate is generally cooperative rather than polarized. Overall, Metosia's party system is seen as a model of democratic health in Edrobe, blending ideological diversity with institutional functionality, and ensuring that government remains both accountable and adaptable to changing public needs.

Administrative subdivisions

Metosia is divided into nine administrative regions, known officially as governorates, each functioning as a decentralized unit of governance under the framework of a unitary state. While the central government in Serijegrad retains overall sovereignty, these regions enjoy a degree of administrative autonomy in areas such as infrastructure, education, public health, and local development.

Each governorate is headed by a Regional Governor, appointed by the Council of Ministers in consultation with the President. Governors serve as the primary link between regional administrations and the central government, ensuring the implementation of national policies, coordinating emergency services, and overseeing major projects and intermunicipal planning.

Within the governorates, the country is further subdivided into municipalities, which serve as the basic level of local government. Mayors and municipal councils are directly elected by residents every four years and are responsible for managing local services such as waste collection, road maintenance, zoning, local policing, and cultural programs. Municipalities have the authority to raise local taxes and issue municipal regulations, although their powers are regulated by national law.

While Metosia’s administrative structure is centralized in terms of legislative and fiscal authority, there is a strong emphasis on municipal self-management and citizen participation. Local referendums, town councils, and public hearings are common, especially in urban centers.

The capital city, Serijegrad, holds special administrative status and functions as its own independent district with a metropolitan council. As the political, economic, and cultural heart of Metosia, it receives direct funding and planning oversight from the central government.

Foreign relations

Metosia pursues a highly active, diplomatic, and multilateral foreign policy, grounded in its role as a core member of the Edrobean Community and its identity as a regional mediator and peacebuilder. Known for its neutral posture, legalistic diplomacy, and commitment to democratic norms, Metosia has earned a reputation as a consensus-builder and honest broker in Edrobe's most complex political and security dialogues.

As the host of the Edrobean Community’s capital, Serijegrad, Metosia plays a central institutional role in the region’s political affairs. The country provides headquarters to several major Edrobean institutions, including the Edrobean Assembly and Edrobean Commission. This status enhances Metosia’s international visibility and reinforces its identity as a symbol of unity and dialogue in the continental framework.

Metosia’s foreign policy is explicitly pro-Edrobe, supporting deeper economic integration, standardized legal frameworks, shared environmental goals, and joint infrastructure projects. It advocates for a more federalized Edrobean governance model, while respecting the sovereignty of member states. Metosian diplomats are frequently involved in drafting continental agreements and leading intergovernmental negotiations on trade, energy, and regional security.

On the global stage, Metosia is a committed member of the World Union, Global Reserve Fund, International Tribunal for Justice, World Trade Network, Global Health Organization, and the International Energy Agency. Its international cooperation emphasizes development aid, peacebuilding, human rights, and environmental diplomacy. The country often sends civilian experts and mediators to conflict zones and multilateral talks.

Historically, Metosia has taken part in conflict mediation across Edrobe, including de-escalation talks between rival states, economic normalization dialogues, and cross-border security frameworks. Its neutrality and political stability allow it to serve as a platform for quiet diplomacy, civil society summits, and backchannel negotiations.

Bilateral relations are strong with most neighbors, especially Alveria, Laocitia, Verdania, and Jarnovia, with whom Metosia shares trade links, environmental programs, and democratic values. While more cautious in its engagement with Comussania or Valmirica, Metosia still maintains open communication and often acts as a channel between opposing blocs.

Military

Metosia maintains a modern, professional, and defense-oriented military under the banner of the Metosian Armed Forces. Structured to ensure territorial integrity, civil support, and international peacekeeping, the MAF operates under the Ministry of Defence and is subject to full civilian oversight by the government and the State Assembly. The armed forces are composed of three main branches:

  • Metosian Army – A well-trained and highly mobile army tasked with border security, national defense, and crisis response. It emphasizes rapid deployment units, mechanized brigades, and defensive fortification along key terrain, particularly the southern and eastern frontiers.
  • Metosian Air Force – Responsible for airspace protection, surveillance, and aerial support, it operates a fleet of multi-role aircraft, unmanned drones, and early warning systems. The Air Force is also involved in natural disaster response and environmental monitoring.
  • Metosian Navy – While Metosia does not maintain a full navy, this specialized branch secures the country’s limited coastline along the Gulf of Silmara. It operates fast patrol boats, maritime radars, and customs enforcement units, ensuring port security, anti-smuggling operations, and maritime search and rescue.

The Metosian military doctrine is strictly non-aggressive, emphasizing defensive readiness, interoperability with regional allies, and civil-military cooperation. The country does not seek regional dominance but rather aims to contribute to collective security through the Edrobean Community and international diplomacy under the United Nations.

Metosia invests approximately 1.8% of its GDP in defense, focusing on technology, training, and logistics modernization rather than sheer size. Military service is voluntary, but strongly encouraged through civic programs, scholarships, and career incentives. The armed forces are also involved in disaster relief, cybersecurity, and civil engineering, reinforcing their integration into civilian life.

The National Defence Academy in Serijegrad trains officers and specialists, with a curriculum based on ethical leadership, international law, and multinational operations. Metosian personnel regularly participate in joint exercises with Edrobean partners and contribute to peacekeeping and reconstruction missions abroad.

Public trust in the military is high, due to its professionalism, political neutrality, and focus on protecting democratic institutions, not intervening in them. While not designed for offensive campaigns, the MAF stands as a capable and responsible force—prepared, respected, and aligned with the country’s peaceful but vigilant foreign policy posture.

Law enforcement

Law enforcement in Metosia is structured around a centralized yet community-responsive model, led by the National Police Service, operating under the authority of the Ministry of Interior and Justice. The system is designed to uphold democratic values, public trust, and institutional transparency, and is widely regarded as one of the most efficient and rights-respecting in the Edrobean region.

The National Police Service is responsible for maintaining public order, crime prevention, traffic regulation, and emergency response across the country. It operates uniformly throughout all nine governorates, ensuring consistency in law enforcement practices and equal protection under the law. The NPS is organized into specialized divisions:

  • Public Safety Division – Handles patrol operations, community policing, and rapid response in urban and rural areas.
  • Criminal Investigation Division – Investigates serious crimes, including homicide, fraud, and organized crime, working closely with prosecutors and the judiciary.
  • Border and Customs Enforcement Division – Secures land crossings and port facilities, coordinating closely with the military’s coastal security for maritime surveillance.
  • Cyber and Economic Crimes Division – Focuses on digital security, cyber fraud, financial crime, and data protection enforcement.
  • Tactical Support Division – A small but elite force trained in counterterrorism, high-risk arrest operations, and critical infrastructure protection.

At the local level, municipal police departments support the NPS by enforcing local ordinances, overseeing public events, and maintaining contact with neighborhoods. Though they operate independently under elected mayors, all local departments follow national policing standards and training requirements. All police officers receive education at the Metosian Police Academy, where the curriculum includes not only law and procedure but also civil rights, de-escalation techniques, and ethical conduct. The academy has been recognized internationally for promoting human rights-centered policing and producing officers who combine professionalism with civic empathy. To ensure accountability, the Independent Police Oversight Commission conducts internal investigations, audits use-of-force incidents, and processes citizen complaints. In addition, the NPS is subject to oversight by parliamentary committees and judicial review in cases of alleged misconduct.

Economy

Metosia possesses one of the most dynamic and technologically advanced economies in Edrobe, with a GDP of 600 billion DTR, ranking 11th globally. Despite its modest land area and population, the country has emerged as a regional economic powerhouse, driven by a diversified, innovation-led, and service-oriented model. Its economic structure reflects a careful balance between market competitiveness, state coordination, and social investment.

The service sector accounts for over 70% of national GDP, with major contributions from finance, information technology, scientific research, education, and digital infrastructure services. The capital city, Serijegrad, functions as both a political and economic hub, hosting multinational corporations, Edrobean institutions, tech startups, and research foundations. Metosia is widely recognized as a center for legal arbitration, digital security, and policy innovation across the continent.

The industrial sector remains strong, focused primarily on high-value manufacturing, medical technology, aerospace components, and green energy systems. The government supports industrial R\&D through targeted subsidies, export credits, and public-private partnerships. Key industrial regions lie along the Liden Corridor, linking the central savannahs with the port zone on the Gulf of Silmara.

Although the country lacks significant natural resources, its energy strategy is among the most advanced in the world. Metosia has heavily invested in solar fields in the Sarenic Plain, hydropower in the north, and smart grid integration, significantly reducing reliance on imported fossil fuels. It also serves as a strategic energy transit state, connecting gas and electricity flows from Destenia and Kashiria to the wider Edrobean grid.

Agriculture, though limited in scale, is highly efficient and focused on sustainable cultivation of grains, vegetables, and dairy products in the Liden Savannah. The country maintains strict environmental standards, ensuring both food quality and ecological preservation.

Metosia’s economy is further distinguished by its digital governance, e-taxation platforms, and state-run investment banks that finance infrastructure, education, and innovation. Unemployment is low, and the labor force benefits from free university education, vocational training programs, and flexible labor protections.

Internationally, Metosia is a founding member of the Edrobean Community, World Trade Network, Global Reserve Fund, and International Energy Agency. It maintains trade partnerships with Verdania, Laocitia, Destenia, and beyond, and is often at the forefront of continental regulatory policy and innovation frameworks.

Demography

Metosia has a population of approximately 6.458.920, making it a small populated nation. Despite its size, it boasts a highly urbanized, well-educated, and socially cohesive population, contributing to the country’s global reputation for civic stability, innovation, and institutional trust.

The population is predominantly ethnic Metosian (about 88%), with the remaining 12% comprising ethnic minorities including Laocitians, Alverians, Kashirians, and a growing number of Tayanusan immigrants. While culturally diverse in pockets, the society is unified by strong linguistic integration, universal civic education, and a shared commitment to democratic values.

The median age is 35 years, reflecting a balanced demographic structure with both a productive workforce and a steadily aging segment. Fertility rates are modest but stable, supported by state-sponsored family benefits, maternal healthcare, and affordable childcare services. Life expectancy is high, driven by excellent access to universal healthcare, nutrition, and public health infrastructure.

Gender equality is firmly established in both law and practice. Women participate actively in politics, academia, entrepreneurship, and defense, and benefit from generous parental leave policies, pay equity regulations, and anti-discrimination protections. The government monitors gender representation in leadership positions and publishes annual equity benchmarks.

Metosia has one of the highest literacy rates in Edrobe—above 99%—and a fully publicly funded education system that spans from early childhood to university. The country emphasizes STEM education, civic instruction, and multilingualism.

The public healthcare system is universal and ranked among the most efficient in the continent. It is complemented by national wellness programs, preventive medicine, and digital health tracking systems. Specialized care, mental health services, and elderly support are accessible and integrated with local communities.

Migration in Metosia is carefully regulated but generally positive. The country attracts skilled migrants, academics, and foreign students, particularly from neighboring Edrobean nations and Tayanusa. Integration programs include language training, civic education, and employment support.

Language

The official language of Metosia is Metosian, a modern Edrobean language with linguistic roots distinct from Romance or Slavic families, though it shares structural affinities with neighboring Laocitian and Alverian due to historical contact and regional convergence. Metosian is spoken natively by over 95% of the population and functions as the sole language of government, education, law, national media, and public institutions.

The Academy of Metosian Language and Literature, a government-supported linguistic body, oversees orthographic standards, publishes dictionaries and grammar guides, and promotes literary excellence. Language policy emphasizes standardization, clarity, and accessibility, ensuring that all citizens—regardless of region or background—are proficient in the national tongue. Minority languages are spoken privately among immigrant communities but hold no official status. However, freedom of linguistic expression is protected, and translation services are offered in courts and hospitals when necessary.

Religion

Religion in Metosia plays an important but non-dominant role in public life, reflecting the country’s identity as a modern, secular, and pluralistic democracy. While 70% of the population identifies as Christian—primarily belonging to Protestant and Reformed traditions—religious affiliation is largely personal and cultural, rather than political or institutional.

The largest denomination is the Metosian Reformed Church, which has historical roots in the post-unification period and maintains a modest national presence through parishes, community services, and ceremonial participation in state events. Although the Church does not influence law or public policy, it retains moral authority among segments of the population, especially in rural areas and during national holidays.

A significant portion of the population—around 25%—identifies as non-religious, agnostic, or atheist. This reflects Metosia’s strong tradition of scientific education, rational public discourse, and a civic culture that emphasizes ethics over theology. Religious neutrality is especially strong among younger generations, urban populations, and the academic community.

The remaining 5% includes minority faiths, such as Islam, Catholicism, and other spiritual traditions, mostly represented within immigrant or mixed-heritage communities. These groups are free to practice and organize under Metosia’s constitutional guarantees of freedom of religion, worship, and non-discrimination.

The state is officially secular, and public institutions—including schools, courts, and government agencies—are strictly separated from religious influence. Religious education is optional in public schools and, when offered, focuses on comparative ethics and interfaith understanding, rather than doctrinal instruction.

Metosia also maintains a tradition of interfaith dialogue and tolerance, regularly hosting conferences and civic initiatives that promote coexistence, freedom of conscience, and cultural respect. Religious symbols and language are generally avoided in political life, but national ceremonies—such as Independence Day or Remembrance Events—may include non-denominational blessings or silent reflections.

Ethnicities

Metosia is a diverse yet integrated society, with 88% of its population identifying as ethnic Metosians. This majority group shares a common language, civic culture, and historical experience rooted in the unification of the Metosian lowland and highland communities during the 19th and early 20th centuries. Ethnic Metosians form the cultural backbone of the country, shaping its institutions, language, and national identity.

The remaining 12% of the population is composed of various ethnic minorities, including Laocitians, Alverians, Boravians, Kashirians, and an increasing number of Tayanusan migrants. These groups are concentrated primarily in urban centers, port regions near the Gulf of Silmara, and areas close to the southern and eastern borders. While they maintain some linguistic and cultural distinctions, most minority citizens are fluent in Metosian and fully participate in civic life, education, and employment.

Metosia promotes a model of civic integration rather than ethnic pluralism. The government emphasizes common citizenship, legal equality, and linguistic unity, ensuring that all citizens, regardless of background, have access to the same rights, public services, and opportunities. Anti-discrimination laws, language support programs, and local participation initiatives are in place to support minority inclusion.

Ethnic identity is not politicized, and there are no major ethnically-based political parties or separatist movements. National statistics track diversity for policy purposes, but public discourse focuses more on shared democratic values than ancestry. Interethnic relations are generally harmonious, supported by civic education, social mobility, and public messaging that emphasizes unity through diversity.
