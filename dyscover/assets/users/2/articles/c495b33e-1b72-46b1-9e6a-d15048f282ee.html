Boravia, officially the Republic of Boravia, is a sovereign country located in northeastern Edrobe. It borders Alveria to the southwest, Metosia to the south, Carpetia to the west, and is bounded by the Northern Sea to the east and north. It also holds a significant coastline along the Gulf of Silmara. With an area of 121,500 km² and a population of approximately 23.9 million, Boravia is a unitary presidential republic with its capital and largest city in Belisava.

Founded under the 2020 Constitution, Boravia is governed through a strong presidential system. The President holds wide executive powers, supported by a Vice President, a unicameral National Assembly, and an independent Supreme Court. Boravia is administratively divided into eight provinces, each headed by a centrally appointed governor, and further subdivided into municipalities led by locally elected mayors.

Boravia’s official language is Boravian, a Slavic-derived Edrobean language, and 96% of the population identifies as ethnic Boravian. The country is overwhelmingly Orthodox Christian and maintains a close relationship between church and state. The median age is 36, and Boravia has near-universal literacy, a robust public education system, and universal healthcare. Urbanization is moderate, with around 68% of the population living in cities.

Boravia has a GDP of 350 billion DTR, ranking among the top 20 global economies. Its economy is heavily industrial, with strengths in defense manufacturing, heavy industry, and infrastructure, alongside growing energy transit and logistics sectors. While it sources weapons and military systems primarily from Comussania, its economic and trade ties are strongest with Destenia.

The Boravian Armed Forces are well-funded, with mandatory conscription and a doctrine of total defense. The country maintains a large standing army, modern air force, and a strategic coastal guard. Law enforcement is centralized and structured under the National Police Directorate, with specialized units for cybercrime, border control, and public security.

Internationally, Boravia is a member of the United Nations, Edrobean Community, Global Reserve Fund, World Trade Network, International Tribunal for Justice, Global Health Organization, and the International Energy Agency. Its foreign policy balances military alignment with Comussania and economic cooperation with Destenia, reflecting a strategy of sovereignty, regional influence, and strategic self-reliance.

Etymology

The name Boravia originates from the ancient Boravic term "Bōrăvë," meaning “land of the tall hills” or “highland territory”. The term was historically used by early pastoral and warrior tribes to describe the elevated, forested terrain that dominates the region’s northern interior. Over time, as these tribes consolidated into a unified cultural and linguistic identity, the word evolved into Boravia, symbolizing both the geographic distinctiveness and resilient spirit of the people who inhabited the highlands.

The name became politically significant during the early modern period, when Boravian intellectuals and nationalists invoked it to express a sense of historical continuity, territorial integrity, and ethnic unity. During the movement for constitutional reform and independence, “Boravia” was widely adopted as the symbol of the emerging modern nation-state, uniting fragmented territories under a single national identity.

Geography

Boravia is located in the northeastern quadrant of Edrobe, occupying a strategically important position that connects the continental interior to key maritime routes. The country shares its southwestern border with Alveria and its southern border with Metosia, while its eastern and northern frontiers open onto the Northern Sea, giving Boravia extensive access to vital sea lanes and coastal resources. To the west, Boravia borders Carpetia, forming a long land frontier marked by a mix of forested hills and fortified transport corridors. To the southeast, the country reaches the shores of the Gulf of Silmara, a crucial maritime zone that supports fishing, trade, and naval operations. Boravia's terrain is largely elevated and rugged, characterized by vast highland plateaus, dense pine forests, and steep river valleys. While the interior is mountainous and sparsely populated, the coastal plains and river deltas near the Northern Sea are more fertile and urbanized.

Politics

Government

Boravia is a unitary presidential republic, governed under a framework that emphasizes strong executive leadership, institutional discipline, and national unity. Its current political system was established following the ratification of the Constitution, which reorganized the state along presidential lines and enshrined a model of governance centered on sovereignty, order, and civic duty. The country’s governmental structure is divided into three main branches: the executive, the legislature, and the judiciary, with all power ultimately derived from the Boravian people.

The President of Boravia is the head of state, head of government, and commander-in-chief of the armed forces. Elected directly by popular vote every six years, the President holds expansive powers in both domestic and foreign policy, including the authority to appoint and dismiss ministers, issue executive orders, declare states of emergency, and represent Boravia internationally. The presidency is designed to function as the primary source of national leadership, symbolizing unity, continuity, and strength. The Vice President assists in executive duties and may assume the presidency in the event of incapacity, resignation, or death.

The Cabinet of Ministers, appointed by the President, includes heads of key ministries such as Defense, Foreign Affairs, Finance, Internal Affairs, Education, and National Development. Ministers are tasked with formulating and implementing state policy within their respective sectors and are held accountable both to the President and, to a lesser extent, to parliamentary oversight. The executive operates a vast and centralized civil service apparatus, ensuring that state policy is efficiently carried out throughout Boravia’s provinces and municipalities.

Legislative authority is vested in the National Assembly, a unicameral parliament composed of 150 members elected through a mixed electoral system combining proportional representation and regional constituencies. Members serve five-year terms and are responsible for enacting laws, approving the national budget, ratifying treaties, and supervising the activities of the executive branch. The Speaker of the Assembly presides over parliamentary sessions, sets legislative agendas, and ensures orderly debate. While the Assembly plays an important role in shaping legislation, it generally functions in alignment with the President’s strategic direction, especially when the ruling party holds a majority.

The judiciary in Boravia is headed by the Supreme Court, which is responsible for interpreting the Constitution, ensuring the legality of governmental actions, and protecting fundamental rights. The Chief Justice, appointed by the President with the consent of the Assembly, leads the Supreme Court and oversees the national court system. Below the Supreme Court are appellate and district courts, which handle civil, criminal, and administrative cases. The judiciary is formally independent, though in practice, judicial rulings often reflect the country’s patriotic and conservative legal philosophy.

Complementing the core institutions are several key oversight bodies, including the Office of the Attorney General, who serves as the chief legal advisor to the government and the principal prosecutor of the republic. The Attorney General plays a central role in enforcing the law, combating corruption, and defending the state’s legal interests both domestically and abroad.

Political parties

Since its founding, Boravia has been governed by the National Unity Party (NUP), a conservative, nationalist political force that has shaped the country's political culture and state ideology. The NUP adheres to a vision inspired by Destenism, the political doctrine developed in Destenia, and aligns itself closely with the principles of totalitarian democracy—a model that emphasizes strong central leadership, national unity, and limited pluralism within a tightly controlled democratic framework. The party promotes sovereignty, social order, and cultural preservation, and maintains a dominant position in both the executive and legislative branches.

Under the leadership of the NUP, Boravia has prioritized military strength, civic discipline, and national self-reliance, positioning itself as a regional bastion of conservative stability. The party has historically commanded overwhelming support in elections, benefiting from its deep ties to the civil service, security forces, and rural communities.

The principal opposition is the Boravian Social Democratic Front (BSDF), a center-left party that advocates for expanded welfare, greater institutional transparency, and a more pluralistic democratic framework. Although the BSDF holds a minority in the National Assembly, it plays a significant role in parliamentary debates and represents a growing urban and youth constituency. However, its influence remains limited due to the entrenched dominance of the NUP and the broader political system’s structural preference for continuity and centralized control.

Other minor parties exist, including environmentalist, regionalist, and liberal factions, but none currently hold enough support to significantly alter the national political landscape. While the system allows for legal opposition, political life in Boravia remains heavily shaped by the ideological dominance and organizational strength of the National Unity Party.

Administrative subdivisions

Boravia is divided into eight administrative provinces, each serving as a regional unit of governance under the authority of the central government. These provinces are further subdivided into municipalities, which function as the basic units of local administration. Due to Boravia’s unitary system, power remains highly centralized, with provinces primarily acting as extensions of the national executive rather than autonomous entities.

Each province is headed by a Governor, appointed directly by the President of Boravia. Governors are responsible for coordinating the implementation of national policy, supervising provincial departments, and maintaining security in their jurisdiction. They act as the chief liaison between the central government and local authorities, overseeing infrastructure projects, economic planning, and public order.

Municipalities, which include both urban centers and rural districts, are led by Mayors, who are locally elected for five-year terms. Mayors manage day-to-day governance, including waste management, urban development, education services, and public utilities. While municipalities enjoy a degree of operational independence, they remain fiscally and administratively dependent on the provincial and national governments.

In line with Boravia’s top-down political culture, local governments are expected to align closely with national priorities and uphold the values of unity, discipline, and civic responsibility. Public administration at all levels is highly professionalized and hierarchical, with frequent audits and supervision by the central authorities to ensure efficiency and compliance.

The administrative model emphasizes stability and control, enabling the state to maintain a strong grip on governance throughout its territory while allowing for basic local service provision and civic representation at the municipal level.

Foreign relations

Boravia maintains a strategic and pragmatic foreign policy, grounded in the principles of national sovereignty, non-interference, and regional cooperation. The country actively participates in several major international and regional organizations, including the United Nations, Edrobean Community, Global Reserve Fund, International Tribunal for Justice, World Trade Network, Global Health Organization, and International Energy Agency. Through these institutions, Boravia engages in multilateral diplomacy while safeguarding its independent decision-making and security priorities.

Boravia’s foreign policy is defined by a careful balancing of geopolitical alliances and economic interests. On the military level, Boravia maintains a longstanding defense relationship with Comussania, from which it acquires most of its advanced weaponry, military vehicles, and surveillance systems. The two countries share similar views on defense policy, sovereignty, and regional security, and often collaborate in joint military exercises and arms development projects. This alignment has strengthened Boravia’s armed forces and positioned it as one of the most heavily fortified states in the northern Edrobean region.

Economically, however, Boravia is closely aligned with Destenia, its largest trade partner and financial ally. Destenian firms operate extensively in Boravia, particularly in the fields of infrastructure, telecommunications, and industrial manufacturing. Bilateral agreements with Destenia have provided Boravia with preferential market access, investment flows, and technical assistance, accelerating the country’s development and integration into the Edrobean economic space. This alignment also extends to ideological spheres, as both countries embrace variations of Destenism, fostering shared values of strong state leadership and controlled democratic governance.

While Boravia remains open to diplomatic engagement with a wide range of states, it is skeptical of supranational interference, particularly in matters of domestic policy, security, and cultural identity. Its diplomacy often emphasizes mutual respect, non-alignment in external power conflicts, and a firm commitment to defending national interests above all else.

In recent years, Boravia has increased its outreach to emerging economies and neutral nations, seeking to diversify trade partnerships and reduce dependency on any single bloc. Nonetheless, its core alliances—militarily with Comussania and economically with Destenia—remain central to its foreign policy strategy.

Military

The Armed Forces of Boravia represent one of the most disciplined, centralized, and strategically oriented militaries in Edrobe. Structured around the principle of total defense, the military plays a critical role not only in protecting national sovereignty but also in shaping the country's political identity and state doctrine. Boravia’s armed forces are composed of three main branches: the Boravian Army, the Boravian Air Force, and the Boravian Coastal Guard, each operating under the authority of the President, who serves as commander-in-chief.

The Boravian Army is the largest and most prominent branch, consisting of highly trained infantry, mechanized divisions, and specialized mountain units, reflecting the country’s rugged and elevated terrain. It is tasked with defending the national territory, securing borders, and maintaining internal stability. Military conscription is mandatory for all eligible males at age 18, contributing to a well-prepared reserve force and reinforcing a strong culture of patriotism and civic duty.

The Boravian Air Force is equipped with a modern fleet of multirole fighter jets, unmanned aerial vehicles, and surveillance aircraft acquired primarily from Comussania, Boravia’s principal defense partner. It is responsible for airspace control, reconnaissance, and rapid-response missions, and plays a growing role in technological modernization and counterintelligence operations.

Given its extensive access to the Northern Sea and Gulf of Silmara, Boravia also maintains a Coastal Guard with both military and civil functions. While not a full-fledged navy, the Coastal Guard operates patrol vessels, fast interceptors, and surveillance drones to secure the country's maritime interests, combat smuggling, and assist in disaster response. The focus is on asymmetric naval defense rather than power projection.

Boravia allocates a significant portion of its national budget—approximately 6% of GDP—to defense, reflecting the strategic importance of the military in national policy. The country prioritizes domestic arms manufacturing for basic equipment and relies on Comussanian military exports for advanced systems, including missiles, armored vehicles, and radar platforms.

The armed forces are overseen by the Ministry of Defense, which coordinates strategic planning, procurement, and training. In times of crisis, the President has the authority to declare martial law and mobilize both regular and reserve forces. The military operates under a doctrine of defensive readiness, but is fully capable of conducting preemptive strikes or border operations if national security is deemed to be at risk.

Beyond its military role, the armed forces contribute to civil engineering projects, emergency relief, and national education, reinforcing their integration into Boravian society. Military parades, public service campaigns, and veteran recognition ceremonies are commonplace, promoting a culture in which the military is seen not only as a shield of the republic but as a pillar of national identity.

Law enforcement

Law enforcement in Boravia is centralized, highly professionalized, and closely tied to the state’s broader goals of order, discipline, and national security. The system is structured around the National Police Directorate (NPD), which operates under the authority of the Ministry of Internal Affairs and is tasked with maintaining public order, preventing crime, and ensuring the enforcement of state laws across all provinces and municipalities.

The National Police is organized into several specialized branches, each with distinct responsibilities:

  • Public Security Police, responsible for routine patrols, crowd control, traffic enforcement, and neighborhood presence.
  • Criminal Investigation Division (CID), which handles serious crimes such as homicide, organized crime, narcotics, and corruption. It works closely with judicial authorities and the Attorney General’s Office.
  • Border and Customs Police, securing Boravia’s land and maritime borders in coordination with the Coastal Guard and military units.
  • Special Operations Units, including tactical teams (such as the Rapid Response Force) trained to handle counterterrorism, hostage rescue, and high-risk arrests.
  • Cyber and Financial Crime Division, a growing branch responsible for digital surveillance, cybercrime prevention, and anti-money laundering operations.

At the local level, municipalities have their own Municipal Police Departments, which manage local security, traffic, and ordinance enforcement. While nominally independent, municipal police are integrated into the national command structure and are subject to federal oversight.

Boravian law enforcement agencies are known for their discipline, effectiveness, and zero-tolerance stance on crime. Officers undergo rigorous training at state-run police academies, where they are educated not only in law and procedure but also in civic ethics, patriotism, and crisis response. The use of modern surveillance systems, body cameras, and centralized criminal databases allows for rapid information sharing and high levels of operational efficiency.

In terms of judicial coordination, law enforcement works closely with the Attorney General’s Office, which oversees prosecutorial decisions, and the court system, particularly in the issuance of warrants and pre-trial measures. Boravia also maintains close cooperation with international agencies through its participation in the Edrobean Community’s Judicial Cooperation Mechanism and the International Police Agency (IPA) of the United Nations.

While the police are respected by a large segment of the population for maintaining order and combating crime, civil liberties organizations have occasionally raised concerns about excessive force, preventive detentions, and broad surveillance powers, particularly during periods of national security alerts or public protests. Nonetheless, the government consistently defends its policing model as essential to preserving internal stability and national integrity.

In Boravia, law enforcement is viewed not only as a public service but as a pillar of state authority, integral to the functioning of its strong-state model and deeply intertwined with the country’s ideological identity.

Economy

Boravia possesses a mid-sized, industrialized economy with a GDP of 350 billion DTR, placing it among the top 20 economies globally. Despite lacking vast natural resources or a large consumer market, Boravia has carved out a role as a strategic manufacturing hub, an exporter of military and industrial equipment, and a key player in regional energy logistics and infrastructure development. Its economy is characterized by state coordination, high capital investment, and a strong emphasis on self-reliance and strategic autonomy.

The industrial sector is the backbone of the Boravian economy, contributing over 50% of national GDP. Core industries include defense manufacturing, heavy machinery, automotive components, steel production, and energy infrastructure engineering. Defense exports, particularly to Edrobean allies and neutral states, are a critical source of foreign income, supported by the country’s long-standing military partnership with Comussania. Boravia also produces advanced electronic systems, transport equipment, and construction materials, driven by a highly skilled technical workforce.

The energy sector plays a central strategic role. While Boravia does not produce significant quantities of fossil fuels domestically, it serves as a key transit and storage country for energy flows between Edrobe’s eastern producers and northern markets. Major pipeline networks and power corridors run through Boravian territory, generating substantial revenue through transit fees, logistical services, and regional grid integration. Recent efforts have focused on developing nuclear energy, with cooperation from Comussania and Verdania, to reduce dependency on imports and meet future demands.

The service sector, though smaller than in many developed nations, is expanding steadily. It includes telecommunications, logistics, finance, and a robust public sector. Government investment in public infrastructure, transportation, and digital modernization has created growth opportunities in urban planning, smart mobility, and e-governance. However, consumer services, retail, and tourism remain modest due to the country's security-first posture and limited openness to mass travel.

Agriculture plays a secondary but strategic role, with production focused on grains, livestock, and processed food. The sector is mechanized and heavily subsidized to ensure food security, especially in rural areas. Agro-industrial development is supported by cooperative networks and state-owned enterprises.

Boravia’s economic model is defined by a strong state role in strategic sectors, protection of key industries from foreign ownership, and tight regulation of financial capital. The state guides economic development through five-year plans, industrial strategies, and targeted investments in infrastructure and research. The government also runs sovereign development funds, used to finance national champions and large-scale industrial projects.

Internationally, Boravia is a member of the World Trade Network, Edrobean Community, and Global Reserve Fund, which gives it access to important trade agreements and development funding. While it maintains close trade ties with Destenia, especially in civil infrastructure and industrial finance, Boravia is cautious about foreign economic dependence, favoring bilateral agreements over deep economic integration.

Despite its authoritarian tendencies, Boravia has achieved relatively low unemployment, strong industrial growth, and a balanced trade surplus. Income inequality is moderate, thanks to state-led redistribution, price controls, and universal basic services such as healthcare, housing subsidies, and vocational training.

Demography

Boravia has a total population of approximately 23,894,238, making it one of the more populous nations in Edrobe. The demographic profile reflects a balanced and stable society, marked by moderate population density, urbanization, and a strong public investment in human capital. The country has maintained demographic stability through a combination of state planning, family support policies, and strict border control, ensuring both population growth and social cohesion.

The median age in Boravia is 36 years, reflecting a population that is neither aging rapidly nor disproportionately young. Fertility rates are slightly below replacement level, a trend common in industrialized nations, but offset by government-sponsored birth incentives, child benefits, and housing support for young families. Life expectancy continues to rise, thanks to significant improvements in healthcare access, sanitation, and public health education.

Boravia places a strong emphasis on gender equality within a traditional framework. While public discourse often promotes conventional family roles, the state ensures equal legal rights, workplace protections, and educational opportunities for both men and women. Women are well-represented in the public sector, particularly in healthcare, education, and law, and the military has gradually expanded female participation in non-combat and leadership roles.

The education system in Boravia is fully public, free at all levels, and highly centralized. Literacy rates exceed 99%, and school attendance is compulsory from age 6 to 18. The curriculum emphasizes patriotic education, technical skills, and moral instruction, with a focus on preparing citizens for national service and productive employment. Vocational schools and engineering colleges are particularly prominent, reflecting the country's industrial and defense-oriented economy.

Healthcare in Boravia is provided through a state-run universal system, funded by taxation and employer contributions. Clinics and hospitals are widely available, including in remote regions, and the government maintains emergency preparedness infrastructure as part of its national security doctrine. Public health campaigns focus on preventive care, military fitness, and family health, contributing to low infant mortality and high vaccination rates. The country also invests in military hospitals, veteran care, and rehabilitation services, further integrating health with civic responsibility.

Urbanization is moderate, with around 68% of the population living in cities, especially in major urban centers like Belisava, Karsgrad, and Ordelnik. Rural areas are well-connected through national infrastructure and remain socially and economically active, benefiting from agricultural subsidies and patriotic rural development initiatives.

Language

The official language of Boravia is Boravian, a Edrobean language with ancient roots and a long-standing literary and legal tradition. It is spoken natively by over 96% of the population and serves as the sole language of government, education, military, and national media. Boravian is deeply tied to the country’s cultural identity and national ideology, often seen not just as a means of communication, but as a symbol of unity, loyalty, and patriotic heritage.

Boravia has minimal linguistic diversity, owing to its homogeneous population and limited immigration. Minority languages are not officially recognized, and their use is confined to private or community settings. In urban centers, small enclaves may use other Edrobean tongues informally, but these have no public or institutional standing.

The government sees the Boravian language as a pillar of national security and cultural defense, particularly in the face of globalization and regional pluralism. As such, language policy is strict, centralized, and aimed at ensuring long-term preservation and national cohesion. In the Boravian worldview, to speak Boravian is to belong to the fatherland—a view deeply embedded in both law and society.

Religion

Religion in Boravia is overwhelmingly Christian, with 96% of the population adhering to the Orthodox faith, which holds a central place in both the cultural identity and historical development of the nation. The Boravian Orthodox Church is the dominant religious institution, deeply intertwined with the country’s traditions, national holidays, and public rituals. Although the state is officially secular under the Constitution, in practice, Boravia upholds a model of cooperation between church and state, grounded in shared values and mutual respect.

Orthodox Christianity in Boravia is marked by ceremonial richness, traditional liturgy, and strong community integration, particularly in rural and mountainous regions, where churches often serve as social and spiritual hubs. The Church also plays a visible role in national celebrations, military blessings, and public education, and it is common for government officials to attend religious services during key festivals and remembrance days.

The Patriarch of Belisava, based in the capital, is the highest-ranking cleric in the country and frequently consulted on moral and ethical issues. While not holding formal political power, the Patriarch is considered a moral authority, and Church pronouncements carry significant weight in public discourse, especially on matters of national identity, family, and ethics.

The remaining 4% of the population follows other religions or identifies as non-religious. These include small communities of Catholics, Tayanusan Muslims, and agnostics, primarily in urban areas. These groups are legally permitted to worship and organize but are not prominent in public life.

Religious education is optional but widely practiced, with Orthodox theology taught in many schools under the supervision of the Ministry of Education and in partnership with the Church. While religious freedom is protected under law, the dominance of Orthodoxy is reflected in the symbolism, architecture, and national ethos of Boravia.

Ethnicities

Boravia is one of the most ethnically homogeneous nations in the world, with 96% of its population identifying as ethnic Boravian. This overwhelming majority has shaped the country’s language, traditions, political values, and strong emphasis on national unity. The Boravians trace their heritage back to a long-standing lineage of highland warrior clans and early Orthodox communities, which over centuries evolved into a cohesive and deeply rooted national identity.

The remaining 4% of the population is composed of small ethnic minorities, including Tayanusans, Metosians, and Laocitians, who primarily reside in the urban centers and border regions. These communities are fully integrated into Boravian society, though their presence is limited and does not significantly affect the cultural or political landscape. While minority languages and customs may be practiced privately, Boravian remains the sole public and institutional language, and national identity is defined in civic and linguistic terms, rather than multiculturalism.

The government promotes a vision of national cohesion through cultural uniformity, and integration policies are designed to reinforce a shared identity centered on the Boravian language, Orthodox faith, and patriotic values. Immigrants and naturalized citizens are expected to adopt Boravian customs, participate in civic life, and contribute to the collective development of the republic.

Thanks to its demographic composition, Boravia enjoys a high degree of social cohesion, low interethnic tension, and strong national consensus around cultural and political priorities. The state's approach to ethnicity emphasizes unity, duty, and loyalty to the homeland, viewing diversity as manageable so long as it aligns with the broader values of the republic.
